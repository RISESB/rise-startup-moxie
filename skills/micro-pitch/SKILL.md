# Micro Pitch

A pitch has three parts, in this order:
- **Problem:** who has it and what it costs them. Make the listener feel it.
- **Solution:** what you do about it, in plain words.
- **Ask:** the one thing you want from this listener, right now.

Sixty seconds is about 130 to 150 spoken words. At 60 you land the plane.

## What you need from the student
- Their business in their own words (messy is fine).
- Who they're pitching to and what they want from that person. The ask changes with the audience.
- Any real proof: a customer, a sale, a number, a quote.

## Steps
1. Draft the 60-second version in their voice. Short sentences they could say out loud without running out of air.
2. Cut every word a 12-year-old wouldn't use.
3. Put the strongest real proof in. If they have none, don't invent it. Say what they're testing instead.
4. Make the ask specific enough that the listener could say yes on the spot.
5. Give the 30-second and 2-minute versions only if they asked or have an event coming.

## Output
**60-second Micro Pitch** (with a word count)
> ...

**Ask:** ...
**30-second version** (hook, what you do, ask)
**2-minute version** (adds proof, how it works, what's next)

**Coach notes (3 max):** the weakest line, what to cut, what to practice.

## Practice plan
Say it out loud 3 times with a timer. Record the third. Then pitch one real person and ask what they remember an hour later.
