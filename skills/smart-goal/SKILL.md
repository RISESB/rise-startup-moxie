# SMART Goal

RISE grades weekly goals on four things, 25 points each. This skill gets a goal to full marks before the student turns it in. The student writes the goal. You sharpen it.

## The rubric
| Part | Full marks looks like |
|---|---|
| **Specific** (25) | Highly specific, with a clear deliverable. A stranger would know exactly what gets made or done. |
| **Measurable** (25) | Clear metrics and success criteria. You could check it off with a yes or no and a number. |
| **Business alignment** (25) | Strongly connected to moving the business forward, with the reasoning stated. |
| **Action plan** (25) | Milestones, a timeline, and what they'll do if the likely obstacle shows up. |

## What you need from the student
- Their goal as they'd write it now, even if rough.
- Where their business is this week and what's due in class.

## Steps
1. Score their draft against each part, honestly, out of 25. Say why in one line each.
2. For the weakest part, ask the one question that would fix it. Let them answer.
3. Repeat for the next weakest. Don't rewrite the whole goal for them.
4. Have them state the final goal in one or two sentences, then build the action plan together: 2 to 4 steps with days, plus one obstacle and what they'll do about it.
5. Score it again.

## Common fixes
- "Work on my business" becomes "Interview 3 dog owners on my street by Thursday."
- "Get more customers" becomes "Get 2 pre-orders at $5 from people outside my friend group."
- "Because it's important" becomes "Because my riskiest assumption is that strangers will pay, and I haven't tested it."

## Output
**Goal:** one or two sentences.
**Why this, why now:** how it moves the business.
**How I'll know it's done:** the number.

**Action plan**
| Day | Step |
|---|---|

**Likely obstacle and my plan for it:** ...

**Rubric check**
| Part | Before | After | Note |
|---|---|---|---|

## Honest scoring
Score what's on the page, not what they meant. This is practice, not the official grade. A staff member grades the submitted goal.
