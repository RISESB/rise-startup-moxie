# Content Post

Posts written by AI from nothing sound like everyone else's. The fix is to interview the student first and build the post from their exact words. The interview is the step you never skip.

## What you need from the student
- What the post is about: a sale, a mistake, a customer, something they made, something they learned.
- Where it's going (Instagram, TikTok caption, a flyer, the school announcements) and who should see it.
- The one thing they want that person to do.

## Step 1: Interview (always)
Ask 5 to 6 questions, two at a time. Push back once on any vague answer ("what did they actually say?"). Save their exact phrases. Good angles:
- **The moment:** "What happened, right before and right after?"
- **The detail:** "What's one specific thing a stranger wouldn't guess?"
- **The customer:** "Who was it for, and what did they say or do?"
- **The stakes:** "What almost went wrong, or what were you worried about?"
- **The change:** "What do you do differently now?"
- **The skeptic:** "Why would someone scroll past this? What would stop them?"

## Step 2: Draft from their words
- Open with the most specific, surprising thing they said. Not a greeting, not a question to the audience.
- Use their phrases. If they said "the glue kept failing," write that, not "I faced production challenges."
- One idea per post. Short lines.
- End with the one action, said plainly.
- Fit the channel: caption length, hashtags only where they belong (3 at most), no emoji walls.

## Step 3: Check it
Read it back with the student and ask:
- Is every fact true? No invented customers, numbers or reviews.
- Would a classmate know you wrote this?
- Is anyone named or shown who hasn't said yes?

## Output
- **The post**, ready to paste, with a character count.
- **A second version** with a different opening line, so they can A/B test (see the campaign-ab-test skill).
- **Their best line:** the phrase from the interview worth reusing as a tagline.
- **To post it:** from the business or program account, not a personal one.

## Keep it safe
No last names, addresses, phone numbers or personal handles. No photos of other people without permission. Nothing about a customer they wouldn't want the customer to read.
