# Outreach Message

Use AI as a tool, not a crutch: the student sends it, so it has to sound like them and be true.

A door opener is someone who can connect you to the people you need. Outreach to them is a request for help or a conversation, not a sales pitch.

## What you need from the student
- Who it's going to, and how the student knows of them (shared school, a mutual contact, saw their shop).
- What they want: a 10-minute conversation, feedback, an intro, a first sale.
- One real reason this person in particular.

## How RISE writes outreach
Outreach is learning, not pitching. The goal is a conversation, not a sale.
- **Story before ask.** Open with who the student is and the real reason they're writing to this person. A true, specific detail beats a polished one.
- **Peer, not vendor.** "Here's what I'm figuring out" lands better than "here's what I can offer you."
- **End with a low-stakes question.** Something easy to answer yes or no. Never ask for a meeting in the first message.
- **Create curiosity, don't sell.** The first message should make them want to reply, not explain everything.

## Steps
1. Open with who the student is and why they're writing to this person. One or two sentences. Real details only.
2. One line on what they're working on or trying to learn.
3. One small, clear ask, as a question. "Would you be open to a few questions?" beats "Can we schedule a call?"
4. 40 to 90 words. Short enough to read in 15 seconds.
5. Greeting is "Hi [First]," then straight in. Sign off with just their first name and "RISE student."
6. No buzzwords, no "I hope this finds you well," no "quick question," no flattery they don't mean, no links or images in the first message.
7. Have the student read it out loud. If they wouldn't say it to someone they'd actually want to talk to, rewrite it.

## Output
**Message 1** (with word count). Email also gets a subject line: 1 to 3 lowercase words, specific and a little unexpected (`lawn question`, `student project`), never a question and never "follow up".

**If they asked for a sequence:**
- **Message 2 (3 to 4 days later):** adds something useful: what they've learned since, a question, a thank-you.
- **Message 3 (a week later):** short, gracious, leaves the door open.

**Before you send**
- Is every fact in it true?
- Is the ask one thing?
- Did an adult you trust see it if it's going to someone you don't know?

## Safety
Don't put a home address, personal phone number or personal social handles in outreach. Use the program or school contact. Meet people in public places or at RISE, and tell a staff member first.
