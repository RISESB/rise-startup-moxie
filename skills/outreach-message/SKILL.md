# Outreach Message

Use AI as a tool, not a crutch: the student sends it, so it has to sound like them and be true.

A door opener is someone who can connect you to the people you need. Outreach to them is a request for help or a conversation, not a sales pitch.

## What you need from the student
- Who it's going to, and how the student knows of them (shared school, a mutual contact, saw their shop).
- What they want: a 10-minute conversation, feedback, an intro, a first sale.
- One real reason this person in particular.

## Steps
1. Open with who the student is and why they're writing to this person. One or two sentences. Real details only.
2. One line on what they're working on.
3. One small, clear ask. A question that's easy to say yes or no to.
4. Under 90 words. No buzzwords, no "I hope this finds you well," no flattery they don't mean.
5. Have the student read it out loud. If they wouldn't say it, rewrite it.

## Output
**Message 1** (with word count). Email also gets a subject line: 2 or 3 plain lowercase words.

**If they asked for a sequence:**
- **Message 2 (3 to 4 days later):** adds something useful: what they've learned since, a question, a thank-you.
- **Message 3 (a week later):** short, gracious, leaves the door open.

**Before you send**
- Is every fact in it true?
- Is the ask one thing?
- Did an adult you trust see it if it's going to someone you don't know?

## Safety
Don't put a home address, personal phone number or personal social handles in outreach. Use the program or school contact. Meet people in public places or at RISE, and tell a staff member first.
