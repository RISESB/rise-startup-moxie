# Value Proposition and Pricing

**The "So what?" test.** Say the value proposition, then ask "so what?" If there's an obvious answer, you haven't reached the real payoff yet. "We help people eat better" fails. Keep asking until you name what the customer actually gets.

**Three ways to find a price**
- **Cost-plus:** what it costs you to deliver one, plus a margin.
- **Value-based:** what the result is worth to the customer.
- **Competitive:** what people already pay for the nearest alternative.

## What you need from the student
- Who the customer is and the problem they have.
- What it costs to deliver one unit: materials, their time, fees. Real numbers.
- What customers do or pay now instead.

## Steps
1. Draft the value proposition: "For [who], we [do what] so they [real payoff]."
2. Run "So what?" on it with the student, up to 3 rounds. Let them answer.
3. Work the three prices with the math shown. Don't guess competitor prices: ask the student what they've seen, or tell them to go check 2 or 3.
4. Recommend a starting price and say what would make them change it.
5. Name which common pricing model fits (per unit, subscription, tiered, pay-what-you-want, etc.) and why.

## Output
**Value proposition:** one sentence.
**So what? chain:** the rounds that got there.
