# Product Listing

Good listing copy is specific. It says what the thing is, who it's for, what makes it different, and what to do next. It never claims anything the student can't back up.

## What you need from the student
Ask for these a few at a time. If something is missing, leave it out of the copy and list it under "to add."
- What it is, in plain words, and what it's made of or what's included.
- Who buys it and when they use it.
- Price, sizes, colors, options, how long it takes.
- How to order and how they get it (pickup at lunch, delivery, at a table).
- What's different from the obvious alternative.
- Proof: how many sold, something a customer said, how long they've been making it.

## Steps
1. Pick the lead: the one fact most likely to make this customer stop and look. Lead with it.
2. Write 3 headline options in different styles: plain and clear, benefit-first, and playful.
3. Write the description. Start with what they get. Then details. Then how to order.
4. Cut every word that could describe any product ("high-quality," "unique," "amazing"). Replace with the actual detail.
5. Run the "So what?" test on the first line (see the value-prop-and-pricing skill).

## Output
**Headlines (pick one)**
1. ...
2. ...
3. ...

**Description** (60 to 120 words)

**Short version** (under 25 words, for a caption or sign)

**Key selling points** (3 to 5 bullets, each a fact)

**Details** (price, options, how to order, turnaround)

**To add before you publish:** facts that were missing.

## Keep it honest
Only real prices, real reviews and real numbers. No "best in South Bend." No health, safety or money claims. If it's food, say what's in it and tell the student to ask a staff member about what rules apply before selling.
