# End of Year Recap

_Most of the year points forward, toward the next build. This week we turn around and look back, because the growth is real and students usually cannot see it until you make them._

Students almost never notice how far they have come. They live inside the daily work, so the change is invisible to them, the way you cannot watch yourself get taller.

That is the trap Week 33 is built to spring. Not another push forward. A deliberate turn around to look at the whole road, name the growth out loud, and start telling the story of it.

This week is reflective on purpose, and it sets up the finale. Weeks 34 through 36 are the showcase build, prep, and the presentation itself. So the job here is quieter but it matters just as much: help students see what they built, and feel it.

## The guiding question is a story question

We open with one question and let it run all week.

> How far have you actually come this year - and how do you tell that story?

The word "actually" is doing real work there. Left alone, most students will either shrug the year off as no big deal or inflate it into something it wasn't. The reflection we want lives between those two, in the honest version.

And notice it is a story question, not a scoreboard question. The point is not just the numbers. It is the arc: where they started, what they built, and who they became doing it.

## Walk the whole road, unit by unit

Monday we walk the class back through the year in order, so the arc is visible instead of a blur. For each unit, students jot the biggest thing they built or learned.

- **Unit 1 (Weeks 1-6)** - mindset, your why, observing problems, your first pitch.
- **Unit 2 (Weeks 7-17)** - design thinking, customer discovery, MVP, sales, and the final pitch.
- **Unit 3 (Weeks 18-30)** - marketing, campaigns, and the finances.
- **Unit 4 (Weeks 31-33)** - analytics, operations, and now the recap.

Seeing it laid out like this lands differently than remembering it in pieces. Most students are surprised by the sheer amount of ground they covered.

If you saved their Week 1 Entrepreneurial Identity sheets, this is the moment to hand them back. Watching a student read what they wrote in September, before any of this existed, is the whole point of the warm-up. The reactions tell you the year worked.

## Two kinds of growth, and the second one matters more

Then we ask students to write honestly about growth in two directions. This split is the core framework of the week, and I would not skip it.

- **Business growth** - what is real now that did not exist in September. Customers, sales, a product, a skill they can name.
- **Personal growth** - how they think, speak, or show up differently than they did in Week 1.

The line I want them to sit with: "The business might be big or small. That is not the point. The point is who you became by building it."

That reframe protects the student whose business stayed small. A kid who sold three products but learned to talk to a stranger, hear no, and keep going grew more than the one who got lucky with a viral post. This week is where we say that out loud and mean it.

## From reflection to evidence

Tuesday we move from feeling to proof. Students pull their strongest artifacts from the whole year into one place: the pitch deck, the best campaign, sales and traction numbers, customer quotes, photos of the product or an event, the dashboard.

They have been building this portfolio all year without calling it that. Now they curate the highlights.

Then they draft the year-end story on a simple arc: where I started, what I built, what I learned, where I am headed. That draft becomes the backbone of the showcase presentation a few weeks out, so we get the honest version down now while the year is fresh.

Friday's Profit or Broke runs a year-in-review edition using real numbers from students' actual businesses. Celebrate the wins, and treat the losses as lessons, not failures. Same tension we have worked all year: the numbers are real, and what you make of them is up to you.

## Where students get stuck

The most common miss is students defaulting to modesty. Ask them what grew and they say "not much." That is not honesty, it is blindness to their own change, and your job is to surface the evidence they are ignoring. The Week 1 sheets and the unit walk-through exist for exactly this.

The second miss is students who confuse business size with growth. A small business is not a failed one. Keep pulling the reflection back to the person, not the revenue.

And a few will polish the story into something that is not true - all wins, no struggle. The honest version, struggle included, is always the better story and the one that lands at the showcase. Push them toward true, not impressive.

## What good looks like by Friday

By the end of the week, a student should be able to tell the one-line version of their year: where they started, what they built, and where they are headed. Not polished yet. Honest and theirs.

They should have their evidence pulled into one place, ready for the showcase build, and they should be able to point to at least one specific way they think or show up differently than they did in September.

We close the way we opened, with each student saying one sentence out loud: the person I was in Week 1 versus the person I am now. That sentence is the whole year compressed, and hearing themselves say it is what makes the growth real.

The finish line is three weeks out. This is the week they turn around, see the road they ran, and get ready to tell someone about it.
