# The End Part 3

_The last week is not a test. It is a stage. Students show a real audience the business they built, then look back at the whole year and name what changed in them._

Most programs end with a final exam. Ours ends with a room full of people and a booth you have to stand behind.

That is the whole difference. A test asks you to recall what a teacher already knows. A showcase asks you to present something real to people who have never seen it, and to hold your ground when they ask questions. One is about memory. The other is the actual skill we have been building since Week 1.

So the last week of the year does two jobs. It gets every student ready to show the world what they built, and it gives them room to close the year with pride instead of just relief.

## The showcase is the culminating event, not a report card

The guiding question this week is plain: are you ready to show the world what you built, and to close this year with pride?

The Student Showcase is the biggest moment of the year. Students present their businesses to family, community members, and guests - people who are not grading them, just meeting their work for the first time.

You get to pick the format that fits your space and your audience:

- **Booth-style** - guests circulate, students pitch and demo one-on-one. Great for products and real conversation.
- **Stage-style** - each student delivers their two-minute presentation to the full room. Great for a formal program.
- **Hybrid** - short stage presentations first, then open booth time for the deeper conversations.

There is no single right shape. There is only the one that lets your students get seen.

## How we get ready: setup, one last rehearsal, then trust the prep

The week opens on Monday with final setup, not new work. Students print everything, load their deck on the right device, assemble their booth pieces, and confirm the product or demo works. You run a logistics check: room layout, tables, screens, signage, guest list, and a run-of-show for the event.

Then every student runs their presentation one last time, timed. Quick, encouraging notes only. No big changes now.

That "no big changes" rule matters more than it sounds. The night before something real, the instinct is to rip it up and start over. We do the opposite. The prep is done. The job now is to trust it.

I like to open Monday with one word around the room: how do you feel about tomorrow? Say the nerves out loud and then name what they are.

> Nervous means you care. It is not a sign you are unready. It is a sign it matters. Channel it, do not fight it.

Then you close with the only thing left to say: you are ready, get sleep, dress the part, show up proud.

## The showcase is a beginning, so the follow-up is part of the work

Here is where students get stuck, and it is worth naming before the event. They treat the showcase as the finish line. It is not.

During the event, students keep capturing contacts and interest. The CRM habit does not stop because there is a crowd. Every guest who leans in is a name worth writing down.

The day after, we run a debrief and then we follow up. What surprised you? What did guests ask most? What did people respond to? Then students message every contact they made - thank-you notes, promised info, next steps.

The line I repeat all day: the showcase is a beginning, not an ending. Who do you owe a message?

A student who pitches beautifully and follows up with nobody has practiced performing. A student who follows up has practiced business. We want the second one.

## Closing the year: name the growth, then commit to what's next

The back half of the week turns from the event to the person.

Students write a full-year reflection, and it is a keeper for their portfolio. Four prompts, in their own words:

- **The biggest thing I built this year.**
- **The biggest thing I learned about business.**
- **The biggest thing I learned about myself.**
- **What I want to do with this next.**

Then we celebrate, out loud and by name. Superlatives, shoutouts, certificates, food - whatever fits your group. Every student gets named for something real they did or became this year. Not a participation trophy. A specific thing you watched them do.

We end where we always end, in a closing circle. Each student shares one thing they are proud of and one thing they will carry forward. The facilitator goes last.

And because this is entrepreneurship, we do not stop at feelings. We talk paths forward: keep running the business over the summer, next-level programs, competitions like McCloskey, and how to stay connected to the people they built alongside. Each student writes one commitment - this summer, I will do this with my business or my skills.

## What "good" looks like at the close

By Friday you are not looking for the most polished pitch in the room. You are looking for a student who can stand behind their own work, talk about it plainly with a stranger, and look back at the year and name what changed.

The reframe to leave them with is the one this whole class was built on. You started the year thinking entrepreneurship was for other people. You end it as a founder who found a problem, built a solution, talked to customers, made a little money, and pitched to a room full of people.

That does not go away over the summer. Whatever you do next, you now know how to see a problem and start moving toward it.

That is the whole thing. Go do it.
