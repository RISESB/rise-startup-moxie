# Digital Marketing Tools

_There are dozens of marketing tools and a student does not need any of them all at once. Week 21 is about picking the two or three where their customer already is, and building one real asset they can publish._

Ask a student how they plan to market their business and you usually get a list. Instagram, TikTok, a website, flyers, email, maybe a booth somewhere. All of it, all at once, none of it started.

That instinct is the trap. The tools are not the hard part. Choosing the few that fit your customer, and actually using them well, is the hard part. Most young founders spread themselves across every channel and go deep on none.

So Week 21 does one job. It cuts the list down and gets one real asset out the door. The guiding question we keep coming back to is plain: what are the actual tools you use to put your brand in front of your customer, and how do you use them well?

## You do not need all of them

We start Monday by taking the pressure off. There are dozens of marketing tools. You need the two or three where your specific segment already spends time.

This is a callback to Week 19 - go where they are. If a student did the segment work, they already know something about where their customer's attention lives. Marketing tools are just how you show up in that place.

The main channels we put on the board:

- **Social media** (Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, LinkedIn) - best for awareness and story
- **Landing pages** (Carrd, Google Sites) - one page that turns interest into a signup or sale
- **Short video** (Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts) - the highest reach for most young founders right now
- **Email and text lists** - the only audience you actually own, and great for repeat customers
- **Local and community** (flyers, events, community groups) - still powerful for a local business

Then we make them match the channel to the segment out loud. Selling to teens? Short video. Selling to local parents? Facebook groups and community events. Selling to businesses? LinkedIn and direct email. The channel is not a preference. It follows the customer.

## Organic first, then decide if paid is worth it

The second idea Monday is one students tend to get backwards. They assume marketing means paying for ads.

So we name the two kinds of reach clearly. Organic is free reach you earn with content - slow, but it builds trust. Paid is when you pay to put your message in front of people - fast, but it stops the moment the money stops.

> Start organic. Prove the message works for free before you ever pay to boost it.

The logic is simple and it sticks: paying to promote a message that does not work just loses money faster. Get the message right for free first. Then, if you want to reach more people faster, paid is a tool you understand instead of a gamble.

## Every asset needs a next step

Here is the mistake I see most, and we hit it hard: getting attention and having nowhere to send it.

A student films a great video, it gets views, and then nothing happens, because there was no next step. Attention with nowhere to go is wasted attention.

So the rule is that every marketing asset points somewhere. A link, a signup, a DM to send, a form to fill. A simple Google Form or a link in the bio is enough to start. You are not building a funnel with software. You are just making sure interested people have one obvious thing to do next.

## Build one real thing, not a plan to build things

Monday ends with each student picking their top two or three channels and choosing the ONE asset they will build Tuesday: a post, a landing page, or a short video. One asset. Publishable.

Tuesday is a build day, and this is where the week earns its keep. Students make something real that carries their message from Week 19, reflects their brand from Week 20, and has a clear next step baked in.

AI does the first draft, not the final one. A student can prompt, "Draft three captions for an Instagram post advertising my business to my segment," or, "Write the copy for a one-page landing page for my business." Then the actual work starts - editing until it sounds like them, not like a machine. Same rule as every week: AI drafts, you decide.

By Thursday's publish block, they are not planning anymore. They finish, they actually publish or schedule it, and they set up the lead capture so someone who sees it can respond. The 1-on-1 question is blunt on purpose: did you publish, where can people find it, and how will you know if it works?

## Where students get stuck

Two places, mostly.

The first is channel sprawl. A student wants to be on everything and ends up committing to nothing. The fix is to keep pointing them back at their segment - one customer, two or three channels, done.

The second is the missing next step. The asset looks finished but there is no link, no form, no way to act. Walk the room asking one question over and over: what is the next step for someone who sees this, and is it actually ready? If the answer is fuzzy, the asset is not done.

## What good looks like by Friday

By Friday, a student has published something real and can name the channel that fits their segment and why. Not five things half-built. One thing live in the world with a next step attached.

Friday's Profit or Broke drives the money point home with marketing-cost scenarios - spend thirty dollars boosting a post, do the math, was it profit or broke - so students start pricing what a click or a follower is actually worth to them.

The reframe to leave them with is the one we opened on. You do not need every tool. You need the few where your customer already is, one asset worth publishing, and a next step for anyone who bites. Pick your channels, build one real thing, put it in front of people, and watch what happens.
