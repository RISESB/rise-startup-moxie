# Launch a Real Marketing Campaign

_Last week students built a campaign and an A/B test on paper. This week they put it in front of real people, watch the numbers move, and adjust while it is still live. No more planning. Just launch and measure._

Most marketing units end with a plan. The student makes a nice deck, describes the campaign they would run, and never runs it. It looks like work. It generates nothing.

Week 24 refuses that ending. This is not a lecture week. It is an execution week. Students already have their campaign kit and their A/B test from Week 23, so the only job left is the one that actually teaches: get it live and measure it.

The guiding question is blunt on purpose. Can you run a real campaign, in the real world, and produce real results this week? Not a mockup. Results.

## The whole week is one sentence: launch and measure

We open by putting the goal on the board and asking each student the same thing. What is your campaign goal, and what number would make this week a win? They say it out loud, we write it down, and now there is a target to compare against on Friday.

Then we say the part that matters most this week. No more planning. Get it live. Measure real results.

That framing does something. It moves the work from "describe a campaign" to "run one," which is a much harder and much more useful thing. A plan you never ship teaches you almost nothing. A campaign that goes out and underperforms teaches you a lot.

## The pre-launch checklist

Before anything goes live, students run a short checklist. It is boring and it saves the week.

- **All pieces point to ONE next step** - every post, email, or flyer sends people to the same link, form, or DM. Not five different asks.
- **Lead capture is set up and tested** - does the form actually work? Someone has to click submit and confirm it lands.
- **A/B versions are ready** - version A and version B, staged to post at the planned times.
- **Tracking sheet is built** - six columns: date, piece, reach, clicks, leads, sales.

The one I lean on hardest is the form test. A dead form is the quietest way to lose a whole week of leads, because it looks fine from the outside while the results go nowhere. Test the link. Test the form. If it breaks, the leads are gone and you never know they existed.

## Go live in the room

Then students publish. In class, not "tonight at home." They post, send, hand out, or share, whatever their channel is, and then they log the launch in the tracking sheet.

I walk the room while it happens and ask one question over and over. It's live? Good. What is your version A versus your version B, and when does B go out? A launch with no A/B timing is just a post. A launch with A now and B on Wednesday at the same time is a test.

Offline channels count too. A student running flyers or an event does not get to opt out of this week. They confirm the distribution plan and make the first drop today. The channel changes, the discipline does not.

Here is the reframe I want students to feel in this moment. The thing that used to require a marketing team and a budget is now this plain. Publish, log, watch. You can do the whole loop with a phone and a spreadsheet.

## The part beginners skip: adjust mid-campaign

Most people set a campaign and forget it. They post, they hope, and they never look again until it is over. Thursday is where we break that habit.

Students pull up their live data and make one real adjustment. Not a rewrite. One move, driven by what the numbers are actually saying.

- Post the winning A/B version more often.
- Change a weak hook that is not landing.
- Follow up with everyone who clicked but did not act.
- Push the offer harder.

The line I use is simple. Real marketers don't set it and forget it. They watch and adjust. What is your data telling you to do right now?

This is the OODA Loop from Week 1 wearing marketing clothes again. Observe the numbers, orient on what they mean, decide the one adjustment, act, and keep watching. A student who makes one data-driven change mid-campaign has learned something no finished deck could teach them.

The place students get stuck is reading a small number as a verdict. Three clicks is not a failure and it is not nothing. It is signal. The skill is deciding what to do next with it instead of getting discouraged and quitting on a campaign that is two days old.

## Profit or Broke, then the report

Friday we run the numbers for real. Profit or Broke uses each student's actual campaign data. You spent this much money and time, you got this many leads and this many sales. Profit or broke? And what is a lead actually worth to you?

Then every student writes a short campaign report for their portfolio: goal versus actual, the best-performing piece and why, the A/B winner, cost in money and time versus what they got, and one thing they would do differently next time.

We celebrate real results, even small ones. A single real sale from a campaign a teenager ran themselves is a big deal, and we treat it that way.

## What "good" looks like by Friday

By Friday, a student should have run a live campaign, tracked it with real numbers, made at least one mid-course adjustment, and written up what happened.

You are not looking for a big win. Plenty of campaigns will come back broke, and that is fine. You are looking for a student who can point at real data, say what worked and what died, and tell you their one adjustment for next time.

The reflection we close on is the whole point of the week. What did running a real campaign teach you that no lecture could? Almost every student has an answer, because they finally have something a lecture cannot give them: numbers they earned from the world instead of guessed in their head.

Next week we take it in person. Week 25 is Open House prep, where they get ready to show the business to the community. Time to make it real, face to face.
