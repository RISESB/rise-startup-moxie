# Semester 1 Review

_The first week back from break is not a fresh start. It is a checkpoint. Before students set new goals, they take an honest look at what they have already built._

Most people treat January like a blank page. New year, new plan, wipe the slate and go.

That framing feels good and it wastes what you already have. A student who "starts fresh" in Week 18 is quietly throwing away seventeen weeks of real work - a customer they talked to, an offer they tested, a price they defended.

So Week 18 does the opposite of a reset. Before anyone sets a single new goal, we make them look hard at what is already on the table. The guiding question sits over the whole week: what have you actually built so far, and what does your business need most in the next 18 weeks?

## An audit before ambition

The temptation coming off break is to jump straight to goals. Big number, clean plan, feels like momentum.

We slow that down by one day on purpose. Monday is an honest audit of everything built in Units 1 and 2, before anyone is allowed to dream about Semester 2.

The audit walks five areas of the business, one at a time:

- **Problem + customer** - who do you serve, what problem do you solve, and is it still the same one you named in Week 1?
- **May-B-P + MVP** - what did you actually build, what did you test, and what did you learn from it?
- **Customer discovery** - how many real customers have you talked to, not imagined?
- **Value proposition + price** - what is your offer, and what do you charge for it?
- **Traction** - any sales, pre-orders, or committed customers yet, or none so far?

That last one tends to sting, and it is supposed to. An audit that only flatters you is not an audit.

## Name the strongest part, then the weakest

Here is the move that makes the week work. Once the audit is filled in, each student circles the strongest part of their business - the piece they feel most confident about - and underlines the weakest, the thing that scares them or that they keep avoiding.

> Circle the strongest part of your business. Underline the weakest - the thing you keep avoiding. That underline is your Semester 2.

Then they take it to a partner. Both share strong and weak, and the partner asks two questions: why is that the weak spot, and what would it take to fix it?

We bring it to the whole room too. Four or five students name their weakest area out loud, and I write the patterns on the board. Usually the same gaps cluster - nobody has really sold anything, or nobody can say their price with a straight face. Those clusters shape where we push all semester. The class tells me what to teach next.

## Turn the audit into three goals, and a place to keep them

Tuesday is where the honest look becomes a plan. First, students pull everything into one organized place - persona, business model canvas, value proposition, price and rationale, SWOT, sales funnel, the pitch deck from Week 17, any traction data. That becomes the business portfolio, and from here on it is the single source of truth they add to every unit.

Then each student writes three SMART goals for Semester 2 - specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound. We ask for one of each kind so the goals do not all collapse into the same wish:

- **A traction goal** - a real number. Sales, customers, or revenue.
- **A skill goal** - something to get better at, like marketing, sales, or finance.
- **A growth goal** - how you want to grow as an entrepreneur.

The traction goal is the one I lean on hardest in 1-on-1s. "What is your number one traction goal, and what is the first step this week?" A goal you cannot start on Monday is a wish, not a goal.

## Where students get stuck

Two places, every year.

The first is the vague goal. "Grow my business" is not a goal, it is a mood. If a student cannot tell me the number and the first action, we are not done. Realistic and specific beats big and blurry.

The second is the flinch away from the weak spot. Given the choice, students will set three goals that all sit on top of their strongest area, because that is where it feels good to work. The whole point of the underline on Monday is to stop that. The weak spot is usually the most valuable place to aim, precisely because it is the part they have been avoiding.

This is the same loop from Week 1 wearing new clothes. Observe where you actually are, orient on what it means, decide the next move, act. The audit is just the observe step done with real numbers instead of hope.

## What "good" looks like by Friday

By Friday, momentum should be back and pointed somewhere.

A student who has it can do three things: say where their business actually stands after an honest audit, name their weakest part without wincing, and state three concrete goals for Semester 2 with a real number in at least one of them. On Friday we go around the room and each student says their number one goal out loud, then we post them somewhere visible for the rest of the year.

Then we name the turn. Semester 1 was about building your business. Semester 2 is about growing it - getting customers to find you and making the numbers work. Unit 3 starts next week with marketing, and Week 19 asks the first real growth question: who exactly are you talking to, and what do you say to make them listen?

The reframe to leave them with is simple. You did not come back to start over. You came back to build on what is already here.
