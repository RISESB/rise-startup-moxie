# How We Facilitate

A facilitator's job isn't to lecture — it's to create space for student thinking to emerge. This is the framework every Startup Moxie week is built on. Read it first, then paste it alongside any week into Claude to give it full context.

## Throw out what a classroom is supposed to look like

Startup Moxie runs on constructivism: you don't learn to be an entrepreneur by reading about it — you become one by becoming one. So you're not the guru at the front of the room. You're the guide on the side — the connector who opens doors to the ecosystem and then releases students to go build. Your students are your customers: figure out what they actually need, and find creative ways to deliver it. Not sure what they need? Ask them.

## Six field strategies

### Be the Guide, Not the Guru

🔍

Spark discovery, don't deliver answers.

You're the mapmaker, not the hero. Resist the urge to over-explain — use questions, curiosity, and calm presence to move students toward their own insights, and make sure every voice gets airtime.

Swap “Let me tell you…” for “What do you notice about…?”

Aim for students talking ~70% of the time, you ~30%.
### Share, Then Shift

📖

Use stories to ignite, not dominate.

A short, real anecdote can spark courage or connection — but it's a bridge, not a monologue. Tee up the concept with a story, then immediately pivot to student dialogue. Structure the share with C3O: Context → Challenge → Choice → Outcome.

“When I rushed my first customer interviews… what would you do differently?”

Then shift: “What does this look like in your project right now?”
### Make it Real

🎯

Build around lived experience — and build the skill.

Start with what students are actually building, testing, or stuck on, and use that as the entry point to teach. Bring in a guest speaker at least monthly to model a real path. Most students want more depth, not more lecture — lean on 1:1 and small-group touchpoints to break through.

Ask: “What would this look like in your next customer conversation?”

Fewer concepts, more real action.
### Lead with Questions, Not Conclusions

❓

The best tools in your kit are great questions.

Socratic facilitation is about knowing how to ask, not what to say. Prepare a mix of question types and be ready to flex between them. Asking instead of answering is what turns participation into ownership.

Build muscle memory for the four question types (see below).

Open-ended and non-leading beats a question with the answer baked in.
### Feedback = Fuel

📬

Let students shape the experience.

Collect feedback often, as a source of design insight, not a formality. What clicked? What missed? Where did students light up or stall? Build for the edges — outlier feedback reveals gaps; when one student speaks up, they usually represent many who didn't.

End class with a 1-minute exit ticket.

Evolve your sessions from what you hear — creatively, not defensively.
### Iterate Everything

🔄

Test, learn, and refine continuously.

Treat every artifact — why statements, business hypotheses, pitches — as living, not final. The classroom is a testing ground. Return to core statements regularly to sharpen them against real-world feedback. Students don't need more volume; they need tighter feedback loops tied to action.

Ask: “What did you test this week? What changed because of it?”

Build in 1:1 moments to go deep on assumptions and strategy.

## The question bank

Four types to draw from and flex between. Keep them open-ended and non-leading.

Clarifying

“Can you give an example?”
Probing

“What's driving that choice?”
Reflective

“How has your thinking changed?”
Application

“How might you test that this week?”

## The RISE educator mindset

- Love the adventure of learning.
- Embrace the chaos of the creative process.
- Be comfortable leading a pack of Doers — without always knowing where it goes.
- Accept all feedback as a gift.
- Understand there's rarely just one right answer.
- Lead with empathy.
- View failure as a chance to learn, pivot, and try again.

## The weekly rhythm

Monday

Lecture — introduce the week's concept and guiding question.
Tuesday

Activities that apply the concept to each student's venture.
Wednesday

Guest speaker or tour — a real path modeled from the ecosystem.
Thursday

Speaker recap and work time — build, test, get unstuck.
Friday

Founder Fight, 1-on-1s, and a week reflection to close the loop.
