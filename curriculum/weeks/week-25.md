# Week 25: Open House Prep

Unit 3: Marketing & Brand

**Guiding question:** How do you present your business to the community so they understand it, remember it, and want to support it?

_When it last ran: Week of February 23, 2026 · 7:30–9:00 AM · Mon–Fri. Dates are from a previous year; this year's calendar may differ._

## By the end of the week, students can
- Build a clean, professional booth/table display for their business.
- Prepare a 30-second and a 2-minute version of their pitch.
- Create a simple visual (poster, one-pager, or slides) for visitors.
- Practice engaging guests and capturing contacts at an event.

## Lesson walkthrough
_Written as the facilitator's script for the classroom. Use it to know what was taught and how; don't read it back to students._

### Monday

### Monday — Lecture: What Makes a Great Open House Presence

Facilitator Goal: Students know what a strong, professional event presence looks like and start planning theirs.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Think of a booth or table you've walked past at a fair or event. What made you stop — or keep walking?" 3-4 students share.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: Event Presence

Opening (2 min)

Say: "The Open House is your chance to show real people what you've built. Families, community members, maybe future customers or supporters. You have about 5 seconds to earn a stop and 30 seconds to earn a conversation."

The 3-Second Rule (6 min)

Say: "From across the room, someone should instantly get what your business does. That means one bold headline and a clean look — not a wall of text."

Big, clear business name + one-line what-you-do

One strong image or product visible

Clean, uncluttered table — less is more

The 30-Second Hook (6 min)

Say: "When someone stops, you need a quick, friendly hook — not your life story. Problem, solution, and one interesting thing."

Example: "Hi! We make fresh meal kits from local farms for busy South Bend families — dinner in 20 minutes, no grocery run. Want to see how it works?"

Engaging Guests + Capturing Contacts (8 min)

Say: "Do not stand behind your table on your phone. Stand beside it, make eye contact, and ask a question. And always have a way to capture interest."

Open with a question, not a pitch: "Have you ever...?"

Have a signup sheet, QR code, or form ready

Give people a reason to leave their info (updates, a freebie, early access)

Follow up within 48 hours — this is where the CRM comes back in

#### 8:05–8:50 AM — Open House Plan

Students plan their booth: headline, visual, what they'll display or demo, and how they'll capture contacts. Sketch the table layout.

Walk the room: "What does someone see from 10 feet away? What is your 30-second hook?"

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

Preview: "Tomorrow you build your actual materials."

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Build Booth Materials + Display

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"What is the one visual that will make people stop at your booth?" Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:45 AM — Build Day

Students build their materials: a poster or sign (Canva or by hand), a one-page handout or product card, a signup sheet or QR code, and any product samples or demo.

Reuse everything they already have: brand (Week 20), message (Week 19), campaign visuals (Weeks 21-24). Nothing here is from scratch.

Walk the room: "Is your headline readable from across the room? Is your table clean, not cluttered?"

#### 8:45–9:00 AM — Speaker Tour Prep

Distribute Speaker Tour Prep Worksheets.

### Wednesday

### Wednesday — Speaker/Tour

Run the speaker or tour per standard Wednesday format.

### Thursday

### Thursday — Pitch Practice + Booth Dry Run

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Give your 30-second hook to the person next to you." Everyone, at once. Then 3-4 share to the room.

#### 7:35–7:50 AM — Thank You Cards

Quick thank you cards for Wednesday's speaker. Specific, handwritten.

#### 7:50–8:50 AM — Booth Dry Run

Set up booths around the room. Half the class works their booth, half plays visitors. Then switch.

Visitors give feedback: "Did I understand your business in 5 seconds? Did the hook make me want to stay? Was it easy to leave my info?"

Facilitator: play a tough visitor — walk fast, look distracted. See who can earn your stop.

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Fix List

Each student writes their top 2 fixes before the real Open House.

### Friday

### Friday — Founder Fight + Final Prep + Week Reflection

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"What is the one fix you made to your booth since yesterday?" Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Founder Fight (Week 25 — Rotation: Founder Fight)

Presentation edition — students pitch assigned booths and the class judges which would draw a crowd:

Round 1: A flashy booth with a weak product vs. a plain booth with a strong product

Round 2: A founder who talks at visitors vs. one who asks questions

Round 3: A booth with a clear one-liner vs. one that explains everything at once

Debrief: "What actually makes people stop, stay, and remember you?"

#### 8:05–8:40 AM — Final Prep + 1-on-1s

Students finalize all materials. 1-on-1 focus: "Are you ready? What is your hook, and how will you capture contacts?"

#### 8:40–8:55 AM — Week Reflection

What is your business's one-line hook for the Open House?

How will you capture and follow up with people you meet?

What are you most excited — or nervous — to show?

#### 8:55–9:00 AM — Preview Week 26

Week 26: Unit Economics + Revenue Projections. "Marketing is done — now we make the numbers work. Next week: do you actually make money on each sale?"
