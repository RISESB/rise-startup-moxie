# Week 15: Crafting a Compelling Value Proposition + Pricing Models

Unit 2: Customer & Product

**Guiding question:** Why should someone choose you — and what should they pay?

_When it last ran: Week of December 1, 2025 · 7:30–9:00 AM · Mon–Fri. Dates are from a previous year; this year's calendar may differ._

## By the end of the week, students can
- Write a one-sentence value proposition that passes the "so what?" test.
- Explain 4 common pricing models and which fits their business.
- Calculate cost-plus, value-based, and competitive pricing for their offer.
- Connect pricing strategy to their target customer and competitive position.

## Lesson walkthrough
_Written as the facilitator's script for the classroom. Use it to know what was taught and how; don't read it back to students._

### Monday

### Monday — Lecture: Value Proposition + Pricing Models

Facilitator Goal: Students can write a clear value proposition and explain why their pricing is what it is.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"What do you currently charge — or plan to charge — and why that number?" Around the room. Listen for: "I guessed," "I looked at competitors," "I don't know." Most are guessing.

Say: "By Thursday you will have a defensible price — not a guess."

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: Value Proposition + Pricing

Opening (2 min)

Say: "Your value proposition answers: why should someone choose you? Not your category — specifically you, this version, at this price. And your price is not just a number. It is a signal — it tells the customer who you are, who you are for, and what the experience will be."

Value Proposition Framework (10 min)

Format: "We help [specific customer] [do/achieve/avoid something] by [your unique approach] — unlike [alternative], which [limitation]."

Example: "We help busy working parents in South Bend get fresh, affordable meal kits delivered to their door by partnering with local farms — unlike national meal kit services, which are too expensive and use produce shipped from across the country."

The "so what?" test: after your value proposition, someone asks "so what?" If you can't answer in one sentence, it is not specific enough.

Bad: "We help people eat better." — So what?

Good: "We help single parents spend less than $50 a week on healthy dinners without spending Sunday cooking." — So what? "More time with their kids and more money in their pocket."

Pricing Models (12 min)

Cost-Plus: Cost per unit × (1 + margin %). Example: costs $12, want 50% margin → price $18. Use for: physical products with clear, consistent costs.

Value-Based: Price based on what it is worth to the customer. Example: A tax preparer charges $300, saves customer $200 vs. H&R Block. Use for: services, expertise, time-saving solutions.

Competitive: Price relative to market — above (premium), at (competing on quality), or below (volume/penetration). Warning on below: hard to raise prices later.

Subscription/Recurring: Customer pays a regular fee for ongoing access. Use for: ongoing services, communities, content. Advantage: predictable revenue.

#### 8:05–8:50 AM — Value Prop Draft + Pricing Analysis

Students write 3 versions of their value proposition. Apply the "so what?" test. Pick the strongest. Then: which pricing model fits your business? Calculate your price.

Walk the room: "Say your value proposition. So what?" Keep pushing until the answer is specific and credible.

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

Preview Tuesday: "Tomorrow you stress-test your pricing and refine your value proposition with AI and peer feedback."

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Activities: Value Prop Workshop + Pricing Calculation

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Read your value proposition. One word from the room: compelling or unclear." Fast round.

#### 7:35–8:10 AM — Value Prop Stress Test

AI prompt: "Here is my value proposition: [paste]. What objections would a skeptical customer raise? What is the weakest word or phrase?"

Revise. Then pairs: one reads value prop, partner plays skeptical customer asking "so what?" and "why should I believe that?"

#### 8:10–8:45 AM — Pricing Calculation Workshop

Work through all three approaches:

Cost-plus: What does it cost you? What is your margin?

Value-based: What is the outcome worth to the customer?

Competitive: What are others charging? Where do you fit?

Students land on one final price with written rationale: "My price is $[X] because [reasoning]."

#### 8:45–9:00 AM — Speaker Tour Prep

If a guest speaker or site visit is scheduled this week, spend 10 minutes preparing questions. If not, use the time to refine pricing rationale with a partner.

### Wednesday

### Wednesday — Competitive Pricing + Revenue Modeling

Warm-Up: Would You Rather — Pricing Edition (5 min)

Post two pricing scenarios. Students choose and defend.

Charge $5 and sell 200 units OR charge $20 and sell 40 units?

Offer a free trial to 100 people OR charge $10 to 30 people upfront?

Debrief: both scenarios can generate the same revenue — pricing is about strategy, not just gut feel.

Competitive Pricing Analysis (20 min)

Students research 2-3 competitors and map pricing:

What do competitors charge?

What do customers get at each price point?

Where does my price sit — above, below, or at market rate?

If above market: what justifies it? If below: is that sustainable?

Revenue Modeling Intro (20 min)

Walk through a simple revenue projection: Price × Units Sold = Revenue. If I charge $15 and sell 20 units/month → $300/month → $3,600/year.

Students build their own monthly revenue model:

Realistic: how many units can I actually sell this month?

Goal: what would I need to sell to hit $500/month?

Stretch: what is the ceiling if everything goes right?

Closing Reflection (5 min): Exit ticket — "My realistic monthly revenue target is $____ because ____."

### Thursday

### Thursday — Speaker Recap + Thank You Cards + Pricing Work Day

Speaker Recap (15 min)

If a speaker visited or a site tour occurred this week, debrief:

What was the most useful thing you heard or saw?

What surprised you?

What will you apply to your business?

If no speaker, open with a 10-minute pricing share-out: two students present their pricing rationale and get class feedback.

Thank You Cards (10 min)

Handwritten. Specific. Required elements: one specific thing you learned, one way you are applying it, a genuine thank you.

Pricing Work Day (25 min)

Open work time. Students finalize: final price with written rationale, competitive pricing analysis, and monthly revenue model (realistic, goal, stretch).

1-on-1 focus: any student who has not set a real price yet or whose revenue model is blank. Goal: every student leaves with a price and a revenue target they can defend.

### Friday

### Friday — Profit or Broke + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

Warm-Up: Profit or Broke (15 min)

Present 3-4 business scenarios with revenue and cost numbers. Students vote: profitable or not? Reveal and discuss. Use scenarios close to students' own business models.

1-on-1 Check-Ins (25 min)

What is your price and why?

What is your realistic monthly revenue target?

What is the one thing blocking you from selling more?

Flag any student whose price is clearly unsustainable — too low to cover costs, or too high with no differentiation.

Week Reflection (10 min)

This week I figured out…

My price is $____ because…

Next week I need to…
