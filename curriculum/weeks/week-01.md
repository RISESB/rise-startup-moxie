# Week 1: What Is Entrepreneurship? Mindset, Practice, and Life Design

Unit 1: Foundations & Identity

**Guiding question:** What does it mean to think like an entrepreneur, and why does it matter?

## By the end of the week, students can
- Define entrepreneurship in their own words.
- Identify at least two entrepreneurial traits they already have.
- Apply the OODA Loop to a real problem they care about.
- Write one honest business idea or problem they want to explore.

- **Monday:** Lecture — What Is Entrepreneurship? + OODA Loop

### Monday

### Monday — What Is Entrepreneurship?

Facilitator Goal: Set the tone for the year. Students leave knowing what entrepreneurship actually means, how to use the OODA Loop, and feeling like this class is different from anything they have done before.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

This is day one. Goal is movement and connection — break the awkward energy fast.

Say: "Stand up. Find two people you don't already know well. Three minutes. Find out: their name, their school, and one thing they have done that they are proud of. Go."

Facilitator notes:

Start the timer visibly. If students freeze, physically point them toward someone.

Walk the room. Make eye contact. You are setting the expectation this is a participatory class.

At 3 minutes call them back. Ask: "Who learned something that surprised them?" — 3 students. Keep it fast.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: What Is Entrepreneurship? + OODA Loop

30 minutes. Do not go over.

Opening Question (2 min)

Say: "When I say the word entrepreneur, what comes to mind? Shout it out."

Expected: Elon Musk, Mark Zuckerberg, rich people, startups, risk, working for yourself.

Write a few on the board. Then: "Almost all of these are versions of the same myth. Let's break it."

Talking Point 1 — The Myth (3 min)

"Most people think entrepreneurship means starting a tech company, raising millions, and getting famous. That is one version — an incredibly narrow one."

"The people we study in school are the lottery winners of entrepreneurship. Interesting, but not the whole story."

"Entrepreneurship is happening right now in your neighborhood, at your dinner table, in your garage. It does not look like Silicon Valley. It looks like someone who saw a problem and decided to do something about it."

Talking Point 2 — A Better Definition (3 min)

Write on the board: "Entrepreneurship is the pursuit of opportunity beyond the resources you currently control."

Break it down:

"Pursuit of opportunity" — you see something others don't or aren't doing yet.

"Beyond the resources you currently control" — you don't wait until you have everything.

"Currently control" — what you have right now is enough to begin.

"Read that again. Nowhere does it say you need money or a degree. It says see an opportunity and start moving toward it."

Talking Point 3 — Types of Entrepreneurs (3 min)

"There is more than one way to be an entrepreneur:"

Startup founder — building a scalable product or tech company

Small business owner — a restaurant, shop, or service in your community

Social entrepreneur — solving a community or environmental problem

Intrapreneur — acting like an entrepreneur inside someone else's company

Side hustler — building something while keeping a job or going to school

Ask: "Which sounds most like you right now?" Let a few respond.

Talking Point 4 — The Entrepreneurial Mindset (4 min)

"This class is not just about starting a business. It is about how you think. Three core traits:"

See problems as opportunities — where most people see an annoyance, an entrepreneur sees a gap.

Take action with what you have — don't wait for perfect conditions. They don't exist.

Learn from failure instead of stopping — the question is never "did I fail?" It's "what did I learn?"

"By May, you should be able to look at any problem and immediately start thinking about solutions."

Talking Point 5 — The OODA Loop (7 min)

"Entrepreneurs make decisions fast — often with incomplete information. Best tool for this: the OODA Loop."

Write on the board: O — O — D — A

O — OBSERVE: What is actually happening? What are you seeing and noticing?

O — ORIENT: What does it mean? How does it connect to what you already know?

D — DECIDE: What is your move? Not the perfect move — a move.

A — ACT: Execute it. Then loop back to Observe.

Example — say this out loud:

"You are selling cookies at school. You OBSERVE people keep asking for chocolate chip but you only make sugar. You ORIENT — this keeps happening, it's a pattern. You DECIDE to add chocolate chip next batch. You ACT — go home and bake them. Then loop back: did they sell?"

"You did not wait for a market research report. You observed, oriented, decided, acted. That is entrepreneurship."

Ask: "Where else in your life could you use the OODA Loop?" Let 2-3 students respond.

Closing the Lecture (3 min)

"Entrepreneurship is a method. A way of moving through the world. You don't need a business idea to be an entrepreneur — you just need to start practicing the mindset."

"Some of you will start a business this year. All of you will think differently by May."

#### 8:05–8:20 AM — Small Group + Whole Group Discussion

Pairs or trios. Prompt: "Think of a problem in your school, neighborhood, or daily life that genuinely annoys you. Could it be a business?"

5 minutes. Walk the room. If stuck: "What takes too long? What can you never find? What costs too much?"

Call on 3-4 groups. Write problems on the board. Ask: "Which of these could actually be a business?"

Close: "This is what entrepreneurs do every single day — walk through the world looking for problems."

#### 8:20–8:50 AM — Activity: My Entrepreneurial Identity

Paper and pen. Three columns:

Column 1: Problems I Notice — at least 3 you actually care about

Column 2: Things I'm Good At — at least 3 real skills, not just "nice" or "smart"

Column 3: People I Want to Help — 1-2 specific groups

"This is the beginning of your entrepreneurial identity. We come back to this all year. Be honest — vague answers are not useful."

Walk the room. Push shallow answers. If finished early: "Do any columns connect? That is your starting point."

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close + Goal Setting

"Write one sentence at the bottom of your paper: This year I want to figure out or build _______."

"Share it with the person next to you. Not for feedback — just to say it out loud."

Collect sheets or have students keep them in a folder. They will revisit these all year.

Preview: "Tomorrow we go deeper on entrepreneurial traits and get ready for Matt Sheldon on Wednesday."

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Entrepreneurial Traits + Speaker Tour Prep

Facilitator Goal: Students identify entrepreneurial traits through real stories, then prepare genuinely good questions for Matt Sheldon on Wednesday.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

Say: "Find someone whose business idea or problem from yesterday is completely different from yours. 2 minutes. Go."

When back: "What did you find out?" — 2-3 pairs. Keep it fast.

#### 7:35–8:00 AM — Case Study Activity: Spot the Trait

"I am going to read three short stories. After each one, tell me what trait you see — not from a list, from your gut."

Story 1 — Sara Blakely (Spanx)

Read aloud: "Sara Blakely was selling fax machines door to door. No fashion background, no investors, no connections. She cut the feet off her pantyhose because she wanted a smoother look under white pants — and thought, why doesn't this product exist? She spent her $5,000 in savings, cold-called hosiery mills until one agreed, and drove the product to a Neiman Marcus buyer herself. Spanx is now worth over a billion dollars."

Ask: "What trait do you see?" Pause. Let them respond.

Expected: Resourcefulness, persistence, courage, taking action with what she had.

Add: "She did not wait for permission or the right background. She saw a problem she personally had and acted on it."

Story 2 — Daymond John (FUBU)

Read aloud: "Daymond John grew up in Queens. Couldn't afford the hats his friends wore so he started sewing his own. Made $800 in one day selling 40 hats — no training, no manufacturer, no connections. Kept making them in his mom's house. Got LL Cool J to wear a FUBU hat in a Gap ad without Gap knowing. FUBU went on to generate $6 billion in revenue."

Ask: "What trait do you see?"

Expected: Creativity, hustle, resourcefulness, finding a way around obstacles.

Add: "He used what he had — his mom's house, his friend's fame, his own hands. Classic OODA Loop."

Story 3 — Local Prompt

Ask: "Do you know anyone — family, neighbor, community — who built something from nothing? Tell me about them."

Let a student tell it. Ask: "What trait do you see?" Makes it personal — not just famous people on a screen.

Introduce the 6 Entrepreneurial Traits

Write on the board:

\1. Curiosity — always asking why, always learning

\2. Resilience — bouncing back when things go wrong

\3. Adaptability — changing direction when something isn't working

\4. Decisiveness — making a call even without perfect information

\5. Vision — seeing what could be, not just what is

\6. Collaboration — knowing you cannot do it alone

"Go back to the three stories. Which traits showed up? Map them."

Trait Mapping — Tactile Activity (10 min)

Give each student a sheet with the 6 traits. Rate yourself 1-5 on each. Not a grade.

1 = Not me at all.  5 = One of my real strengths.

"Circle your top 2 — assets. Underline your bottom 2 — growth edges."

Pair share: "Tell your partner one top trait and one real example of when you showed it." Push for stories, not just words.

#### 8:00–8:45 AM — Speaker Tour Prep: Matt Sheldon

Distribute Speaker Tour Prep Sheets.

"Wednesday we have Matt Sheldon coming in. Your job: show up prepared, not just present. That means you know who he is, what he does, and have a question worth asking."

Fill in together (5 min): Name, Position, Company, Industry.

Research (10 min): "Open laptops. Research Matt and his company. Fill in Background and Company + Industry. How did he get started? What is unusual about his story?"

Write questions (15 min): "At least two questions you genuinely want to ask. Rule: if you can Google it in 5 seconds, it is not a good question."

Bad questions:

"When was your company founded?"

"How many employees do you have?"

Good questions:

"What was the hardest decision you've ever made in your business?"

"Was there a moment you almost quit? What happened?"

"If you were starting over with nothing, what would you do first?"

#### 8:45–9:00 AM — Share Questions + Close

Around the room — one question each, no repeats. Write the best on the board. Class votes on top 3-5 to ask Wednesday.

"Bring your prep sheet Wednesday. If Matt says something interesting, write it down."

### Wednesday

### Wednesday — Guest Speaker: Matt Sheldon

Facilitator Goal: Students engage actively, connect Matt's story to the week's content, and practice professional listening and questioning.

#### 7:30–7:45 AM — Set Expectations

"Phones away, eyes up, prep sheets out. Listen for three things:"

A moment where you see one of the 6 entrepreneurial traits in action

Something that connects to the OODA Loop

One thing that surprises you

"Write them in the Notes section of your prep sheet as he talks."

Quick discussion: "If you had one minute with a founder, what would you most want to know?" 2-3 students share.

#### 7:45–8:00 AM — Introduce Matt

3-4 sentences max. Do not read a long bio. One sentence on what he does, one interesting personal detail, then hand it over.

#### 8:00–8:35 AM — Matt Talks

Let Matt lead. Listen actively. Prompts if conversation stalls:

"What was the moment you realized this was actually going to work?"

"Tell us about a time you had to decide with no good options."

"What does failure look like in your world and how do you handle it?"

#### 8:35–8:50 AM — Student-Led Q&A

Use top questions from Tuesday's board. Call on students by name. After each answer: "Does anyone have a follow-up?"

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

"In your Notes section, write one sentence: What is the most important thing Matt said today?" 2 minutes. Then 2-3 students share.

Thank Matt in front of the class. Group photo.

"Tomorrow we write thank you cards and debrief. Come ready to share your takeaways."

### Thursday

### Thursday — Speaker Recap + Thank You Cards + Work Day

Facilitator Goal: Students process what they heard from Matt, practice professional follow-up, and keep building their entrepreneurial identity.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"In one sentence — no more, no less — what is the most interesting thing Matt said yesterday? Around the room. No repeats."

Keep it fast. Forces students to process Wednesday.

#### 7:35–7:55 AM — Speaker Recap

Two columns on the board: "What Matt said" | "What it means for me"

Pull 4-5 key moments. For each: "What does this mean for you? For your idea? For how you think?"

Connect back:

"Where did you see the OODA Loop in Matt's story?"

"Which of the 6 traits showed up most?"

"Did anything he said change how you think about entrepreneurship?"

#### 7:55–8:20 AM — Thank You Cards

"Real entrepreneurs know how to follow up. A handwritten thank you card is a professional skill. Most adults don't do it. You will."

Write on the board — required:

\1. One specific thing you learned — not generic

\2. One question his talk made you think about

\3. A genuine, specific thank you

Bad example (write on board): "Dear Matt, thank you for coming. It was great. I learned a lot."

"This tells Matt nothing. He thinks you weren't listening. Redo it."

Good example: "Dear Matt, the thing that stuck with me was when you said [specific quote]. It made me wonder [question]. Thank you for being honest about the hard parts — most people only talk about the wins."

Walk the room. Hand back any generic card. Do not accept "it was great."

#### 8:20–9:00 AM — Work Day

Return to Entrepreneurial Identity sheet from Monday. Review and add based on the week.

If finished early: "Write a one-sentence business idea: I want to help [who] do [what] by [how]. Write three versions."

Use this time for informal 1-on-1s — sit with 3-4 students, ask what they're working on, give specific feedback.

### Friday

### Friday — Founder Fight + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

Facilitator Goal: End the week with energy and connection. Students leave feeling like this class is worth showing up for every week.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Turn to the person next to you. Tell them one thing from this week that surprised you. Go."

90 seconds. Then: "Who wants to share what their partner said?" 2-3 students.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Founder Fight (Week 1 of Friday Rotation)

Friday rotation: Founder Fight → Deal or No Deal → Profit or Broke → repeat. Week 1 = Founder Fight.

What it is: Students argue for a randomly assigned business — not one they chose. Builds persuasion, critical thinking, and quick thinking.

Setup: Two students at the front. Each assigned a random business. Class is the judge.

Rules — say out loud before starting:

"60 seconds to argue why YOUR business is better."

"Argue for what you were assigned — not your personal opinion."

"No interrupting. After both, class votes thumbs up A or B."

"Winner stays. New challenger steps up."

Week 1 matchups:

Round 1: Dog walking app vs. Food truck

Round 2: Tutoring service vs. Clothing brand

Round 3: Lawn care business vs. Social media management

During: Keep energy high. If struggling, prompt: "What problem does your business solve? Who is your customer?"

Debrief (5 min): "What made the winning arguments stronger?"

Expected: Specifics, knowing the customer, examples, confidence, answering the "so what."

"Those are the same things that make a real pitch work. Every Friday we rotate — Founder Fight, Deal or No Deal, Profit or Broke. Each builds a real skill."

#### 8:05–8:40 AM — 1-on-1 Sessions

Students work independently: journal, Entrepreneurial Identity sheet, or one-sentence business idea.

Pull students one at a time for 3-4 minute check-ins.

Questions:

"What's sticking with you from this week?"

"What confused you or didn't land?"

"What is your business idea looking like right now?"

"What do you need from me this year?"

Most important 3 minutes of your week with each student. No idea yet — ask what problems they notice. Big idea — help them narrow to a first step.

#### 8:40–8:55 AM — Week Reflection

Bring class back together. Ask:

"What is entrepreneurship in your own words — not the textbook version?"

"What's one trait you want to work on this year?"

"What problem are you most interested in solving?"

4-5 students per question. If energy drops, call on someone by name.

"Entrepreneurship is a method. You started practicing it this week. Next week: your WHY — values, strengths, and what kind of life you want to design. See you Monday."

Week 2: Finding Your Why — Values, Strengths, and Life Design (Ikigai + Wingfinder + 16 Personalities).

Weekend prompt: "What would you do for free that also helps other people?"
