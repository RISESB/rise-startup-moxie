## Lesson walkthrough
_Written as the facilitator's script for the classroom. Use it to know what was taught and how; don't read it back to students._

### Monday

### Monday — Lecture: MVP Principles + Early User Testing

Facilitator Goal: Students know exactly what their MVP is, what it is not, and what a good early user test looks like.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"What is the current state of your May-B-P? Still planning, started building, or already tested?" Show of hands for each. Note the distribution.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: MVP + Early User Testing

Opening (2 min)

Say: "Today we cross the most important line: we start actually building something real enough that a person outside this class can react to it. The goal is not perfect. The goal is real."

MVP by Business Type (8 min)

Service business: a one-time version of the service delivered to one paying customer

Product business: a handmade or manually produced version — no factory, no inventory

Digital/app: a mockup, landing page, or manually delivered version of what the app will eventually automate

Content/media: one piece of content distributed to a real audience to measure engagement

Say: "Notice what is not on this list: a full website, polished logo, business entity, or finished product."

What Makes a Good Early User Test (8 min)

Say: "Testing your MVP is not asking people what they think. It is watching what they do."

The right user — someone who matches your persona, not your friends

The right context — let them experience it as naturally as possible, without explaining everything first

The right questions — after: What did you expect that didn't happen? What confused you? What would make you come back?

Say: "The hardest part is shutting up. When someone is confused, the instinct is to explain. Do not. Their confusion is data."

Vibe Coding Introduction (5 min)

Say: "For students building a digital product, we are introducing vibe coding — using AI to build a basic app, website, or digital prototype with no programming experience required. Tools: Bolt.new, Glide, Carrd, Cursor. This is an option — not required. If your business is a service or physical product, you will not need it."

#### 8:05–8:50 AM — MVP Definition + Test Plan

Each student writes:

My MVP is: [one sentence]

My first early user is: [name or specific description]

I will test it by: [what they will do, watch, and ask]

I will know it worked if: [success looks like this]

I will know I need to change if: [failure looks like this]

Walk the room: "Is this testable by Friday? Who is your first early user — do you have their name?"

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

Tomorrow: "Build day. Digital builders explore vibe coding. Physical and service businesses build and launch."

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Activities: MVP Build Day + Vibe Coding Option

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"What is the one thing standing between you and launching your MVP today?" Around the room. Address the patterns.

#### 7:35–7:45 AM — Split the Room

Digital product builders: Vibe Coding track. Physical product / service builders: MVP build and test execution track.

#### 7:45–8:45 AM — BUILD TIME

VIBE CODING TRACK:

Open bolt.new or preferred tool. Type your product idea as a detailed prompt — describe what the app should do, who uses it, what the main screen looks like. Iterate: run the prompt, see output, give AI feedback to improve. You are directing the AI like a project manager. Be specific.

PHYSICAL / SERVICE TRACK:

Make the first batch of product in class or at home tonight

Deliver the first version of the service to a real customer this week

Set up a simple sales channel (Google Form, social post, flyer) and launch today

Walk the room: "When is your first user experiencing this? What do you need from me right now?"

#### 8:45–9:00 AM — Speaker Tour Prep

Distribute Speaker Tour Prep Worksheets.

### Friday

### Friday — Profit or Broke + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"MVP test update — one sentence. What happened, what changed, what is next?" Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Profit or Broke (Week 12 — Rotation: Profit or Broke)

Scenario 1: "You spend 3 days building an MVP. First customer pays $40. You spent $25 in materials. Profit or Broke?"

Answer: $40 - $25 = $15 Profit. Follow up: "At 12 hours of work, how much did you make per hour?"

Scenario 2: "You build a vibe-coded landing page in 2 hours. You run a $20 Instagram ad. 3 people sign up for a waitlist. Profit or Broke?"

Answer: Spent $20, no revenue yet. BROKE — but with 3 leads. Follow up: "What is a waitlist signup worth? What do you do next?"

Scenario 3: "You offer a service at $75, deliver it once, customer refers 2 more people. Cost: $10. Profit or Broke?"

Answer: $75 - $10 = $65. Follow up: "What is a referral worth? How do you account for that in your model?"

#### 8:05–8:40 AM — 1-on-1 Sessions

1-on-1 focus: "What did you learn from testing? How are you iterating? When is the next test?"

#### 8:40–8:55 AM — Week Reflection

What is the most important thing you learned from putting your MVP in front of a real person?

What assumption got confirmed or disproven?

What is the next version of your MVP?
