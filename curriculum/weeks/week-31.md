# Week 31: Business Analytics + Data-Driven Decisions

Unit 4: Finance & Operations

**Guiding question:** What are the few numbers that actually tell you how your business is doing — and how do you use them to decide?

## By the end of the week, students can
- Identify the key metrics (KPIs) that matter for their business.
- Build a simple dashboard to track those numbers.
- Read data to spot a trend, not just a single number.
- Make one real decision based on their data.

- **Common Small-Business KPIs:** Revenue · units/customers · conversion · acquisition cost · repeat rate.
- **Match KPIs to Your Goal:** Goal = growth → watch new customers. Goal = profit → watch margin & repeat rate.

- **Reading Trends:** $100 this week means nothing until you know last week. Compare across time.
- **The Data Loop:** Track → notice a trend → ask why → make one change → track again. (OODA, Week 1.)
- **Choose Your KPIs:** Pick 3–5 that match your goal. Cross out the vanity metrics.
- **Close / Preview:** Tomorrow: build a dashboard to track these automatically.

## Lesson walkthrough
_Written as the facilitator's script for the classroom. Use it to know what was taught and how; don't read it back to students._

### Monday

### Monday — Lecture: KPIs + Data-Driven Decisions

Facilitator Goal: Students learn to pick the few numbers that matter and use data to decide instead of guessing.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"What is one number you could check today that would tell you if your business is healthy?" Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: Analytics

Opening (2 min)

Say: "You have been collecting numbers all year — sales, costs, campaign results. Analytics is turning those numbers into decisions. The best founders are not the ones with the most data. They are the ones who watch the RIGHT few numbers and act on them."

What Is a KPI? (6 min)

Say: "KPI stands for Key Performance Indicator — a number that actually predicts the health of your business. Key is the important word. You could track 50 things. You should track 3-5."

Common small-business KPIs:

Revenue — money in over a period

Units sold / customers served

Conversion rate — how many interested people actually buy

Customer acquisition cost — what it costs to get one customer

Repeat rate — how many come back

"Pick the ones that match YOUR goal. If your goal is growth, watch new customers. If your goal is profit, watch margin and repeat rate."

Vanity Metrics vs. Real Metrics (6 min)

Say: "A vanity metric looks good but doesn't drive decisions. Likes and followers feel great but don't pay you. A real metric connects to money or customers."

"1,000 followers and 0 sales is a vanity number. 40 followers and 12 buyers is a real one. Watch what matters, not what flatters."

Reading Trends (8 min)

Say: "One number alone means little. The power is in the trend — is it going up, down, or flat over time?"

"$100 in sales this week means nothing until you know last week was $60 (growing) or $200 (falling). Always compare across time. That is where the story is."

Data-Driven Decisions (6 min)

The loop: Track → Notice a trend → Ask why → Make one change → Track again. "This is the OODA Loop from Week 1, powered by your actual numbers."

#### 8:05–8:50 AM — Choose Your KPIs

Students pick 3-5 KPIs that match their current goal and write why each one matters. Cross out any vanity metrics they were secretly proud of.

Walk the room: "Does this number connect to money or customers? If not, is it really key?"

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

Preview: "Tomorrow you build a dashboard to track these automatically."

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Activities: Build Your Dashboard

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Name your #1 KPI and what it is right now." Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:20 AM — Dashboard Build

Students build a simple dashboard in a spreadsheet: their KPIs down one side, weeks across the top, and a spot to log each number over time. Add a simple chart if they can.

Fill in whatever real data they have from the year (sales, campaign results, CRM). Even a few weeks of history shows a trend.

AI prompt: "I want to track [KPIs] for my business in Google Sheets. How should I set up a simple dashboard, and what formula gives me week-over-week change?"

#### 8:20–8:45 AM — Read Your Data

Students write down: "My clearest trend is ___. It is going up/down/flat. My best guess for why is ___."

#### 8:45–9:00 AM — Speaker Tour Prep

Distribute Speaker Tour Prep Worksheets.

### Wednesday

### Wednesday — Speaker/Tour

Run the speaker or tour per standard Wednesday format. Prompt: "Ask the speaker what numbers they check every week."

### Thursday

### Thursday — Speaker Recap + Thank You Cards + Data Work Day

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"One sentence — most interesting thing from Wednesday." Around the room.

#### 7:35–7:55 AM — Speaker Recap

Two columns: "What we heard" | "What number should I start tracking because of it?"

#### 7:55–8:20 AM — Thank You Cards

Handwritten. Specific. Required elements: one specific observation, one question it sparked, a genuine thank you.

#### 8:20–9:00 AM — Data Work Day

Students finalize the dashboard in their portfolio and make one real decision from it: "My data says ___, so I am going to ___." 1-on-1 focus: "What is your data telling you, and what will you do about it?"

### Friday

### Friday — Founder Fight + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"One decision you are making this week because of your data." Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Founder Fight (Week 31 — Rotation: Founder Fight)

Data edition:

Round 1: A founder who decides by gut vs. one who decides by data

Round 2: A business with 5,000 followers and few sales vs. one with 50 loyal paying customers

Round 3: A founder who tracks weekly vs. one who checks numbers once a year

Debrief: "When is gut instinct still useful? When does data have to win?"

#### 8:05–8:40 AM — 1-on-1 Sessions

1-on-1 focus: "Show me your dashboard. What is your strongest trend and what are you doing about it?"

#### 8:40–8:55 AM — Week Reflection

What are your key metrics, and why those?

What is the clearest trend in your data?

What decision did your data help you make?

#### 8:55–9:00 AM — Preview Week 32

Week 32: Team Roles + Operational Planning. "Next week: who does what, and how you plan the work so the business runs smoothly."
