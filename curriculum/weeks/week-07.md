# Week 7: Matchmaking + Walk, Talk, Show Up: Deepening Discovery

Unit 1: Foundations & Identity

**Guiding question:** Who is already doing the work you want to do — and what can you learn from getting close to them?

## By the end of the week, students can
- Return to Walk, Talk, Show Up with more focused eyes after Unit 1 work.
- Identify mentors, peers, and resources in their entrepreneurial ecosystem.
- Strengthen Discovery Tracker entries with deeper 5 Whys analysis.
- Connect personal WHY and May-B-P to real community observations.

## Lesson walkthrough
_Written as the facilitator's script for the classroom. Use it to know what was taught and how; don't read it back to students._

### Monday

### Monday — Lecture: Entrepreneurial Ecosystem + Matchmaking

Facilitator Goal: Students understand that entrepreneurship is not a solo sport. They can map their own ecosystem and identify specific people and resources they should be connecting with.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Think about someone outside this classroom — a person in your community, your family, your neighborhood — who is doing something entrepreneurial. What are they doing?" 3-4 students share.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: Ecosystem + Matchmaking

Opening (2 min)

Say: "In Unit 1 you found a problem, built a BMC, drafted a May-B-P, and pitched. Now we go deeper. The next phase is about building the relationships and resources that make your idea real."

"You do not build a business alone. You build it with an ecosystem."

Talking Point 1 — What Is an Entrepreneurial Ecosystem? (8 min)

Say: "An entrepreneurial ecosystem is the network of people, organizations, and resources that support entrepreneurs in a given community."

Draw on the board — 5 rings around the student:

Mentors — people who have done what you want to do

Peers — other entrepreneurs at your stage

Customers — the people whose problem you are solving

Resources — money, tools, spaces, programs

Champions — people who will open doors for you

Say: "Most people only think about customers. But the strongest entrepreneurs invest in all five rings. Each one accelerates your growth."

Talking Point 2 — Matchmaking (8 min)

Say: "Matchmaking is the process of intentionally connecting yourself with the right people at the right time — not waiting for it to happen."

"There are three questions that drive matchmaking:"

Who has solved a version of my problem before?

Who knows my potential customer better than I do?

Who has resources or access that I do not have yet?

"Once you know the answers, your job is to find those people and show up with something to offer — not just something to ask for."

Talking Point 3 — Walk, Talk, Show Up Revisited (8 min)

Say: "You used Walk, Talk, Show Up in Unit 1 to find problems. Now we use it again — this time to find people and resources."

"Same three moves, different lens:"

Walk — move through spaces where your ecosystem exists. Farmers markets, business districts, community events, makerspaces.

Talk — have real conversations. Not pitching. Asking. "How did you start? What do you wish you had known?"

Show Up — be present in spaces where your ecosystem gathers. Volunteer. Contribute. Be seen.

Close: "This week we build your ecosystem map and identify your top 3 matchmaking targets — the three people you most need to connect with right now."

#### 8:05–8:50 AM — Ecosystem Mapping (First Draft)

Students draw their ecosystem on paper — themselves in the center, five rings around them. Fill in what they know already.

Walk the room. Push: "Who in your life fits in the Mentors ring? Even if you are not connected yet — who should be there?"

After 15 minutes: "Circle the three biggest gaps in your ecosystem. Those are your matchmaking targets."

Pairs share. Ask: "What does your partner's ecosystem have that yours does not? Could you connect them to someone in your network?"

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

Preview Tuesday: "Tomorrow we go deeper on the Discovery Tracker and finalize your ecosystem map with your matchmaking targets named."

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Activities: Discovery Walk Debrief + Ecosystem Mapping

Facilitator Goal: Students finalize ecosystem maps, identify 3 matchmaking targets by name (or description), and reconnect their Discovery Tracker observations to real people they could talk to.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Who is one person outside this room who would be a valuable addition to your ecosystem — and why?" Around the room fast.

#### 7:35–8:00 AM — Ecosystem Map: Final Version

Students complete their ecosystem map. This time: every person or organization they identify must have a name or a specific description — not "a mentor" but "someone who runs a food business in South Bend."

Top 3 matchmaking targets: Name them. Write one sentence on why each one and how you would Show Up for them first.

#### 8:00–8:30 AM — Discovery Tracker + Ecosystem Connection

Students review their Discovery Tracker from Unit 1. For each strong observation: "Is there someone in my ecosystem who is already trying to solve this? Who could I Talk to about it?"

Add a new column to the tracker: "Who should I talk to about this?"

#### 8:30–8:50 AM — Outreach Planning

Using the outreach skills from Week 5: students draft one outreach message to one of their three matchmaking targets.

Use AI to help draft. Then edit until it sounds like them.

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Speaker Tour Prep

Distribute Speaker Tour Prep Worksheets. Prepare for Wednesday.

### Wednesday

### Wednesday — Speaker/Tour

Run the speaker or tour per standard Wednesday format.

### Thursday

### Thursday — Speaker Recap + Thank You Cards + Discovery Deep Dive

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"One sentence — most interesting thing from Wednesday." Around the room.

#### 7:35–7:55 AM — Speaker Recap

Two columns: "What we heard" | "Does this person belong in someone's ecosystem map?"

Ask: "Based on what you heard — which ring of the ecosystem did this speaker represent for you?"

#### 7:55–8:20 AM — Thank You Cards

Handwritten. Specific. Required elements:

One specific thing you heard or observed

One question it sparked

A genuine, specific thank you

#### 8:20–9:00 AM — Discovery Tracker Work + 1-on-1s

Students strengthen Discovery Tracker entries and ecosystem maps. 1-on-1 focus: "Who are your three matchmaking targets? Have you reached out yet?"

### Friday

### Friday — Founder Fight + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Tell your partner: who is one person you are going to reach out to this week from your ecosystem map and what will you say?" 90 seconds.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Founder Fight (Week 7 — Rotation: Founder Fight)

Same format as before. Two students, assigned businesses, 60 seconds each, class votes. This week: tie businesses to ecosystem themes.

Round 1: A solo freelance graphic designer vs. a small design agency with 3 employees

Round 2: An entrepreneur who bootstraps with no outside help vs. one who actively builds a mentor network

Round 3: A business that markets only online vs. one that relies entirely on community relationships

Debrief: "Which of these businesses has the stronger ecosystem? Why does that matter?"

#### 8:05–8:40 AM — 1-on-1 Sessions

1-on-1 focus: "Show me your ecosystem map. Who is missing? Who have you already reached out to?"

#### 8:40–8:55 AM — Week Reflection

What is the biggest gap in your entrepreneurial ecosystem right now?

Who is one person you plan to connect with before next Friday?

How does your ecosystem connect back to your May-B-P?

#### 8:55–9:00 AM — Preview Week 8

Week 8: Creativity + Design Thinking in Action. "Next week we learn how the best entrepreneurs use creative problem-solving — not as a personality trait, but as a repeatable method."
