# Week 3: Observing Your World: Walk, Talk, Show Up

Unit 1: Foundations & Identity

**Guiding question:** What problems are hiding in plain sight — and how do you train yourself to see them?

## By the end of the week, students can
- Explain the Walk, Talk, Show Up discovery method.
- Use the 5 Whys to dig beneath surface-level problems.
- Complete a Discovery Tracker with real observations from the community.
- Identify at least one problem worth exploring further.

## The week at a glance
- **Monday:** Lecture — Walk, Talk, Show Up + 5 Whys
- **Tuesday:** Activities — Discovery Tracker + 5 Whys Practice
- **Wednesday:** Speaker/Tour + Discovery in the Field
- **Thursday:** Speaker Recap + Thank You Cards + Discovery Tracker Review
- **Friday:** Profit or Broke + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

## Materials
- Printed Discovery Tracker (one per student)
- Journals or notebooks for field observations
- Whiteboard and markers
- Speaker Tour Prep Worksheets
- Thank you cards and pens

## Lesson walkthrough
_Written as the facilitator's script for the classroom. Use it to know what was taught and how; don't read it back to students._

### Monday

### Monday — Lecture: Walk, Talk, Show Up + 5 Whys

Facilitator Goal: Students understand the Walk, Talk, Show Up framework as a discovery method and know how to use the 5 Whys to get beneath surface-level observations.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"On your way to school today — or sometime this week — what did you notice that seemed broken, inefficient, or annoying? Tell the person next to you."

90 seconds. Call on 3-4 students. Write their observations on the board. Say: "Hold onto these — we are going to use them today."

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: Walk, Talk, Show Up + 5 Whys

30 minutes.

Opening (2 min)

Say: "The biggest mistake early entrepreneurs make is building something nobody actually needs. They have an idea, they fall in love with it, they build it — and then nobody buys it."

"The way to avoid that is to start with the problem, not the solution. And the way to find real problems is to go look for them — not sit in a classroom and guess."

Talking Point 1 — Walk (5 min)

Say: "Walk means you physically move through the world with your eyes open — looking for friction, gaps, and problems."

"Most people walk through their neighborhood every day and see nothing because they are not looking. Entrepreneurs train themselves to notice."

Three things to look for on a Walk:

Friction — something that is harder than it should be

Gaps — something that is missing or underserved

Patterns — something you see more than once, in more than one place

Say: "A Walk does not have to be literal. It can be scrolling through a community Facebook group, reading reviews on Yelp, sitting in a waiting room. Anywhere people experience problems."

Talking Point 2 — Talk (5 min)

Say: "Talk means you engage people in natural, ad-hoc conversations about what you are observing — not a formal interview, not a survey. Just a real conversation."

"The goal is not to pitch your idea. The goal is to understand their experience. Big difference."

What good Talk looks like:

"I noticed [observation]. Does that happen to you a lot?"

"What do you do when [problem] happens?"

"What do you wish existed for [situation]?"

What bad Talk looks like:

"I have this idea for an app — what do you think?"

"Would you buy this if I made it?"

Say: "The second set of questions gets you what you want to hear. The first set gets you what you need to know."

Talking Point 3 — Show Up (5 min)

Say: "Show Up means you put yourself in the spaces where your potential customers already are — not to sell, but to understand."

"It also means operating with a give-first mentality. You show up and contribute before you ask for anything."

Examples:

If you want to solve a problem for small business owners — go to a local business networking event.

If you want to solve a problem for athletes — show up at practices, games, the locker room culture.

If you want to solve a problem for your school — eat lunch in different spots, talk to different groups.

Say: "Show Up expands your luck surface area. The more places you are, the more you observe, the more conversations you have — the more likely you are to find the real problem worth solving."

Talking Point 4 — The 5 Whys (8 min)

Say: "Here is the problem with most observations: they are surface level. The 5 Whys is a tool that helps you dig deeper until you find the root cause."

How it works: Start with an observation. Ask WHY. Take the answer and ask WHY again. Repeat 5 times.

Walk through this example on the board:

Observation: "The school cafeteria always has long lines."

Why? — There are only 2 registers open during peak lunch.

Why? — Only 2 staff are scheduled during lunch.

Why? — Budget only covers 2 staff for that shift.

Why? — The school has not prioritized cafeteria staffing in the budget.

Why? — There is no data showing how much student productivity is lost during long lunch lines.

Say: "Now we went from 'long lines' to 'a data problem.' Those are very different problems — and they lead to very different solutions."

"The 5 Whys tells you where the real opportunity is."

Closing (2 min)

Say: "This week your job is to go be a detective. Walk your world. Talk to people. Show up somewhere new. And when you find something interesting — ask why five times."

"Wednesday we do this together as a class. Tomorrow you practice the tools."

#### 8:05–8:20 AM — Small Group Discussion

Go back to the warm-up observations on the board. Pick one as a class.

Walk through the 5 Whys together as a group. Let students drive each "why."

Show how far the root cause is from the original observation.

#### 8:20–8:50 AM — Discovery Tracker Introduction

Distribute the Discovery Tracker.

Say: "This is your field notebook for the next few weeks. Every time you observe a problem, have a conversation, or show up somewhere new — you log it here."

Walk through the columns:

Observation — what did you see or hear?

Where/When — context matters

Who is affected — be specific

5 Whys — dig to the root

Possible opportunity — what could this become?

"Start filling it in right now. Use the observations from your warm-up. You should have at least 3 entries by end of week."

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

Preview Tuesday: "Tomorrow we practice the 5 Whys on real problems and start preparing for the speaker/tour on Wednesday."

Homework: "Between now and tomorrow, write down 2 new observations in your Discovery Tracker. They can be anywhere — home, your neighborhood, your commute."

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Activities: Discovery Tracker + 5 Whys Practice

Facilitator Goal: Students practice the 5 Whys on real observations and deepen their Discovery Tracker entries. Speaker Tour Prep for Wednesday.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"What did you observe since yesterday? Any new entries in your Discovery Tracker?" Quick share — 3-4 students. Write new observations on the board.

#### 7:35–8:00 AM — 5 Whys Practice Activity

Small groups of 3. Each group picks one observation from their Discovery Trackers.

Task: Run the full 5 Whys on that observation together. Write each "why" on paper or a whiteboard section.

After 10 minutes: Each group shares their observation and their root cause. Class responds: "Does the root cause surprise you? Does it change what the solution might look like?"

Facilitator note: Push groups that stop at obvious answers. "That is why 2 — keep going. What causes THAT?"

#### 8:00–8:15 AM — Discovery Tracker Deep Dive

Individual work. Students review their Discovery Tracker and apply the 5 Whys to at least one observation they have not dug into yet.

Walk the room. Sit with students one at a time. Ask: "What is the most interesting problem you have found so far? Why does it matter to you?"

#### 8:15–8:50 AM — Speaker Tour Prep

Distribute Speaker Tour Prep Worksheets. Students research and prepare for Wednesday's speaker or tour.

Remind them: good questions are not Google-able. Come with questions about their journey, their decisions, and what they have learned.

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

"Tomorrow we take this outside. Come ready to observe, listen, and take notes. Bring your Discovery Tracker."

### Wednesday

### Wednesday — Speaker/Tour + Discovery in the Field

Facilitator Goal: Students apply Walk, Talk, Show Up in a real-world context during the speaker or tour.

Run the speaker or tour per the standard Wednesday format. Prep sheets out, active listening.

Before leaving or starting: "Your job today is not just to listen. It is to observe like an entrepreneur. What problems do you see in this environment? What gaps exist? What could be better? Add observations to your Discovery Tracker during or right after."

### Thursday

### Thursday — Speaker Recap + Thank You Cards + Discovery Tracker Review

Facilitator Goal: Process Wednesday, write thank you cards, and strengthen Discovery Tracker entries.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"One sentence — what is the most interesting problem you observed this week?" Around the room, no repeats.

#### 7:35–7:55 AM — Speaker Recap

Two columns on the board: "What we observed" | "What it means for our ideas"

Pull 3-4 moments from Wednesday. For each: "Does this connect to any observation in your Discovery Tracker?"

#### 7:55–8:20 AM — Thank You Cards

Handwritten. Specific. Required elements on the board:

One specific thing you observed or heard

One question it sparked for you

A genuine, specific thank you

Walk the room. Hand back generic cards.

#### 8:20–9:00 AM — Discovery Tracker Work Day

Students review and strengthen their Discovery Tracker entries. Goal: at least 3 complete entries with full 5 Whys by end of class.

If finished: "Pick your strongest observation. Write a one-paragraph problem statement: who has this problem, how often, what do they currently do about it, why is that insufficient?"

Use this time for informal 1-on-1s.

### Friday

### Friday — Profit or Broke + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

Facilitator Goal: End the week with energy. Connect financial thinking to real observations from the Discovery Tracker.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Tell the person next to you the most interesting problem you found this week and why it matters." 90 seconds. 2-3 share with the group.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Profit or Broke (Week 3 of Friday Rotation)

What it is: Facilitator reads a business scenario with numbers. Students race to figure out if the business is profitable or not.

Purpose: Build financial intuition fast. Students start thinking about the numbers behind the problems they are observing.

Week 3 scenarios — tie to problems students have been observing this week where possible:

Scenario 1: "You start a tutoring business. You charge $25/hour. You tutored 12 hours this week. You spent $15 on gas and $30 on printed materials. Profit or Broke?"

Answer: Revenue $300 - Costs $45 = Profit $255. PROFIT.

Follow up: "What would you need to charge to make $500 a week tutoring?"

Scenario 2: "You sell homemade hot sauce. Each bottle costs $3 to make. You sell them for $8. You sold 20 bottles at a farmers market. The booth cost $60. Profit or Broke?"

Answer: Revenue $160 - Costs ($60 + $60 materials) = $40. PROFIT — barely.

Follow up: "What is the minimum number of bottles you need to sell just to break even?"

Scenario 3: "You offer a grass cutting service. You charge $35 per yard. You cut 5 yards this week. Gas $20. New blade $40. Profit or Broke?"

Answer: Revenue $175 - Costs $60 = $115. PROFIT.

Follow up: "Should the blade cost count this week or spread over time? Why does that matter?"

After each scenario: "What would you change to increase the profit?"

#### 8:05–8:40 AM — 1-on-1 Sessions

Students continue Discovery Tracker or journal independently.

1-on-1 focus questions this week:

"What is the strongest problem you found this week?"

"Who specifically has this problem?"

"Have you talked to anyone who has it?"

#### 8:40–8:55 AM — Week Reflection

Ask:

"What is the best problem you found this week and why?"

"Did the 5 Whys change how you see that problem?"

"What would you need to know to be confident this is a real problem worth solving?"

#### 8:55–9:00 AM — Preview Week 4

Week 4: Business Model Canvas + MVPs + May-B-P.

"Next week we take your best problem and start building the simplest possible version of a solution. Come Monday with your strongest Discovery Tracker entry."
