# Week 6: Practice Your Pitch: Micro Pitch #1 + Networking Skills

Unit 1: Foundations & Identity

**Guiding question:** How do you communicate what you do in a way that makes people lean in — not tune out?

_When it last ran: Week of September 29, 2025 · 7:30–9:00 AM · Mon–Fri. Dates are from a previous year; this year's calendar may differ._

## By the end of the week, students can
- Deliver a 60-second Micro Pitch of their business idea.
- Use the Problem-Solution-Ask framework to structure a pitch.
- Practice active networking — introducing themselves and asking good questions.
- Give and receive specific, useful pitch feedback.

## Lesson walkthrough
_Written as the facilitator's script for the classroom. Use it to know what was taught and how; don't read it back to students._

### Monday

### Monday — Lecture: How to Pitch + Networking Skills

Facilitator Goal: Students understand what makes a pitch work, learn the Problem-Solution-Ask framework, and know how to network like a professional — not like a student at a school event.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"You have 10 seconds. Tell me your business in one sentence. Go." Go around the room fast — every student, no exceptions.

After: "How many of you felt like that was enough? Not enough? How many of you said something that made the person next to you want to know more?"

Say: "That discomfort is what we are fixing this week."

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: How to Pitch + Networking

30 minutes.

Opening (2 min)

Say: "A pitch is not a presentation. A pitch is a conversation starter. The goal is not to explain everything — the goal is to make the other person want to know more."

"The best pitches are short, clear, and specific. Most people's first pitch is long, vague, and forgettable. We are going to fix that this week."

Talking Point 1 — The Problem-Solution-Ask Framework (8 min)

Write on the board: PROBLEM → SOLUTION → ASK

Problem: "Start with the pain, not the product. What problem exists? Who has it? How does it affect them?"

Solution: "What do you do about it? Keep it simple — one sentence."

Ask: "What do you want from this conversation? A customer? A connection? A piece of advice? Be specific."

Example — walk through this:

Problem: "A lot of seniors in South Bend struggle to get to doctor appointments because they can't drive and rideshares feel unsafe to them."

Solution: "I am building a local rideshare service specifically for seniors — vetted drivers, simple booking, familiar faces."

Ask: "I am looking for two or three seniors I could pilot this with for free in the next 30 days. Do you know anyone?"

Ask the class: "What made that pitch work?" Let them respond.

Expected: Specific problem, specific solution, specific ask. No jargon. Sounds like a real person.

Talking Point 2 — What Kills a Pitch (5 min)

Say: "Here are the most common pitch killers:"

Starting with "So basically what I do is..." — leads nowhere fast

Too long — if you need more than 60 seconds, you do not know your idea well enough yet

No ask — you just described your business and then... waited. For what?

Vague problem — "I help people be more productive" tells me nothing

Reading from notes — if you cannot say it without notes, you are not ready

Say: "We are going to practice until none of these show up in your pitch."

Talking Point 3 — Networking Like a Professional (8 min)

Say: "Networking is not standing in a corner waiting for someone to talk to you. It is a skill. Here is how professionals do it."

Step 1 — Introduce yourself with intention:

"Hi, I'm [name]. I'm working on [one sentence about your business]. What do you do?"

Do NOT say: "Hi, I'm [name], I'm a student." Full stop. That ends the conversation.

Step 2 — Ask good questions:

"How did you get started in what you do?"

"What is the hardest part of your business right now?"

"Who else should I be talking to?"

Bad networking questions:

"Can I have your card?"

"Do you have any advice for young entrepreneurs?" — too generic

Step 3 — Follow up within 24 hours:

"A short message: 'Great to meet you at [event]. I wanted to follow up on [something specific you talked about].'"

"Most people do not do this. That is why it works."

Closing (5 min)

Say: "Your Micro Pitch #1 happens Thursday. You will pitch your business to the class in 60 seconds. Then you get feedback."

"This is not a performance. It is a first attempt. The goal is to get it out — not to be perfect."

"Tomorrow you workshop your pitch with partners until it is tight."

#### 8:05–8:50 AM — First Draft: Write Your Pitch

Students write their pitch using the Problem-Solution-Ask framework. 10 minutes independent writing.

Then: Say it out loud to yourself. Time it. Is it under 60 seconds?

Pairs: Say your pitch to your partner. Partner gives one piece of feedback: "What was most clear? What was most confusing?"

Revise and repeat once more.

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

Preview Tuesday: "Tomorrow is pitch workshop day. Come ready to say your pitch multiple times to multiple people. Bring your written draft."

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Activities: Pitch Workshop + Micro Pitch Practice Rounds

Facilitator Goal: Students practice their pitch 3-4 times in different formats, get specific feedback, and refine until it is tight and sounds like them.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Stand up. You have 90 seconds. Find two different people. Give each of them your pitch. Go."

After: "How did it feel the second time vs. the first?" 3 students share.

#### 7:35–8:00 AM — Speed Pitch Rounds

Set up the room so students can rotate. Every 3 minutes, they give their pitch to a new partner.

After each round, partner gives feedback using two sentence starters:

"The clearest part was..."

"I was confused by..."

Run 4 rounds. Students revise between rounds.

Facilitator note: Walk the room. Listen for pitch killers — vague problems, no ask, reading from notes. Coach in the moment.

#### 8:00–8:20 AM — Pitch Refinement

Individual work. Students write the final version of their pitch — the one they will deliver Thursday.

Say: "Write it out word for word. Then practice saying it until you do not need to look at the paper."

Use AI: "Prompt Claude or ChatGPT: 'Here is my 60-second pitch. What is the weakest sentence? How would you make it stronger?'"

#### 8:20–8:50 AM — Networking Simulation

Say: "We are going to simulate a networking event. Stand up, move around the room, and introduce yourself to three people as if you are at a real event."

"Use the intro formula: 'Hi, I'm [name]. I'm working on [one sentence]. What do you do?'"

"Then ask one good follow-up question."

Run for 10 minutes. Debrief:

"What worked? What felt awkward?"

"What did you learn about someone else's business that surprised you?"

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Speaker Tour Prep

Distribute Speaker Tour Prep Worksheets. Students prepare for Wednesday.

### Wednesday

### Wednesday — Speaker/Tour

Run the speaker or tour per standard Wednesday format. Prep sheets out, active listening, notes captured.

Before the speaker: "Pay attention to how they tell their story. Notice their pitch — how do they describe what they do in the first 60 seconds?"

### Thursday

### Thursday — Micro Pitch #1: Official Round + Feedback

Facilitator Goal: Every student delivers their pitch in front of the class. Feedback is specific and constructive. Students leave more confident than when they arrived.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Say your pitch one more time to yourself — quietly, right now." Give them 60 seconds of silence to do it.

Then: "How are you feeling? Nervous is fine. Nervous means you care."

#### 7:35–8:45 AM — Micro Pitch #1

Each student comes to the front. 60 seconds. Timer runs.

After each pitch:

Class writes on a sticky note: one thing that was clear and one question the pitch raised for them.

Facilitator gives one piece of specific feedback — not "great job," but "your problem statement was strong, your ask was unclear."

Facilitator notes:

If a student goes over 60 seconds, stop them gently. "You are at 60. Land the plane."

If a student freezes, prompt: "Start with the problem. Who has it?"

Celebrate every pitch — the act of standing up and saying it matters more than whether it was perfect.

#### 8:45–9:00 AM — Debrief + Close

Students review their sticky note feedback.

Ask:

"What did you hear in other people's pitches that made you think: I need to add that to mine?"

"What is the one thing you are fixing in your pitch before next time?"

Say: "You just pitched your business to a room full of people. That is something most adults have never done. You did it in week 6."

### Friday

### Friday — Profit or Broke + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

Facilitator Goal: End the unit on a high. Connect financial thinking to the businesses students pitched this week.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"What is the one thing you are changing in your pitch based on Thursday's feedback?" Around the room fast.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Profit or Broke (Week 6 — Rotation: Profit or Broke)

This week: use scenarios based on businesses students actually pitched Thursday. Makes it personal and relevant.

Ask: "Can I use your business for a Profit or Broke scenario?" Get 2-3 students to volunteer their businesses.

Build a quick scenario for each: give realistic numbers for their business and ask the class — Profit or Broke?

Example: If a student pitched a custom jewelry business — "You make bracelets for $4 each. You sell them for $18. You sold 15 last month. You bought $30 in new supplies. You paid $20 for a table at a market. Profit or Broke?"

Answer: Revenue $270 - Costs ($60 materials + $30 supplies + $20 table) = $160. PROFIT.

Follow up: "What price would you need to charge to double your profit without selling more units?"

#### 8:05–8:40 AM — 1-on-1 Sessions

Students refine pitch or work on May-B-P independently.

1-on-1 focus this week:

"What was the feedback that stung a little? That is the most useful feedback."

"What is your next real-world action on your May-B-P?"

#### 8:40–8:55 AM — Week Reflection + Unit 1 Close

This is the end of Unit 1. Take a few extra minutes here.

Ask:

"What is the biggest thing you learned about yourself in these first 6 weeks?"

"What does your business idea look like now vs. what it looked like week 1?"

"What is the one thing you are most proud of from Unit 1?"

Let 5-6 students respond. This is a milestone moment — honor it.

#### 8:55–9:00 AM — Preview Unit 2

Unit 2: Entrepreneurial Concepts, Tactics, and Tools.

"Unit 1 was about finding the problem and getting started. Unit 2 is about going deeper — creativity, design thinking, customer discovery, and building out your idea with real tools."

"See you Monday."
