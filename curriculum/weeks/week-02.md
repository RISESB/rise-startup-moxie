# Week 2: Finding Your Why: Values, Strengths, and Life Design

Unit 1: Foundations & Identity

**Guiding question:** What drives you — and how does that connect to the kind of entrepreneur you want to become?

_When it last ran: Week of September 1, 2025 · 7:30–9:00 AM · 4-Day Week (Labor Day Monday) · 4-Day Week. Dates are from a previous year; this year's calendar may differ._

## By the end of the week, students can
- Identify their core values and articulate why they matter.
- Understand their strengths using Ikigai, Wingfinder, and 16 Personalities.
- Connect personal purpose to entrepreneurial direction.
- Create an About Me poster that represents their entrepreneurial identity.

## Lesson walkthrough
_Written as the facilitator's script for the classroom. Use it to know what was taught and how; don't read it back to students._

### Monday

### Monday — NO CLASS

Labor Day. No class today. Lecture moves to Tuesday this week.

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Lecture: Finding Your Why + Ikigai + Wingfinder + 16 Personalities

Facilitator Goal: Students leave with a clearer picture of who they are — their values, their strengths, and the intersection that points toward their entrepreneurial direction. This is the foundation for the About Me poster they build Wednesday and Thursday.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Think about someone you admire — a family member, a person in your community, someone you've seen work. What is it about them that you respect? Tell the person next to you."

90 seconds. Call on 3-4 students. Listen for values showing up in their answers — point it out: "Notice you said you respect that she keeps going even when things are hard. That's a value — resilience."

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: Finding Your Why

30 minutes. This is the Monday lecture shifted to Tuesday due to Labor Day.

Opening (2 min)

Say: "Last week we talked about entrepreneurship as a method. Today we go one layer deeper — because the best entrepreneurs don't just build businesses. They build businesses that are connected to who they actually are."

"There's a reason some people build something and love every minute of it — and others build something and burn out in a year. The difference is usually whether they knew their WHY."

Talking Point 1 — What Is a WHY? (4 min)

Say: "Your WHY is the reason you do what you do — beyond money, beyond status. It is what gets you out of bed on the hard days."

"Simon Sinek, a leadership researcher, says most people and businesses know WHAT they do and HOW they do it — but the ones who inspire others start with WHY."

Write on the board:

WHAT — the product or service you offer

HOW — the way you do it

WHY — the reason it matters to you

Example:

WHAT: I sell handmade candles

HOW: I use natural ingredients and custom scents

WHY: I believe people deserve a peaceful space at home and I want to help create that

"Most businesses lead with WHAT. Great businesses lead with WHY. Your job this week is to start figuring out yours."

Talking Point 2 — Ikigai (8 min)

Distribute the Ikigai diagram.

Say: "Ikigai is a Japanese concept — it roughly translates to 'reason for being.' It sits at the intersection of four questions."

Write the four circles on the board:

What do you LOVE?

What are you GOOD AT?

What does the WORLD NEED?

What can you be PAID FOR?

"Your Ikigai — your sweet spot — is where all four overlap."

"Most people live in one or two of these circles. They love something but can't get paid for it. Or they're good at something but don't love it. Entrepreneurship is one path to living in all four."

Ask: "Can anyone think of a business that clearly lives in all four circles?" Let 2-3 students respond. Push for specifics — "Why does that business fit all four?"

Talking Point 3 — Wingfinder (5 min intro, 10 min to take the assessment)

Say: "Wingfinder is a strengths assessment built by Red Bull. It tells you where you are naturally strong — not what you're good at because you practiced, but what comes easily to you."

"There are four strength areas: Thinking, Creativity, Drive, and Influence. You'll see where you score highest."

"Open your laptop. Go to wingfinder.com. Take the assessment. It takes about 10-12 minutes. Do not overthink the answers — go with your gut."

Facilitator notes:

Walk the room while they take it. Keep them moving — don't let anyone stall.

When done: "Screenshot or write down your top strength area. You'll need it for your poster."

Talking Point 4 — 16 Personalities (3 min intro, 10 min to take)

Say: "16 Personalities gives you a picture of how you think, work, and interact with others. It is based on the Myers-Briggs framework."

"This is not a box you live in forever. It is a mirror — it shows you patterns in how you operate so you can work with them instead of against them."

"Go to 16personalities.com. Take the assessment. Again — gut answers only."

When done: "Write down your personality type and one thing that felt true about the description."

Debrief (3 min): Call on 4-5 students — "What type did you get? Did anything surprise you?"

Facilitator add: "Notice how different everyone is. A great entrepreneurial team has different types working together — that is not a problem, that is the point."

#### 8:05–8:30 AM — Ikigai Personal Mapping

Students fill out their Ikigai diagram individually using what they now know from Wingfinder and 16 Personalities plus their own self-knowledge.

Say: "Use your Wingfinder results to help fill in 'What am I good at?' Use your 16 Personalities results to think about 'What do I love?' Fill in all four circles. Be specific — 'helping people' is not specific enough. 'Helping first-generation students navigate college applications' is specific."

Walk the room. Push for specifics. If students are stuck: "What do you do on a Saturday when no one is telling you what to do? Start there."

#### 8:30–8:50 AM — Pair Share + Whole Group

Pairs: "Share your Ikigai with your partner. Where is your sweet spot? Does it connect to a business idea?"

Whole group: Call on 3-4 students. Ask: "Where does your Ikigai point you? Even if it's fuzzy — what direction does it suggest?"

Close: "Tomorrow you start building your About Me poster. This is the work that feeds it. Keep your Ikigai — you will need it."

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Speaker Tour Prep

Distribute Speaker Tour Prep Worksheets. Students fill out what they can in the remaining time. Complete for homework if needed.

### Wednesday

### Wednesday — Speaker/Tour + About Me Poster Work

Facilitator Goal: Students attend the speaker or tour, then use the remainder of class to begin building their About Me poster.

#### 7:30–8:30 AM — Speaker or Tour

Run the speaker or tour per the standard Wednesday format. Prep sheets out, active listening, notes in the Notes section.

#### 8:30–9:00 AM — About Me Poster: Start

Distribute poster boards and materials.

Say: "Your About Me poster is not a school project. It is the first version of your entrepreneurial brand. It answers the question: who are you and what do you care about?"

Required elements on the poster:

Your name and a word or phrase that describes you as an entrepreneur

Your Ikigai sweet spot — written out in one sentence

Your top Wingfinder strength

Your 16 Personalities type + one sentence on what it means

3 problems you want to solve

One image, drawing, or symbol that represents your WHY

Say: "This does not have to be perfect. It has to be honest. You will present this Thursday."

Walk the room. Ask students what they are putting for their Ikigai sentence. Push for specificity.

### Thursday

### Thursday — About Me Poster: Finish + Present

Facilitator Goal: Students complete their posters and present them to the class. This is their first formal share of the year — build confidence and community.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"In one word — what is your WHY?" Go around the room fast. No explanation, just the word. Write them all on the board.

After: "Look at this board. Every one of these is a reason someone in this room will build something real."

#### 7:35–8:00 AM — Finish Posters

25 minutes to finalize. Walk the room. Check that every poster has all required elements. If something is missing, point it out and give them time to add it.

Facilitator note: The visual quality does not matter. The specificity of the content does. A poster that says "I want to help single moms in South Bend find affordable childcare" is better than a beautiful poster that says "I want to help people."

#### 8:00–8:50 AM — Poster Presentations

Each student gets 60-90 seconds to present their poster to the class.

What to cover in their presentation:

Their name and entrepreneur word/phrase

Their Ikigai sweet spot in one sentence

One problem they most want to solve

After each presentation, class gives one word of feedback — write it on a sticky note and hand it to the presenter. Word should be something they noticed or appreciated.

Facilitator note: Keep the pace moving. 90 seconds is enough. If a student freezes, ask: "Just tell us your Ikigai sentence." That gets them started.

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

Say: "You just did something most adults have never done — you put your WHY on paper and said it out loud in front of people. That takes guts."

"Keep this poster. We are going to come back to it throughout the year to see how your thinking evolves."

Preview: "Next week we go outside. We are going to start observing the world like entrepreneurs — looking for problems, talking to people, showing up where it matters."

### Friday

### Friday — Deal or No Deal + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

Facilitator Goal: End the week with energy. Students connect their WHY to real business decisions through Deal or No Deal.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Tell the person next to you — based on your Ikigai, what kind of business do you think you'd actually enjoy building? 90 seconds. Go."

Call on 2-3 students to share what their partner said.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Deal or No Deal (Week 2 of Friday Rotation)

What it is: Facilitator plays investor or deal-maker. One student (or small group) plays the entrepreneur. Class observes and votes.

Purpose: Build negotiation, risk assessment, and decision-making. Students learn to evaluate whether a deal serves their business — not just whether it sounds good.

How to run it:

Pick a student (or ask for a volunteer) to be the entrepreneur.

Give them a quick business context: "You make and sell custom phone cases. You've sold 50 so far at $30 each."

Present the deal. Give them 60 seconds to decide. Then class votes and explains why.

Week 2 deals:

Deal 1: "I will invest $2,000 in your business. In return I want 40% of everything you make forever. Deal or no deal?"

Deal 2: "A local boutique wants to carry your phone cases. They take 60% of every sale. You keep 40%. Deal or no deal?"

Deal 3: "A school wants to order 100 custom cases for their staff at $15 each — half your normal price — but they want them in 2 weeks. Deal or no deal?"

After each deal ask:

"What information would you need before deciding?"

"What are the risks of taking it? Risks of not taking it?"

"Does this deal connect to your WHY — or does it just sound like money?"

That last question ties back to the week's content. Use it.

#### 8:05–8:40 AM — 1-on-1 Sessions

Students work independently: refine their Ikigai, add to their Entrepreneurial Identity sheet, or journal about their business direction.

Pull students for 3-4 minute check-ins. Focus questions this week:

"Where did your Ikigai point you? Does it connect to a business idea?"

"What surprised you about your Wingfinder or 16 Personalities results?"

"Is your WHY getting clearer or still fuzzy?"

#### 8:40–8:55 AM — Week Reflection

Ask:

"What did you learn about yourself this week that you didn't know before?"

"How does your WHY connect to the kind of business you want to build?"

"What is one thing you want to carry forward from this week?"

4-5 students per question. Keep energy up.

#### 8:55–9:00 AM — Preview Week 3

Week 3: Observing Your World — Walk, Talk, Show Up.

"Next week we leave the classroom. We go into the community with a mission: find real problems worth solving. Start paying attention between now and Monday — what do you notice? What annoys you? What is broken?"
