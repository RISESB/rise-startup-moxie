# Week 28: Financial Statements + Budgeting

Unit 4: Finance & Operations

**Guiding question:** How do you read the health of a business at a glance — and how do you plan your money before you spend it?

_When it last ran: Week of March 16, 2026 · 7:30–9:00 AM · Mon–Fri. Dates are from a previous year; this year's calendar may differ._

## By the end of the week, students can
- Name the 3 core financial statements and what each one shows.
- Read a simple income statement (P&L).
- Build a basic monthly budget for their business.
- Use a budget to make a real spending decision.

## Lesson walkthrough
_Written as the facilitator's script for the classroom. Use it to know what was taught and how; don't read it back to students._

### Monday

### Monday — Lecture: The 3 Financial Statements + Budgeting

Facilitator Goal: Students can name the three statements, read a simple income statement, and understand what a budget is for.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"If a friend said 'my business is doing great,' what is the one number you'd ask to see to check?" Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: Statements + Budgeting

Opening (2 min)

Say: "Every serious business, from a lemonade stand to Apple, uses the same three reports to understand itself. Today you learn to read them — and it is way simpler than it sounds."

The 3 Statements (12 min)

\1. Income Statement (P&L): "Did I make money over a period of time?" Revenue − costs = profit. This is the one you'll use most.

\2. Balance Sheet: "What do I own and what do I owe, right now?" Assets = Liabilities + Equity. A snapshot in time.

\3. Cash Flow Statement: "Where did my cash actually move?" (You already built a version of this last week.)

"Income statement = did I make money. Balance sheet = what am I worth. Cash flow = do I have money right now. Three different questions, three statements."

Reading a Simple P&L (8 min)

Walk through a simple P&L on the board:

Revenue: $1,000

Cost of goods sold (variable): −$350

Gross profit: $650

Fixed/operating costs: −$400

Net profit: $250

"Gross profit is after the cost of making the product. Net profit is after everything. When someone says 'the bottom line,' this is literally the bottom line."

Budgeting (6 min)

Say: "A budget is a plan for your money before you spend it. Businesses without a budget spend reactively and get surprised. A budget lets you decide on purpose."

A simple monthly budget: expected revenue, planned spending by category, and what is left. "If it is not in the budget, you pause before spending."

#### 8:05–8:50 AM — Read + React

Students practice reading 2-3 sample P&Ls the facilitator provides: "Is this business healthy? What is the biggest cost? What would you fix first?"

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

Preview: "Tomorrow you build your own P&L and a real monthly budget."

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Activities: Build a P&L + Monthly Budget

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Gross profit or net profit — which is bigger, and why?" Quick check for understanding. Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:10 AM — Build Your P&L

Students build a simple monthly income statement for their business using real or projected numbers: revenue, cost of goods sold, gross profit, fixed costs, net profit.

If they have real sales data, use it. If not, use their realistic projection from Week 26.

#### 8:10–8:45 AM — Monthly Budget

Students build a monthly budget: expected revenue at top, then planned spending by category (materials, marketing, fees, savings/reinvestment), then what remains.

"Give every dollar a job before the month starts. What are you reinvesting to grow?"

#### 8:45–9:00 AM — Speaker Tour Prep

Distribute Speaker Tour Prep Worksheets.

### Wednesday

### Wednesday — Speaker/Tour

Run the speaker or tour per standard Wednesday format. Prompt: "Ask the speaker how they decide what to reinvest in.");

(Facilitator note: if the prompt above shows a stray character, ignore — just ask the speaker how they decide what to reinvest in.)

### Thursday

### Thursday — Speaker Recap + Thank You Cards + Budget Work Day

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"One sentence — most interesting thing from Wednesday." Around the room.

#### 7:35–7:55 AM — Speaker Recap

Two columns: "What we heard" | "What would I put in my budget because of it?"

#### 7:55–8:20 AM — Thank You Cards

Handwritten. Specific. Required elements: one specific observation, one question it sparked, a genuine thank you.

#### 8:20–9:00 AM — Budget Work Day

Students finalize P&L and budget in their portfolio, then make one real budget-based decision: "Based on my budget, here is what I can afford to spend on growth this month, and here is what I will spend it on."

1-on-1 focus: "Walk me through your P&L. What is your net profit, and what does your budget say you can reinvest?"

### Friday

### Friday — Founder Fight + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"One thing your budget says you should stop spending on." Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Founder Fight (Week 28 — Rotation: Founder Fight)

Money-management edition:

Round 1: A founder who reinvests every dollar into growth vs. one who saves a cash cushion

Round 2: A business that tracks every number vs. one that "just knows" how it's doing

Round 3: A business that spends big on marketing vs. one that spends big on product quality

Debrief: "There is no single right answer — it depends on the business. What matters is deciding on purpose, with the numbers in front of you."

#### 8:05–8:40 AM — 1-on-1 Sessions

1-on-1 focus: "Is your budget realistic? What is one category you are over- or under-investing in?"

#### 8:40–8:55 AM — Week Reflection

What does your income statement tell you about your business?

What is one spending decision your budget helped you make?

Which of the 3 statements matters most to you right now, and why?

#### 8:55–9:00 AM — Preview Week 29

Week 29: Funding Options + Risk Management. "Next week: where money to grow comes from, and how to protect your business from what could go wrong."
