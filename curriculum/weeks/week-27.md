# Week 27: Cash Flow + Break-Even Analysis

Unit 4: Finance & Operations

**Guiding question:** When does the money actually arrive — and how many sales until you stop losing money?

## By the end of the week, students can
- Explain why profit on paper is not the same as cash in hand.
- Build a simple monthly cash flow view.
- Calculate their break-even point in units and in dollars.
- Identify how timing of money in vs. money out can sink a business.

## Lesson walkthrough
_Written as the facilitator's script for the classroom. Use it to know what was taught and how; don't read it back to students._

### Monday

### Monday — Lecture: Cash Flow + Break-Even

Facilitator Goal: Students understand that timing of cash matters as much as profit, and can find their break-even point.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Have you ever had money coming but not yet in your hands — waiting on a paycheck, an allowance, someone who owes you? What did that feel like?" 3-4 students share.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: Cash Flow + Break-Even

Opening (2 min)

Say: "More businesses die from running out of cash than from being unprofitable. You can be profitable on paper and still go broke if the money comes in too late to pay what you owe now."

Cash Flow vs. Profit (8 min)

Say: "Profit is the score at the end of the game. Cash flow is whether you have money in your pocket right now."

Example: "You land a $1,000 order — great profit. But you have to spend $400 on materials this week, and the customer pays you in 30 days. For those 30 days you are $400 in the hole. If you can't cover it, the deal that should make you money instead sinks you."

Money In vs. Money Out (6 min)

Cash flow tracks two things over time: when money actually comes IN and when it actually goes OUT. The gap between them is where businesses get in trouble.

Positive cash flow — more coming in than going out this period

Negative cash flow — more going out than coming in (okay short-term, deadly long-term)

Runway — how long you can survive at your current burn rate

Break-Even Analysis (10 min)

Say: "Break-even is the magic number — how many sales you need just to cover your costs. Below it you lose money. Above it you make money."

The formula: Break-even units = Fixed costs ÷ Contribution margin per unit.

Example: "Fixed costs are $260/month (booth fee + supplies subscription). Each candle contributes $13. $260 ÷ $13 = 20 candles. You must sell 20 candles a month just to break even. Candle 21 is your first real profit."

"Knowing this number changes everything. Now your monthly goal is not a vibe — it is 20, then everything above it."

#### 8:05–8:50 AM — Break-Even Calc

Students calculate their own break-even in units and dollars using their contribution margin from Week 26 and their fixed costs.

Walk the room: "What is your break-even number? How does it compare to what you are actually selling now?"

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

Preview: "Tomorrow you build a cash flow view and see the timing of your money."

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Activities: Cash Flow Build + Break-Even Calc

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"What is your break-even number, and are you above or below it right now?" Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:10 AM — Cash Flow Build

Students build a simple 3-month cash flow view: starting cash, money in (by when it actually arrives), money out (by when it is actually due), and ending cash each month.

Highlight any month that goes negative. "What would you do in that month to stay alive — delay a cost, collect faster, sell more up front?"

#### 8:10–8:45 AM — Break-Even Deep Dive

Students answer: "How many days/weeks until I hit break-even at my current sales pace? What is the fastest realistic way to get there sooner?"

AI prompt: "My fixed costs are $[X]/month and my contribution margin is $[Y] per sale. Help me find my break-even and 3 ways to lower it."

#### 8:45–9:00 AM — Speaker Tour Prep

Distribute Speaker Tour Prep Worksheets.

### Wednesday

### Wednesday — Speaker/Tour

Run the speaker or tour per standard Wednesday format. Prompt: "Ask the speaker about a time cash was tight and how they handled it."

### Thursday

### Thursday — Speaker Recap + Thank You Cards + Numbers Work Day

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"One sentence — most interesting thing from Wednesday." Around the room.

#### 7:35–7:55 AM — Speaker Recap

Two columns: "What we heard" | "What did this teach me about managing cash?"

#### 7:55–8:20 AM — Thank You Cards

### Friday

### Friday — Profit or Broke + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"How many sales to break even? One number." Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Profit or Broke (Week 27 — Rotation: Profit or Broke)

Cash-timing edition:

Scenario 1: "You have $50 cash. You get a $300 order but must spend $120 on materials now; the customer pays in 3 weeks. Profit or Broke — right now?"

Answer: Profitable order, but you only have $50 and need $120 now. Cash-broke. Follow up: "How do you make this deal survivable?"

Scenario 2: "Fixed costs $200/month. You sell 18 units at $13 margin. Profit or Broke?"

Answer: 18 × $13 = $234 vs. $200 = +$34. Just above break-even. Follow up: "What is your break-even in units?" ($200÷$13 ≈ 16)

Scenario 3: "You require payment up front and pay suppliers in 30 days. Money comes in before it goes out. Profit or Broke — and why is this powerful?"

#### 8:05–8:40 AM — 1-on-1 Sessions

1-on-1 focus: "What is your break-even, and what is your realistic timeline to pass it?"

#### 8:40–8:55 AM — Week Reflection

What is the difference between profit and cash flow in your own words?

What is your break-even point?

What is one way you could get cash in faster or hit break-even sooner?

#### 8:55–9:00 AM — Preview Week 28
