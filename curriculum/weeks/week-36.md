# Week 36: The End Part 3: Student Showcase

Unit 5: The Showcase (Capstone)

**Guiding question:** Are you ready to show the world what you built — and to close this year with pride?

## By the end of the week, students can
- Deliver a confident, complete showcase of their business.
- Represent themselves professionally to a real audience.
- Reflect on the full year and name their growth.
- Close the program with celebration and next steps.

## The week at a glance
- **Monday:** Final Setup + Last Rehearsal
- **Tuesday:** SHOWCASE DAY (adjust to your actual event date)
- **Wednesday:** Debrief + Follow-Up
- **Thursday:** Year-End Reflection + Celebration
- **Friday:** Closing Circle + What's Next

## Lesson walkthrough
_Written as the facilitator's script for the classroom. Use it to know what was taught and how; don't read it back to students._

### Monday

### Monday — Final Setup + Last Rehearsal

Facilitator Goal: Everything is ready and every student has run through their presentation one final time.

#### 7:30–7:40 AM — Warm-Up

"One word for how you feel about tomorrow." Around the room. Normalize the nerves — they mean it matters.

#### 7:40–8:20 AM — Final Setup

Students finalize and print all materials, load decks onto the right device, assemble booth pieces, and confirm their product/demo is ready.

Facilitator runs a logistics check: room layout, tables, screens/projector, signage, guest list, schedule, and a run-of-show for the event.

#### 8:20–8:55 AM — Last Rehearsal

Every student runs their presentation one last time, timed. Quick, encouraging notes only — no big changes now.

#### 8:55–9:00 AM — Close

Say: "You are ready. Get sleep, dress the part, and show up proud tomorrow."

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — STUDENT SHOWCASE

The culminating event of the year. Students present their businesses to family, community members, and guests.

Setup (before doors open)

Students arrive early, set up booths, test tech, and take their places. Facilitator greets guests and sets the tone: this is a celebration of real student businesses.

Showcase Format

Choose the format that fits your space and audience:

Booth-style — guests circulate, students pitch and demo one-on-one (great for products and conversation)

Stage-style — each student delivers their 2-minute presentation to the full room (great for a formal program)

Hybrid — short stage presentations, then open booth time for deeper conversation

Facilitator roles during the event: welcome and frame the showcase, keep time and flow, introduce students if stage-style, and make sure every student gets seen and celebrated.

Encourage students to capture contacts and interest — the CRM habit does not stop at the showcase.

Close the Event

Bring everyone together at the end. Thank guests, recognize the students as a group, and celebrate the year. A short word from a student or two lands beautifully here.

### Wednesday

### Wednesday — Debrief + Follow-Up

#### 7:30–7:45 AM — Warm-Up: How Did It Go?

"One high and one thing you learned from the showcase." Around the room. Let it breathe — this is a big moment landing.

#### 7:45–8:20 AM — Showcase Debrief

Discuss as a group: What surprised you? What questions did guests ask most? What did people respond to? What would you do differently?

#### 8:20–9:00 AM — Follow-Up

Students follow up with every contact they made: thank-you messages, promised info, and next steps. "The showcase is a beginning, not an ending. Who do you owe a message?"

Write thank you notes to guests, judges, and community supporters.

### Thursday

### Thursday — Year-End Reflection + Celebration

#### 7:30–7:40 AM — Warm-Up

"The Week 1 version of you vs. the you standing here today — one sentence." Around the room.

#### 7:40–8:20 AM — Full-Year Reflection

Students write a final reflection (a keeper for their portfolio):

The biggest thing I built this year

The biggest thing I learned about business

The biggest thing I learned about myself

What I want to do with this next

#### 8:20–9:00 AM — Celebration

Celebrate the class. Superlatives, shoutouts, certificates, food — whatever fits your group. Every student is named for something real they did or became this year.

### Friday

### Friday — Closing Circle + What's Next

#### 7:30–8:00 AM — Closing Circle

Sit in a circle. Each student shares: one thing they are proud of, and one thing they will carry forward. Facilitator goes last.

#### 8:00–8:40 AM — What's Next

Talk through paths forward: keep running the business over summer, next-level programs, competitions (McCloskey and beyond), and how to stay connected to the community they built.

Students write one commitment: "This summer, I will ___ with my business or my entrepreneurial skills."

#### 8:40–9:00 AM — Final Word

Facilitator close: "You started this year thinking entrepreneurship was for other people. You end it as founders who found problems, built solutions, talked to customers, made money, and pitched to a room. That does not go away. Whatever you do next, you now know how to see a problem and start moving. That is the whole thing. Go do it.");
