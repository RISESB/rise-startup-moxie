# Week 19: Customer Segmentation + Messaging

Unit 3: Marketing & Brand

**Guiding question:** Not everyone is your customer — so who exactly are you talking to, and what do you say to them?

## By the end of the week, students can
- Break a broad market into distinct customer segments.
- Choose a primary segment to focus marketing on first.
- Write targeted messaging that speaks to one segment's specific needs.
- Understand why "talking to everyone" means reaching no one.

## Lesson walkthrough
_Written as the facilitator's script for the classroom. Use it to know what was taught and how; don't read it back to students._

### Monday

### Monday — Lecture: Segmentation + Message-Market Fit

Facilitator Goal: Students stop trying to sell to "everyone" and learn to focus on a specific segment with a specific message.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Who is your customer? Answer in one sentence." Around the room. Listen for "everyone" or "anyone who..." — those are the ones to fix this week.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: Segmentation

Opening (2 min)

Say: "The fastest way to fail at marketing is to try to reach everyone. When you talk to everyone, you sound like nothing. When you talk to a specific person about their specific problem, they lean in."

What Is Segmentation? (8 min)

Say: "Segmentation means breaking a big market into smaller groups that share something in common — so you can serve one group really well instead of all groups poorly."

Common ways to segment:

Demographic — age, income, location, job, family stage

Behavioral — how they buy, how often, what triggers them

Needs-based — what problem they are trying to solve

Psychographic — values, lifestyle, identity

Example: A meal-kit business could segment into (a) busy working parents, (b) college students, (c) seniors who can't cook easily. Same product, three very different messages.

Choosing Your Primary Segment (8 min)

Say: "You pick ONE segment to start. The best first segment is where the pain is highest, they are easiest to reach, and they can pay."

Highest pain — they feel the problem most

Easiest to reach — you know where they are

Ability to pay — they have money for a solution

"Starting narrow is not thinking small. Amazon started with only books. Facebook started with only Harvard students. Narrow first, then expand."

Message-Market Fit (8 min)

Say: "Once you know your segment, your message has to match. Good messaging names the problem, shows you understand it, and offers relief — in the customer's own words."

Message formula: Call out the segment → name their specific pain → show the outcome → one clear next step.

Example: "Busy parents in South Bend: dinner shouldn't cost you your whole evening. Fresh local meal kits, on your porch, ready in 20 minutes. Order your first week free."

#### 8:05–8:50 AM — Segment First Draft

Students list every possible segment for their business, then score each on pain / reach / ability to pay. Pick a primary segment.

Walk the room: "Why that segment first? Could you reach 10 of them this month?"

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

Preview: "Tomorrow you write messaging built for that one segment."

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Activities: Segment Mapping + Message Workshop

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Name your primary segment in 5 words or fewer." Around the room. The shorter and sharper, the better.

#### 7:35–8:00 AM — Segment Profile

Students build a one-page profile of their primary segment: where they spend time, what they already buy, what words they use to describe the problem, who they trust.

AI prompt: "My primary customer segment is [describe]. What words and phrases do they actually use when talking about [the problem]? What messaging would resonate?"

#### 8:00–8:45 AM — Message Workshop

Students write 3 versions of a core marketing message using the formula: call out → pain → outcome → next step.

Pairs: partner plays the segment. "Does this sound like it is talking to me? What word feels off?"

Revise to the strongest version.

#### 8:45–9:00 AM — Speaker Tour Prep

Distribute Speaker Tour Prep Worksheets.

### Wednesday

### Wednesday — Speaker/Tour

Run the speaker or tour per standard Wednesday format.

### Thursday

### Thursday — Speaker Recap + Thank You Cards + Messaging Work Day

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"One sentence — most interesting thing from Wednesday." Around the room.

#### 7:35–7:55 AM — Speaker Recap

Two columns: "What we heard" | "Who was this person's primary customer segment?"

#### 7:55–8:20 AM — Thank You Cards

Handwritten. Specific. Required elements: one specific observation, one question it sparked, a genuine thank you.

#### 8:20–9:00 AM — Messaging Work Day

Students finalize their core message and write 2 short variations for different channels (e.g. one for social, one for in person). 1-on-1 focus: "Say your message. Who is it for and what do you want them to do?"

### Friday

### Friday — Founder Fight + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Say your core message in one breath." Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Founder Fight (Week 19 — Rotation: Founder Fight)

Two students, assigned businesses, 60 seconds each, class votes. Segmentation edition:

Round 1: A business targeting one narrow niche vs. one targeting a huge broad market

Round 2: A product with a sharp specific message vs. one with a broad friendly message

Round 3: A business that knows exactly where its customer is vs. one advertising everywhere

Debrief: "Which is easier to market and why? What does a sharp message do that a broad one can't?"

#### 8:05–8:40 AM — 1-on-1 Sessions

1-on-1 focus: "Is your segment narrow enough? Does your message use their words, not yours?"

#### 8:40–8:55 AM — Week Reflection

Who is your primary segment, and why did you choose them first?

What is your strongest message and who is it for?

What surprised you about narrowing your focus?

#### 8:55–9:00 AM — Preview Week 20

Week 20: USP + Storytelling + Brand Strategy. "Next week: what makes you different, and how do you tell a story people remember?"
