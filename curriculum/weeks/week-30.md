# Week 30: Legal Structures + Operations

Unit 4: Finance & Operations

**Guiding question:** How do you make your business official, keep it legal, and run it so it doesn't depend on you remembering everything?

_When it last ran: Week of March 30, 2026 · 7:30–9:00 AM · Mon–Fri. Dates are from a previous year; this year's calendar may differ._

## By the end of the week, students can
- Compare the main legal structures (sole prop, LLC, partnership, corporation) in plain terms.
- Identify the licenses, permits, or basics their type of business needs.
- Understand what operations means and why systems beat memory.
- Write one simple Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) for their business.

## Lesson walkthrough
_Written as the facilitator's script for the classroom. Use it to know what was taught and how; don't read it back to students._

### Monday

### Monday — Lecture: Legal Structures + Staying Legal + Operations

Facilitator Goal: Students understand the main business structures, what it takes to stay legal, and why operations systems matter.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Is your business 'official' yet? What would make it feel official to you?" 3-4 students share.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: Legal + Operations

Opening (2 min)

Say: "Two boring-sounding things separate a hobby from a real business: being set up legally, and running on systems instead of chaos. Get these right and everything else gets easier. This is not legal advice — it is a map so you know what to ask."

Legal Structures (12 min)

Write the main options on the board:

Sole Proprietorship — you ARE the business. Easiest to start, cheapest. Downside: no separation, so you are personally responsible for debts.

LLC (Limited Liability Company) — separates you from the business, protecting your personal stuff if things go wrong. Most common choice for small businesses.

Partnership — two or more owners share it. Simple, but you need a clear written agreement to avoid conflict.

Corporation (C-corp / S-corp) — most complex, built for raising big investment and scaling. Overkill for most starting out.

"The word that matters most: liability. 'Limited liability' means if the business owes money or gets sued, they generally can't take your personal savings. That protection is why the LLC is so popular."

"Most of you would start as a sole prop and move to an LLC once you have real revenue and real risk."

Staying Legal (6 min)

Depending on the business, you may need:

A business name registration

A license or permit (food, resale, home business — varies by city/state)

A way to handle sales tax if you sell products

Basic records of income and expenses (your P&L and receipts)

"You don't need all of this on day one. But you should know which apply to you so nothing surprises you later."

Operations (8 min)

Say: "Operations is just how the work actually gets done, repeatably. When everything lives in your head, the business breaks the moment you're busy, sick, or scaling."

Say: "A Standard Operating Procedure — an SOP — is a simple step-by-step for a task anyone could follow. 'How I fulfill an order.' 'How I onboard a customer.' Boring, and it is exactly what lets a business grow beyond you."

"If you got hit by a good problem tomorrow — 50 orders at once — could someone help you if you handed them your notes? SOPs make that a yes."

#### 8:05–8:50 AM — Structure + Legal Quick Audit

Students answer: "Which structure fits me now, and which might I move to later? What licenses or permits, if any, does my type of business need? Where would I look them up?"

Walk the room. Use AI: "I run a [type] business in [state]. What licenses or permits typically apply, and what structure do similar small businesses usually choose?" (Verify anything important.)

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

Preview: "Tomorrow you pick your structure and write your first real SOP."

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Activities: Structure Fit + Write an SOP

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Sole prop, LLC, partnership, or corporation — which fits you now? One word." Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:00 AM — Structure Decision

Students write a short structure decision: "Right now I would operate as a [structure] because [reason]. I would consider switching to [structure] when [trigger]." Plus a list of any permits/licenses to look into.

#### 8:00–8:45 AM — Write Your First SOP

Students pick their most important repeatable task and write it as a step-by-step SOP anyone could follow.

Examples: how to fulfill an order start to finish, how to respond to a customer inquiry, how to make one unit of product to standard.

Test it: pairs swap SOPs. "Could you actually do this task using only these steps? Where would you get stuck?"

#### 8:45–9:00 AM — Speaker Tour Prep

Distribute Speaker Tour Prep Worksheets.

### Wednesday

### Wednesday — Speaker/Tour

Run the speaker or tour per standard Wednesday format. Prompt: "Ask the speaker how they keep their business organized as it grows."

### Thursday

### Thursday — Speaker Recap + Thank You Cards + Operations Work Day

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"One sentence — most interesting thing from Wednesday." Around the room.

#### 7:35–7:55 AM — Speaker Recap

Two columns: "What we heard" | "What system or SOP could I steal for my business?"

#### 7:55–8:20 AM — Thank You Cards

Handwritten. Specific. Required elements: one specific observation, one question it sparked, a genuine thank you.

#### 8:20–9:00 AM — Operations Work Day

Students finalize structure decision and SOP in their portfolio, and write a short list of the top 3 systems their business still needs. 1-on-1 focus: "If you got 50 orders tomorrow, what would break first? What SOP would prevent it?"

### Friday

### Friday — Profit or Broke + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"What structure did you choose, and what is the one legal thing you need to look into?" Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Profit or Broke (Week 30 — Rotation: Profit or Broke)

Operations + legal edition:

Scenario 1: "You skip getting a $50 permit. You get shut down at a market and lose a $300 sales day. Profit or Broke — and was skipping the permit worth it?"

Scenario 2: "You spend 3 hours writing SOPs. Next month a friend helps you fulfill orders using them, and you handle 2x the volume. Profit or Broke — and what was the 3 hours worth?"

Scenario 3: "You stay a sole prop and a customer dispute costs you $800 out of your own pocket. Profit or Broke — would an LLC have helped?"

#### 8:05–8:40 AM — 1-on-1 Sessions

1-on-1 focus: "Is your business set up to grow beyond you? What is the one system to build next?"

#### 8:40–8:55 AM — Week Reflection

What legal structure fits your business, and why?

What is one legal or permit item you need to look into?

What is the first SOP you wrote, and what would you systematize next?

#### 8:55–9:00 AM — Preview Week 31
