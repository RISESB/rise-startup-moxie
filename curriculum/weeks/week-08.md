# Week 8: Creativity + Design Thinking in Action

Unit 1: Foundations & Identity

**Guiding question:** How do you solve problems you have never seen before — and how do you make sure you are solving the right problem?

## By the end of the week, students can
- Explain the 5-stage Design Thinking process.
- Apply empathy mapping to understand a customer's experience.
- Use ideation techniques to generate and evaluate multiple solutions.
- Prototype a simple solution concept for their business idea.

## The week at a glance
- **Monday:** Lecture — Design Thinking: Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test
- **Tuesday:** Activities — Empathy Mapping + Ideation Sprint
- **Wednesday:** Speaker/Tour
- **Thursday:** Speaker Recap + Thank You Cards + Prototype Workshop
- **Friday:** Deal or No Deal + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

## Materials
- Printed Empathy Map templates (one per student)
- Sticky notes (at least 20 per student)
- Markers
- Paper for rapid prototyping sketches
- Timer

## Key ideas (from the slides)
- **Warm-Up:** What in your daily life is badly designed?
- **Creativity Is a Skill:** Not a personality trait — a method
- **What Is Design Thinking?:** Design Thinking
- **The 5 Stages:** Empathize → Define → Ideate → Prototype → Test
- **1. Empathize:** Understand the person, not the data
- **2. Define:** Name the core problem — from the user's view
- **3. Ideate:** Generate — don't evaluate
- **4. Prototype:** Build the simplest version
- **5. Test:** Put it in front of real users
- **Where Are You Now?:** Look at your May-B-P
- **Activity: Empathy Map:** Map your most specific customer
- **Close:** Strongest May-B-Ps come from deepest empathy

## Lesson walkthrough
_Written as the facilitator's script for the classroom. Use it to know what was taught and how; don't read it back to students._

### Monday

### Monday — Lecture: Design Thinking

Facilitator Goal: Students understand design thinking as a repeatable method — not a creative personality trait — and can name and explain all 5 stages.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Think of something in your daily life that is poorly designed — something that frustrates you every time you use it. What is it?" 3-4 students share.

Say: "Hold onto those. They are perfect starting points for design thinking."

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: Design Thinking

Opening (2 min)

Say: "Creativity is not something you have or you don't. It is a skill — and like every skill, there is a method behind it."

"Design Thinking is that method. It was developed at Stanford's d.school and is used by companies like Apple, IDEO, and Google to build products people actually want to use."

"The core insight of design thinking: most failed products fail because the team solved the wrong problem. Design Thinking is how you make sure you are solving the right one."

The 5 Stages — write on the board (10 min)

Stage 1 — EMPATHIZE: "Before you solve anything, you have to deeply understand the person who has the problem. Not what you think they need. What they actually experience."

Tools: observations, conversations, empathy maps. You are not a researcher analyzing data. You are a human trying to feel what another human feels.

Stage 2 — DEFINE: "Take everything you learned in empathy and define the core problem — from the user's perspective, not yours."

Format: "[User] needs a way to [do/achieve/feel something] because [insight]."

Example: "A first-generation college student needs a way to navigate financial aid paperwork because the language assumes knowledge she has never been taught."

Stage 3 — IDEATE: "Now you generate solutions. The rule of ideation: quantity over quality. No idea is too wild in this phase. You are not evaluating — you are generating."

Tools: sticky note brainstorm, "How Might We" questions, worst-possible-idea (flip it and find the opposite).

Stage 4 — PROTOTYPE: "Pick your best idea and build the simplest version of it that you could put in front of a real user. Not a finished product — a rough model, a sketch, a storyboard, a cardboard version."

"The goal of a prototype is to make your idea tangible enough that someone else can react to it."

Stage 5 — TEST: "Put your prototype in front of real users. Watch what they do. Ask what confused them. Ask what they loved. Go back and improve."

"Design thinking is not linear — you might test and go all the way back to Empathize. That is not failure. That is how it works."

Connecting it to their work (5 min)

Say: "Look at your May-B-P. What stage of design thinking are you in right now?"

Let students respond. Most will be between Empathize and Define. Some will have jumped straight to Prototype. That is fine — acknowledge it and name what they skipped.

"This week we go back to Empathize and Define before we build anything. The strongest May-B-Ps come from the deepest empathy."

#### 8:05–8:50 AM — Empathy Map Introduction + First Draft

Distribute Empathy Map templates.

The four quadrants: THINK & FEEL | HEAR | SEE | SAY & DO. Center: the user. Bottom: PAINS and GAINS.

Say: "Pick the customer from your BMC — your most specific customer segment. Fill in this map for that person. What do they think and feel about the problem? What do they hear from others? What do they see around them? What do they say and do?"

"This is a hypothesis. You are guessing based on what you know right now. The real test is whether your guesses match reality when you talk to actual customers."

Walk the room. Push for specificity in every quadrant.

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

Preview Tuesday: "Tomorrow you ideate — generate as many solutions as possible — and then sketch your first prototype."

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Activities: Empathy Mapping + Ideation Sprint

Facilitator Goal: Students complete empathy maps, write strong problem statements, and generate 10+ solution ideas before selecting one to prototype.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Read your empathy map to the person next to you. Their job: ask one question the map doesn't answer yet." 90 seconds.

#### 7:35–7:55 AM — Problem Statement Workshop

Students write a problem statement using the format: "[User] needs a way to [do/achieve/feel something] because [insight]."

Write 3 versions. Pick the one that feels most true and most specific.

Share with the class: 4-5 students read theirs. Others give one word of feedback: "specific" or "vague."

#### 7:55–8:25 AM — Ideation Sprint

Each student gets a stack of sticky notes. Timer: 10 minutes.

"Write one solution idea per sticky note. No editing, no judgment. Your job is to get to 10 ideas minimum. If you run out of obvious ideas, try the worst possible idea and then flip it."

After 10 minutes: stick all notes on the wall or desk. Sort into categories. Pick your top 3.

Partners: "Tell me your top 3. Which one feels most like a May-B-P? Which one would teach you the most in 2 weeks?"

#### 8:25–8:50 AM — Prototype Sketch

Students pick one idea and sketch the simplest possible prototype — a drawing, a wireframe, a flowchart, a storyboard of how the user would experience it.

Say: "You have 20 minutes. Your sketch does not have to be beautiful. It has to be specific enough that someone else could look at it and understand what it does."

Walk the room. Ask: "Who is using this? What is the first thing they do?"

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Speaker Tour Prep

Distribute Speaker Tour Prep Worksheets. Prepare for Wednesday.

### Wednesday

### Wednesday — Speaker/Tour

Run the speaker or tour per standard Wednesday format.

### Thursday

### Thursday — Speaker Recap + Thank You Cards + Prototype Workshop

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"One sentence — most interesting thing from Wednesday." Around the room.

#### 7:35–7:55 AM — Speaker Recap

Two columns: "What we heard" | "How does this connect to design thinking?"

Ask: "Did the speaker describe a moment where they empathized, defined, or tested something? What was it?"

#### 7:55–8:20 AM — Thank You Cards

Handwritten. Specific. Required elements: one specific observation, one question it sparked, a genuine thank you.

#### 8:20–9:00 AM — Prototype Workshop

Students refine their prototype sketch and add a "test plan" — a short description of who they will show it to, what they will ask, and what they hope to learn.

Test plan format: "I will show this to [specific person]. I will ask them [these questions]. I will know it is working if [this happens]."

Use AI: "Prompt Claude or ChatGPT: 'Here is my prototype sketch [describe it]. What questions should I ask when I test it with a user?'"

1-on-1 focus: "Walk me through your prototype. Who is the user? What is the first thing they do?"

### Friday

### Friday — Deal or No Deal + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Tell your partner the problem statement for your business in the [User]-[need]-[because] format. They give you one word: specific or vague." 90 seconds.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Deal or No Deal (Week 8 — Rotation: Deal or No Deal)

Week 8 deals — tie to design thinking and prototype investment:

Deal 1: "A partner will build your prototype into a full app for free — but they want 50% of the business forever. Deal or no deal?"

Follow up: "What is your prototype actually worth at this stage?"

Deal 2: "An accelerator will give you $3,000 and 8 weeks of mentorship — but you have to pivot your idea to their preferred market. Deal or no deal?"

Follow up: "When is taking money actually a trap?"

Deal 3: "A major retailer wants to sell your product — but they need 500 units in 30 days and you have never made more than 10. Deal or no deal?"

Follow up: "What would you need to know before you could say yes?"

#### 8:05–8:40 AM — 1-on-1 Sessions

1-on-1 focus: "Who are you testing your prototype with? When? What do you expect to learn?"

#### 8:40–8:55 AM — Week Reflection

Which stage of design thinking is hardest for you — and why?

What did you learn about your customer from the empathy map that changed how you see your idea?

What is the one thing your prototype needs before you can test it?

#### 8:55–9:00 AM — Preview Week 9

Week 9: Effectuation — Starting with What You Have + Team Building. "Next week we flip the entrepreneur mindset: instead of starting with a goal and finding resources, we start with what we already have and figure out what is possible."
