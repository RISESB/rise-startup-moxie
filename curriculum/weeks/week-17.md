# Week 17: Reflection + 2-Minute Pitch

Unit 2: Customer & Product

**Guiding question:** This is the Unit 2 final. Students build an 8-slide pitch deck using Notre Dame McCloskey Business Plan Competition Round 2 standards and deliver a 2-minute pitch to the class. This is a real format used in real competitions — hold students to that standard.

## Lesson walkthrough
_Written as the facilitator's script for the classroom. Use it to know what was taught and how; don't read it back to students._

### Monday

WEEK 17 — MONDAY

Theme: Deck Build Day 1 — Slides 1-4

OPENING (5 min)

Return Unit 2 reflections from Week 16 Friday. Quick orientation: this week students build a real pitch deck and present Friday. Walk through the 8-slide structure and the 2-minute pitch format.

Frame it: "This is the Notre Dame McCloskey format. Real founders use this. You're going to do it for real."

DECK BUILD: SLIDES 1-4 (45 min)

Students work on slides 1-4 today. Facilitator circulates with coaching questions:

Slide 1 — Cover:

Is your business name clear?

Does your tagline say what you do in one sentence?

Slide 2 — Problem:

Who specifically has this problem? (not "everyone")

What evidence do you have that it's a real problem?

Slide 3 — Solution:

What do you actually sell?

Why is it better than what exists now?

Slide 4 — Market:

Who is your ideal customer? Describe them specifically.

How many of them are there? (use TAM/SAM/SOM or a realistic local estimate)

End of day: each student should have a rough version of slides 1-4 in a Google Slides or Canva deck.

### Tuesday

WEEK 17 — TUESDAY

Theme: Deck Build Day 2 — Slides 5-8

CHECK-IN: Slides 1-4 Status (5 min)

Quick show of hands: how many have rough versions of all 4 slides? Flag anyone who is behind — pair them with a student who is ahead for peer support during build time.

DECK BUILD: SLIDES 5-8 (45 min)

Coaching questions by slide:

Slide 5 — Business Model:

How exactly do you make money?

What's your price? Who pays it? How often?

Slide 6 — Traction:

What have you actually done? Sales, interviews, tests?

Numbers are more convincing than stories. "I sold 12 units" beats "people really like it."

Slide 7 — Financials:

Price × units = revenue. Show the math.

What are your main costs? Are you profitable or working toward it?

Slide 8 — The Ask:

What do you need most right now?

End with a one-sentence vision: "In one year, I want to…"

End of Tuesday: all 8 slides should have content — even rough. Wednesday is for refining, not building from scratch.

### Wednesday

WEEK 17 — WEDNESDAY

Theme: Full Deck Review + Refinement

PEER REVIEW ROUND 1 (25 min)

Students pair up and run through each other's full 8-slide deck. Reviewer gives feedback on:

Clarity: Can I understand your business in 2 minutes?

Evidence: Where are you making claims you can't back up?

Gaps: Which slide is weakest? What's missing?

Reviewer completes a written feedback form (or sticky notes): one strength, one gap, one question a judge would ask.

REVISION TIME (20 min)

Students revise based on peer feedback. Facilitator circulates and flags decks that still have major gaps:

Traction slide with no real numbers — must get at least one real data point before Thursday

Financials slide with no actual math — make them calculate it, even roughly

Problem slide too vague — push for a specific person with a specific pain

PITCH LOGISTICS BRIEFING (5 min)

Confirm Friday logistics: order of presentations, timing (2 min pitch + 2 min Q&A per student), screen/projector setup, class Q&A expectations.

Remind students: no reading from notes during the pitch. Know your deck. The slides are your visual — you are the presenter.

### Thursday

WEEK 17 — THURSDAY

Theme: Practice Pitches + Feedback

PRACTICE PITCH ROUND (40 min)

Every student delivers their full 2-minute pitch at least once. Options depending on class size:

Small class (under 12): pitch to the whole group, timed, with Q&A

Larger class: split into groups of 4-5, rotate — each person pitches once and gives feedback twice

Peer feedback format after each practice pitch:

One thing that landed well

One thing to tighten or clarify

One question you'd ask as an investor

Facilitator notes: watch for students who go over 2 minutes, rush through slides too fast, or freeze on Q&A. Coach these specifically.

FINAL PREP TIME (10 min)

Students make final edits and practice their opening line one more time. A strong first 15 seconds sets the tone for the whole pitch.

Homework: practice your pitch out loud two more times tonight. Time yourself.

### Friday

WEEK 17 — FRIDAY: FINAL PITCHES

Unit 2 Final — McCloskey Round 2 Format

SETUP (5 min)

Arrange the room as a pitch space — chairs in an arc or theater-style facing the screen. Remind students of the format: 2 minutes to pitch, 2 minutes of Q&A, then next presenter.

Set the tone: "Today you are founders pitching to investors. The class is the panel. Ask real questions and give real feedback."

FINAL PITCHES (40 min)

Run presentations in order. Facilitator roles:

Timekeeper: signal at 90 seconds, cut off firmly at 2:00

Moderator: prompt class Q&A if silence, protect presenter from pile-ons

Note-taker: jot one strength and one gap per presenter for post-session written feedback

Class Q&A prompts if students are quiet:

"Who is your first customer — describe them."

"What would make you give up on this business?"

"What's the one thing that has to be true for this to work?"

UNIT 2 CELEBRATION + CLOSE (10 min)

After all pitches: class shoutouts. Each student names one thing a classmate did that impressed them — about their business, their pitch, or their growth over Unit 2.

Facilitator close: "In Unit 2 you went from idea to something real. You matched a real customer, designed thinking, found your first assumptions, tested them, and just pitched it to an investor panel. Unit 3 is about getting more customers to find you. We start Monday."

No game this week — the final pitches were the main event.
