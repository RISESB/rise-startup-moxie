# Week 16: Validating Key Assumptions

Unit 2: Customer & Product

**Guiding question:** Every business runs on assumptions: "People will pay this price." "Customers will find me online." "My supplier can deliver on time." This week students identify the riskiest assumptions in their businesses and test them before they become expensive mistakes.

## By the end of the week, students can
- Identify the 3 most critical assumptions their business depends on
- Design a simple test for at least one assumption
- Run or plan a real-world validation experiment
- Interpret results and decide: pivot, persevere, or pause

## Lesson walkthrough
_Written as the facilitator's script for the classroom. Use it to know what was taught and how; don't read it back to students._

### Monday

WEEK 16 — MONDAY

Theme: What Is an Assumption? Assumption Mapping

WARM-UP: Convince Me (8 min)

Pick a student volunteer. They pitch their business in 60 seconds. The facilitator plays skeptic and challenges every claim: "How do you know people will pay that?" "What makes you think they'll find you?" Student must defend or admit they're not sure.

Debrief: every business pitch is full of assumptions. The best founders know which ones are tested and which ones aren't.

MINI-LESSON: ASSUMPTIONS VS. FACTS (10 min)

Define: An assumption is something you believe to be true but haven't proven yet. A fact is something you've tested and confirmed.

Three types of risky assumptions:

Desirability: Do customers actually want this?

Feasibility: Can I actually deliver this?

Viability: Will this make money?

Example: "People will pay $25 for my handmade candles" — is that desirability, viability, or both?

ASSUMPTION MAPPING ACTIVITY (25 min)

Each student lists 5-7 assumptions their business depends on. Then rank them on two axes:

How important is this assumption? (1=low, 5=critical)

How confident are you it's true? (1=just guessing, 5=tested and confirmed)

The highest importance + lowest confidence assumptions are the ones to test first.

Students circle their top 3 "must test" assumptions and share one with a partner.

### Tuesday

WEEK 16 — TUESDAY

Theme: Designing Tests — How Do You Validate an Assumption?

WARM-UP: Assumption or Fact? (5 min)

Read 5 statements aloud. Students hold up A (assumption) or F (fact):

"My friend said they'd buy it." — A

"I sold 12 units last month." — F

"People my age hate going to the DMV." — A

"I interviewed 8 customers and 6 said price was the #1 barrier." — F

"My product is better than the competition." — A

MINI-LESSON: EXPERIMENT DESIGN (15 min)

A good validation test has three parts:

Hypothesis: "I believe [X] is true."

Test: "I will [do this thing] to find out."

Success metric: "I'll know it's true if [specific result]."

Example: "I believe busy parents in our neighborhood will pay $8 for cookie delivery. I will post an Instagram story with a pre-order link. I'll know it's true if 5+ people click and at least 3 pay a deposit."

Common test types: pre-orders, landing pages, surveys (weak), in-person demos, door-to-door sales, social media polls (weak), direct asks with money on the table.

DESIGN YOUR TEST (25 min)

Students write a formal experiment design for their #1 assumption:

Assumption: [what I believe]

Why it matters: [what breaks if I'm wrong]

My test: [exactly what I will do]

Timeline: [when I will run it]

Success metric: [what result confirms or denies it]

Facilitator reviews each design before Wednesday. Flag any test that is too vague or can't produce a real answer in 1-2 days.

### Wednesday

WEEK 16 — WEDNESDAY

Theme: Run the Test (or Plan It in Detail)

WARM-UP: Share Your Experiment Design (10 min)

Two volunteers share their experiment design from Tuesday. Class gives feedback: Is the test real? Can it be done by Friday? Is the success metric clear?

TEST DAY (35 min)

Students run their test — or if the test requires more time, they create a detailed implementation plan:

Students who can test in class: do it now. Make calls, send texts, set up pre-order forms, do demos in the hallway.

Students whose test requires more time: build out the full plan — what they'll do tonight and tomorrow, who they'll reach, what they'll say.

Facilitator tip: push students to get real data, not just opinions. "Would you buy this?" is weaker than "Here's the link — want to order one right now?"

Students who finish early: design a second test for their #2 assumption, OR help a classmate refine their test.

END-OF-DAY CHECK (5 min)

Each student states: "By Thursday class I will have tested [assumption] and will have [specific data point] to share."

Hold them to it Thursday.

### Thursday

WEEK 16 — THURSDAY

Theme: Interpret Results + Pivot / Persevere Decision

RESULTS SHARE-OUT (20 min)

Each student shares in 60-90 seconds:

What was my assumption?

What did I test?

What happened?

What does it mean for my business?

Celebrate honest results — including failed tests. A confirmed wrong assumption is valuable data.

PIVOT / PERSEVERE / PAUSE (15 min)

Teach the three responses to validation results:

Persevere: my assumption was right — keep going and scale it

Pivot: my assumption was wrong — change one key element and retest

Pause: I don't have enough data yet — I need to run the test again or differently

Students write their decision for each tested assumption and one concrete next action.

WEEK 17 PREVIEW + DECK INTRO (15 min)

Next week is the Unit 2 Final: students build a pitch deck and present to the class using Notre Dame McCloskey Business Plan Competition Round 2 standards.

Preview the deck structure (8 slides):

\1. Cover — Business name, tagline, founder

\2. Problem — What problem are you solving? For whom?

\3. Solution — What do you offer? Why is it better?

\4. Market — Who is your customer? How big is the market?

\5. Business Model — How do you make money?

\6. Traction — What have you done so far? Sales? Tests? Customers?

\7. Financials — Price, revenue target, costs

\8. Ask — What do you need? What's next?

Students should start their deck this weekend. Monday-Wednesday of Week 17 = build time. Thursday = practice. Friday = pitches.

### Friday

WEEK 16 — FRIDAY

Theme: Founder Fight + 1-on-1s + Unit 2 Preview

WARM-UP: Founder Fight (15 min)

Run Founder Fight. Two student businesses go head-to-head on a judge prompt: "Which business has stronger proof that customers want their product?" Judges vote and explain. Rotate 2-3 rounds.

Debrief: What kind of evidence is most convincing? Real sales > pre-orders > surveys > "my friend said."

1-ON-1 PREP CHECK (25 min)

Rotate through students while others work on their Week 17 deck start. Focus questions:

Do you know what your riskiest assumption was? Is it tested?

Do you have at least one real data point from this week?

Have you started thinking about your Week 17 deck?

Flag any student who has no tested assumptions — they will struggle on the Week 17 Traction slide. Give them a weekend assignment: reach out to 5 real potential customers before Monday.

UNIT 2 REFLECTION (10 min)

Students write a short Unit 2 reflection (can be collected and returned for Week 17 use):

The most important thing I learned in Unit 2 was…

The biggest thing I proved about my business was…

The biggest unknown I still have going into Week 17 is…
