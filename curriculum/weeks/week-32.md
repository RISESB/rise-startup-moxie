# Week 32: Team Roles + Operational Planning

Unit 4: Finance & Operations

**Guiding question:** Who does what in your business — and how do you plan the work so nothing falls through the cracks?

_When it last ran: Week of April 13, 2026 · 7:30–9:00 AM · Mon–Fri. Dates are from a previous year; this year's calendar may differ._

## By the end of the week, students can
- Define the key roles their business needs, now and as it grows.
- Map which roles they fill and which they need help with.
- Build a simple operational plan or weekly workflow.
- Connect roles and planning back to their Week 9 team map and Week 30 SOPs.

## Lesson walkthrough
_Written as the facilitator's script for the classroom. Use it to know what was taught and how; don't read it back to students._

### Monday

### Monday — Lecture: Roles + Operational Planning

Facilitator Goal: Students understand the roles a business needs and how to plan the actual work week to week.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"What is the one job in your business you love — and the one you keep avoiding?" 3-4 students share.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: Roles + Planning

Opening (2 min)

Say: "Back in Week 9 you learned every business needs a Builder, a Seller, and an Operator. Now that your business is real, we get specific: what jobs actually need doing, who does them, and how the work gets planned."

The Core Roles (10 min)

Every business, even a solo one, needs these functions covered:

Product/Operations — making and delivering the thing

Sales/Marketing — finding customers and closing them

Finance/Admin — money, records, logistics

Leadership — vision, decisions, keeping it all moving

"If you are solo, you wear all four hats — but you should know which hat you are wearing at any moment, and which one you are weakest in."

Founder Trap (6 min)

Say: "The biggest trap is the founder doing everything — including the stuff they are bad at or hate. That is how businesses stall. Great founders do what only they can do, and get help with the rest."

"Help does not have to be employees. It can be a friend, a freelancer, a family member, a tool, or automation."

Operational Planning (10 min)

Say: "An operational plan answers: what work happens, when, and who does it. Most student businesses fail not from bad ideas but from no plan — things just don't get done."

A simple weekly operating rhythm:

A set time each week to make/prepare product or service

A set time to do marketing and outreach

A set time to handle money and admin

A short weekly review: what got done, what is next

"Rhythm beats willpower. When the work has a scheduled home, it actually happens."

#### 8:05–8:50 AM — Role Map First Pass

Students list every role their business needs, mark which they fill well, which they fill poorly, and which need help. Revisit their Week 9 team map.

Walk the room: "What is the role you are worst at? Who or what could cover it?"

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

Preview: "Tomorrow you build a real weekly operating plan."

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Activities: Role Map + Weekly Operating Plan

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Which core role — product, sales, finance, or leadership — is your strongest?" Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:00 AM — Role Map Finalize

Students finalize their role map: every function, who covers it now, and a plan for the gaps (a person, a tool, or an SOP from Week 30).

#### 8:00–8:45 AM — Weekly Operating Plan

Students build a one-week operating plan: what work happens on which days, roughly how long, and how they'll know it got done.

"Be realistic about your actual schedule — school, sports, life. A plan you can't follow is worse than no plan."

#### 8:45–9:00 AM — Speaker Tour Prep

Distribute Speaker Tour Prep Worksheets.

### Wednesday

### Wednesday — Speaker/Tour

Run the speaker or tour per standard Wednesday format. Prompt: "Ask the speaker how they decide what to do themselves vs. hand off."

### Thursday

### Thursday — Speaker Recap + Thank You Cards + Planning Work Day

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"One sentence — most interesting thing from Wednesday." Around the room.

#### 7:35–7:55 AM — Speaker Recap

Two columns: "What we heard" | "How do they divide up the work in their business?"

#### 7:55–8:20 AM — Thank You Cards

Handwritten. Specific. Required elements: one specific observation, one question it sparked, a genuine thank you.

#### 8:20–9:00 AM — Planning Work Day

Students finalize role map and weekly operating plan in their portfolio. 1-on-1 focus: "What role most needs help, and what is your realistic plan to cover it?"

### Friday

### Friday — Deal or No Deal + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"One job you want to hand off first when you can." Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Deal or No Deal (Week 32 — Rotation: Deal or No Deal)

Team + operations edition:

Deal 1: "A skilled friend will handle all your marketing for 20% of profit — no cash up front. Deal or no deal?"

Deal 2: "You can hire someone $15/hour to do your least favorite task 5 hours a week. Deal or no deal?"

Deal 3: "A tool will automate your admin for $25/month, freeing 4 hours a week. Deal or no deal — what is 4 hours worth to you?"

#### 8:05–8:40 AM — 1-on-1 Sessions

1-on-1 focus: "Does your weekly plan fit your real life? What is the first thing that will get skipped, and how do you protect it?"

#### 8:40–8:55 AM — Week Reflection

What roles does your business need, and which do you fill?

What is your weekly operating rhythm?

What is the first role or task you would get help with?

#### 8:55–9:00 AM — Preview Week 33

Week 33: End of Year Recap. "Next week we look back at the whole year, measure how far you've come, and start getting ready for the final showcase."
