# Week 22: CRM + Outreach Strategies

Unit 3: Marketing & Brand

**Guiding question:** Once someone shows interest, how do you keep track of them and follow up in a way that actually earns the sale?

## By the end of the week, students can
- Build a simple CRM (customer tracker) for their business.
- Understand the stages of a customer relationship from lead to repeat buyer.
- Write a follow-up sequence that adds value instead of nagging.
- Track outreach and conversion with real numbers.

## Lesson walkthrough
_Written as the facilitator's script for the classroom. Use it to know what was taught and how; don't read it back to students._

### Monday

### Monday — Lecture: CRM + The Follow-Up Game

Facilitator Goal: Students learn to track customers systematically and follow up in a way that builds trust rather than annoyance.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Think of a business that followed up with you in a way that felt good — and one that felt annoying. What was the difference?" 3-4 students share.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: CRM + Follow-Up

Opening (2 min)

Say: "Most sales are lost not because the customer said no — but because nobody followed up. The money is in the follow-up. Today you learn to track people and follow up like a pro."

What Is a CRM? (8 min)

Say: "CRM stands for Customer Relationship Management. Big companies pay for fancy software. You need a spreadsheet. That is it."

A simple CRM tracks, for each person:

Name + contact info

How you met / where they came from

Their stage (lead → prospect → customer → repeat)

Last contact date + what was said

Next action + date

"The single most valuable column is Next Action. It means nobody ever falls through the cracks."

The Relationship Stages (6 min)

Lead (fits your segment) → Prospect (showed interest) → Customer (bought once) → Repeat/Referrer (buys again, tells others). Your CRM moves people from left to right.

The Follow-Up Game (8 min)

Say: "Good follow-up gives value every time — it does not just ask 'did you decide yet?' Each message should give something: helpful info, a story, a reminder of the benefit, or a small offer."

The rule of thumb: give 3 times before you ask again. And always make the next step easy.

Bad follow-up: "Just checking in! Any thoughts??" (3 times in a row)

Good follow-up: "Saw this and thought of our conversation — [useful thing]. Whenever you're ready, here's the link."

#### 8:05–8:50 AM — CRM First Draft

Students set up a CRM spreadsheet and enter every real lead, prospect, and customer they already have — even if it is only 2-3 people. Add a Next Action for each.

Walk the room: "Who is one person sitting in your CRM with no next action? What should it be?"

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

Preview: "Tomorrow you build a full follow-up sequence and start working your list."

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Activities: Build Your CRM + Outreach Sequence

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"How many people are in your CRM right now? What is your goal number by end of month?" Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:00 AM — CRM Finalize

Students finish their CRM with all current contacts and clear stages. Add a column for source so they learn which channels actually bring people in.

#### 8:00–8:45 AM — Value-First Outreach Sequence

Students write a 4-message follow-up sequence for a warm lead:

Message 1 — thank/connect (no ask)

Message 2 — give value (tip, story, proof)

Message 3 — give value + soft invite

Message 4 — clear, easy ask

Use AI to draft, then edit to sound human. Pairs read each other's: "Would this annoy you or help you?"

#### 8:45–9:00 AM — Speaker Tour Prep

Distribute Speaker Tour Prep Worksheets.

### Wednesday

### Wednesday — Speaker/Tour

Run the speaker or tour per standard Wednesday format.

### Thursday

### Thursday — Speaker Recap + Thank You Cards + Outreach Work Day

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"One sentence — most interesting thing from Wednesday." Around the room.

#### 7:35–7:55 AM — Speaker Recap

Two columns: "What we heard" | "How does this person keep customers coming back?"

#### 7:55–8:20 AM — Thank You Cards

Handwritten. Specific. Required elements: one specific observation, one question it sparked, a genuine thank you.

#### 8:20–9:00 AM — Work Your List

Students actually send real follow-up messages from their sequence to real people in their CRM, and log it. 1-on-1 focus: "How many messages did you send today? What is your reply rate so far?"

### Friday

### Friday — Founder Fight + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Any replies from your outreach? What worked?" Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Founder Fight (Week 22 — Rotation: Founder Fight)

Relationship edition:

Round 1: A business that chases new customers constantly vs. one that focuses on repeat customers

Round 2: A founder who tracks every lead in a CRM vs. one who keeps it all in their head

Round 3: A business with 10 loyal customers vs. one with 100 one-time buyers

Debrief: "Why are repeat customers worth more than new ones? What does a CRM make possible?"

#### 8:05–8:40 AM — 1-on-1 Sessions

1-on-1 focus: "Show me your CRM. Who is your hottest lead? What is the next action and when?"

#### 8:40–8:55 AM — Week Reflection

What did building a CRM change about how you see your customers?

What does good follow-up look like vs. annoying follow-up?

How many people are in your pipeline, and what is your next action?

#### 8:55–9:00 AM — Preview Week 23

Week 23: AI Campaigns + A/B Testing. "Next week we use AI to build a full campaign and test what actually works."
