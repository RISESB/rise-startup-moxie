## Lesson walkthrough
_Written as the facilitator's script for the classroom. Use it to know what was taught and how; don't read it back to students._

### Monday

### Monday — Lecture: Unit Economics + Revenue vs. Profit

Facilitator Goal: Students can calculate whether they make money on a single sale and understand why revenue is not profit.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"If you sell something for $20, how much of that is actually yours to keep?" Around the room. Most will not know — that is the point.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: Unit Economics

Opening (2 min)

Say: "You can be busy, popular, and selling a lot — and still lose money on every sale. Unit economics is how you make sure that each sale actually helps you, not hurts you."

Revenue vs. Profit (6 min)

Revenue = all the money that comes in. Profit = what is left after costs. "Revenue is vanity. Profit is sanity. You can have $10,000 in sales and $0 in profit if your costs are $10,000."

Unit Economics (10 min)

Say: "Unit economics zooms all the way in to ONE sale. If one sale is not profitable, doing more of them just loses money faster."

The core math for one unit:

Price — what the customer pays

Variable cost — what it costs you to make/deliver that one unit (materials, packaging, fees)

Contribution margin = Price − Variable cost (what one sale contributes toward your fixed costs and profit)

Example: Candle sells for $20. Wax, wick, jar, label = $7. Contribution margin = $13 per candle. "Every candle puts $13 toward your rent, your time, and your profit."

Fixed vs. Variable Costs (6 min)

Variable = changes with each sale (materials). Fixed = stays the same no matter how much you sell (a table fee, a subscription, equipment). "You cover fixed costs with the contribution margin from many sales."

Revenue Projections (4 min)

Say: "A projection is an educated guess at future revenue: units × price, month by month. It turns your goal into a plan you can actually track."

#### 8:05–8:50 AM — Unit Economics Calc

Students calculate their own unit economics: price, variable cost per unit, and contribution margin. Be honest about every cost.

Walk the room: "Did you count packaging? Fees? Your materials? What is your real margin per sale?"

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

Preview: "Tomorrow you turn one sale into a 12-month projection."

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Activities: Unit Economics Calc + Revenue Projection

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"What is your contribution margin per sale? One number." Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:00 AM — Sharpen the Unit Economics

Students double-check every variable cost. AI prompt: "My product is [X], I sell it for $[Y]. Here are my costs: [list]. What costs am I likely forgetting?"

#### 8:00–8:45 AM — 12-Month Revenue Projection

Students build a simple month-by-month projection in a spreadsheet: units sold, price, revenue, variable costs, contribution, fixed costs, profit.

Build 3 scenarios: conservative, realistic, and stretch. "Which is most likely, and what would it take to hit the stretch?"

#### 8:45–9:00 AM — Speaker Tour Prep

Distribute Speaker Tour Prep Worksheets.

### Wednesday

### Wednesday — Speaker/Tour

Run the speaker or tour per standard Wednesday format. Prompt: "Ask the speaker about their biggest unexpected cost."

### Thursday

### Thursday — Speaker Recap + Thank You Cards + Numbers Work Day

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"One sentence — most interesting thing from Wednesday." Around the room.

#### 7:35–7:55 AM — Speaker Recap

Two columns: "What we heard" | "What did this teach me about costs or pricing?"

#### 7:55–8:20 AM — Thank You Cards

Handwritten. Specific. Required elements: one specific observation, one question it sparked, a genuine thank you.

#### 8:20–9:00 AM — Numbers Work Day

Students finalize unit economics and projection in their portfolio. 1-on-1 focus: "What is the one number — price, cost, or volume — that most improves your profit? What would you change?"

### Friday

### Friday — Deal or No Deal + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"What is your realistic 12-month revenue number?" Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Deal or No Deal (Week 26 — Rotation: Deal or No Deal)

Unit-economics edition:

Deal 1: "A big customer will buy 200 units — but only at your variable cost plus $1. Deal or no deal?"

Deal 2: "A supplier will cut your per-unit cost 30% if you commit to buying 1,000 units up front. Deal or no deal?"

Deal 3: "You can raise your price 20%. You might lose a third of your customers. Deal or no deal?"

#### 8:05–8:40 AM — 1-on-1 Sessions

1-on-1 focus: "Walk me through one sale. Where does the money go? Is each sale actually profitable?"

#### 8:40–8:55 AM — Week Reflection

What is your contribution margin, and were you surprised by it?

What is the difference between your revenue and your profit?

What one number would most improve your profitability?

#### 8:55–9:00 AM — Preview Week 27

Week 27: Cash Flow + Break-Even Analysis. "Next week: when does the money actually arrive, and how many sales until you stop losing money?"
