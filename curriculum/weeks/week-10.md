# Week 10: Creating Personas + Calculating Total Addressable Market (TAM)

Unit 2: Customer & Product

**Guiding question:** Who exactly is your customer — and how many of them are there?

## By the end of the week, students can
- Build a detailed customer persona with demographics, behaviors, and motivations.
- Explain the difference between TAM, SAM, and SOM.
- Calculate a rough TAM and SOM for their business idea.
- Connect persona insights to their pitch and May-B-P targeting.

## Lesson walkthrough
_Written as the facilitator's script for the classroom. Use it to know what was taught and how; don't read it back to students._

### Monday

### Monday — Lecture: Customer Personas + TAM/SAM/SOM

Facilitator Goal: Students can build a specific customer persona and explain the three rings of market sizing — TAM, SAM, SOM.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Describe your ideal customer in one sentence — be specific. Not 'teenagers.' Age range, location, situation, what they are dealing with right now." Around the room.

Write 4-5 on the board. Note which are specific vs. generic.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: Personas + TAM

Opening (2 min)

Say: "There is a big difference between knowing who your customer is and understanding your customer. A persona forces you to get specific — to give your customer a face, a name, a daily life."

Talking Point 1 — Customer Personas (10 min)

A persona has several layers:

Demographics — name, age, location, job, income, family situation

Goals — what they are trying to achieve in the area your business addresses

Frustrations — what is getting in their way right now

Behaviors — how they currently solve the problem (badly)

Watering holes — where they spend time online and offline

Quote — one sentence they might actually say about their problem

Walk through a full example: Name: Maria. Age: 34. Location: South Bend. Job: Preschool teacher. Income: $38,000/year. Goal: Feed her family healthy meals on a tight budget without spending her entire Sunday cooking. Frustration: Meal prep services are too expensive, grocery store is 20 minutes away, no time to plan. Behavior: Buys frozen meals even though she hates them. Quote: "I just want food that is real and doesn't cost me an hour every night."

Say: "Notice how specific that is. Now I know exactly what to build, where to find her, and what to say when I do."

Talking Point 2 — TAM, SAM, SOM (12 min)

TAM — Total Addressable Market: everyone who could theoretically buy your product. "The full pool."

SAM — Serviceable Addressable Market: the portion you can actually reach given your current geography, channels, and capabilities. "The pool you can swim in."

SOM — Serviceable Obtainable Market: the realistic slice you can capture in year 1-3. "The pool you will actually swim in."

Example: TAM = all budget-conscious families in the US who want meal prep help (~$50B market). SAM = families in South Bend reachable through local channels (~15,000 households). SOM = families I can realistically serve in year 1 (starting with 20, growing to 100).

Say: "Investors want to see a big TAM — it shows the opportunity is real. But they believe your SOM — that is where your credibility lives. A realistic SOM you can defend is worth more than an inflated TAM you cannot explain."

#### 8:05–8:50 AM — Persona First Draft

Students fill out a full persona for their primary customer. All sections. Give them a name.

Walk the room. Find the most specific thing on each persona — reinforce it. Find the vaguest — push: "What does this person actually do when this problem happens?"

After 25 minutes: Pairs read each other's. "Does this feel like a real person?"

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

Preview Tuesday: "Tomorrow you calculate TAM, SAM, and SOM using real data."

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Activities: Persona Workshop + Market Size Calculation

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Read your persona's quote to the room." Every student, 10 seconds each.

#### 7:35–8:00 AM — Persona Refinement

AI prompt: "Here is my customer persona [paste it]. What questions is this persona NOT answering that would be important before building a product for them?"

Add what is missing. Make it more specific where AI identifies gaps.

#### 8:00–8:45 AM — TAM/SAM/SOM Calculation

AI research prompt: "Help me estimate the TAM for a business that [describe]. What data sources should I use to find real market size numbers?"

Walk the room. Push for real data — census data, industry reports, local statistics. "What is your SOM in year 1? Does it match your May-B-P capacity?"

Students document: TAM (with source), SAM (with reasoning), SOM (with rationale). These go in the business portfolio.

#### 8:45–9:00 AM — Speaker Tour Prep

Distribute Speaker Tour Prep Worksheets.

### Wednesday

### Wednesday — Speaker/Tour

Run the speaker or tour per standard Wednesday format.

### Thursday

### Thursday — Speaker Recap + Thank You Cards + Persona + Market Refinement

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"One sentence — most interesting thing from Wednesday." Around the room.

#### 7:35–7:55 AM — Speaker Recap

Two columns: "What we heard" | "Does this change my persona or market size thinking?"

#### 7:55–8:20 AM — Thank You Cards

Handwritten. Specific. Required elements: one specific observation, one question it sparked, a genuine thank you.

#### 8:20–9:00 AM — Persona + Market Size Work Day

Students finalize persona and TAM/SAM/SOM. These feed into the pitch deck. 1-on-1 focus: "Walk me through your numbers. Where did you get them? Why is your SOM realistic?"

### Friday

### Friday — Founder Fight + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"In one sentence — who is your persona and what is the one thing they most need?" Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Founder Fight (Week 10 — Rotation: Founder Fight)

Market-sizing edition:

Round 1: A business that serves high school students only vs. one that serves all people under 25

Round 2: A local South Bend business vs. the same business scaled nationally from day 1

Round 3: A niche product for competitive athletes vs. a general fitness product for everyone

Debrief: "Which has the bigger TAM? Which has the more realistic SOM? Are those the same business?"

#### 8:05–8:40 AM — 1-on-1 Sessions

1-on-1 focus: "Does your persona match your May-B-P target? If your May-B-P reaches 10 people, are they the right 10?"

#### 8:40–8:55 AM — Week Reflection

What is the most specific thing about your persona that changed how you think about your product?

What surprised you about your market size numbers?

How does knowing your SOM change what you do first?

#### 8:55–9:00 AM — Preview Week 11

Week 11: Customer Discovery Interviews. "Next week you go talk to real customers — face to face or voice to voice. You will learn more in one real conversation than in a week of research."
