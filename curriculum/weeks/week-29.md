# Week 29: Funding Options + Risk Management

Unit 4: Finance & Operations

**Guiding question:** Where does money to grow come from — and how do you protect your business from what could go wrong?

_When it last ran: Week of March 23, 2026 · 7:30–9:00 AM · Mon–Fri. Dates are from a previous year; this year's calendar may differ._

## By the end of the week, students can
- Compare the main ways to fund a business and the trade-offs of each.
- Explain the difference between debt and equity in plain terms.
- Identify the top risks to their business and a way to reduce each.
- Decide what kind of funding (if any) actually fits their business right now.

## Lesson walkthrough
_Written as the facilitator's script for the classroom. Use it to know what was taught and how; don't read it back to students._

### Monday

### Monday — Lecture: Funding Options + Debt vs. Equity + Risk

Facilitator Goal: Students understand how businesses get funded, the real cost of each option, and how to think about risk before it hits.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"If someone offered you $10,000 for your business today, what would you want to know before saying yes?" 3-4 students share.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: Funding + Risk

Opening (2 min)

Say: "At some point, growing means needing money you don't have yet. There are many ways to get it — and every one has a cost. Free money is a myth. Today we learn the trade-offs."

The Funding Ladder (10 min)

Bootstrapping — funding growth from your own sales and savings. Cost: slower. Benefit: you keep full control.

Friends & family — early support from people who believe in you. Cost: personal relationships on the line.

Grants & competitions — money you don't pay back (like McCloskey). Cost: time to apply; often restricted use.

Loans (debt) — borrowed money you repay with interest. Cost: you owe it no matter what.

Investors (equity) — money in exchange for a piece of the business. Cost: you give up ownership and some control.

Debt vs. Equity (6 min)

Debt: you borrow money and pay it back with interest. You keep 100% ownership, but you owe it even if the business struggles.

Equity: you sell a share of your business for money. You never repay it, but that person now owns part of everything you build — forever.

"Debt is a guest who leaves once you pay them. Equity is a roommate who never moves out. Neither is bad — they are just different."

Risk Management (8 min)

Say: "Every business has risks. Amateurs ignore them. Pros name them out loud and reduce them before they blow up."

Common risks and how to reduce them:

Customer risk — too dependent on one customer. Reduce: get more customers.

Supply risk — one supplier can cut you off. Reduce: have a backup.

Money risk — running out of cash. Reduce: keep a cushion, watch cash flow.

Founder risk — everything depends on you. Reduce: document and build a team.

"Effectuation's affordable loss (Week 9) is risk management: never risk more than you can afford to lose."

#### 8:05–8:50 AM — Funding Fit First Pass

Students answer: "Does my business actually need outside money right now? If yes, which option fits and why? If no, what is my plan to grow from sales?"

Walk the room: "Many student businesses should bootstrap. Push back on anyone who wants investors without a reason."

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

Preview: "Tomorrow you map your real risks and lock in your funding thinking."

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Activities: Funding Fit + Risk Map

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Debt or equity — which would you rather take and why?" Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Funding Decision

Students write a one-paragraph funding decision: what they need money for (if anything), how much, which source fits, and the trade-off they accept.

AI prompt: "My business is [X]. I want to grow by [goal]. Given I am a high school founder, what funding options realistically fit, and what are the trade-offs?"

#### 8:05–8:45 AM — Risk Map

Students list their top 4 risks and rate each on likelihood (low/med/high) and impact (low/med/high). For the highest ones, write one action to reduce it.

"Circle your single biggest risk. That is the one to act on first."

#### 8:45–9:00 AM — Speaker Tour Prep

Distribute Speaker Tour Prep Worksheets.

### Wednesday

### Wednesday — Speaker/Tour

Run the speaker or tour per standard Wednesday format. Prompt: "Ask the speaker how they funded their business and their biggest risk."

### Thursday

### Thursday — Speaker Recap + Thank You Cards + Work Day

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"One sentence — most interesting thing from Wednesday." Around the room.

#### 7:35–7:55 AM — Speaker Recap

Two columns: "What we heard" | "How did this person fund or protect their business?"

#### 7:55–8:20 AM — Thank You Cards

Handwritten. Specific. Required elements: one specific observation, one question it sparked, a genuine thank you.

#### 8:20–9:00 AM — Work Day

Students finalize funding decision and risk map in their portfolio. 1-on-1 focus: "What is your biggest risk, and what is your one action to reduce it?"

### Friday

### Friday — Deal or No Deal + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Does your business need outside money right now — yes or no, and why?" Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Deal or No Deal (Week 29 — Rotation: Deal or No Deal)

Funding edition — this is the perfect week for it:

Deal 1: "An investor offers $10,000 for 30% of your business. Deal or no deal?"

Follow up: "What does that say you think the whole business is worth? Is it worth giving up a third forever?"

Deal 2: "A bank offers a $5,000 loan at 12% interest over 2 years. Deal or no deal?"

Follow up: "Can your cash flow handle the monthly payment even in a slow month?"

Deal 3: "You win a $2,500 grant — but you must spend it only on equipment you don't really need yet. Deal or no deal?"

#### 8:05–8:40 AM — 1-on-1 Sessions

1-on-1 focus: "What is your funding plan, and what is the risk you most need to manage?"

#### 8:40–8:55 AM — Week Reflection

What funding option best fits your business right now, and why?

What is the difference between debt and equity in your own words?

What is your biggest business risk and your plan to reduce it?

#### 8:55–9:00 AM — Preview Week 30

Week 30: Legal Structures + Operations. "Next week we make it official: business structures, the basics of staying legal, and how to run smooth day-to-day operations."
