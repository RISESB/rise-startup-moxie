# Week 9: Effectuation + Starting with What You Have + Team Building

Unit 1: Foundations & Identity

**Guiding question:** What can you build right now with what you already have — and who do you need beside you to build it?

## Lesson walkthrough

### Monday

### Monday — Lecture: Effectuation + Team Building

Facilitator Goal: Students understand effectuation as the logic experienced entrepreneurs actually use — and connect it to building their first team.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"What do you have right now — skills, relationships, resources, access — that most people your age don't? Tell the person next to you." 90 seconds. 3-4 share with the group.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: Effectuation + Team Building

Opening (2 min)

Say: "Most people think entrepreneurs start with a big plan and then go find the resources to execute it. That is actually how most businesses fail."

"Researchers studied expert entrepreneurs — people who had built and sold multiple companies — and found that they think completely differently. They use a logic called effectuation."

Talking Point 1 — What Is Effectuation? (8 min)

Say: "Effectuation was identified by researcher Saras Sarasvathy. It flips the traditional planning logic."

Traditional logic (causation): Start with a goal → find resources to achieve it.

Effectuation logic: Start with what you have → figure out what is possible.

Write on the board — the 4 principles of effectuation:

Bird-in-Hand: Start with your means. Who are you? What do you know? Who do you know?

Affordable Loss: Don't ask "what's the upside?" Ask "what's the most I'm willing to lose?" Only risk what you can afford to lose.

Crazy Quilt: Build partnerships. Every new stakeholder you bring in adds their means and their constraints — the business evolves with them.

Lemonade: Treat surprises as opportunities, not problems. Unexpected results are data, not failure.

"These are not motivational phrases. These are the actual operating principles of experienced entrepreneurs. You have been using some of them already — now you have names for them."

Talking Point 2 — Bird-in-Hand Deep Dive (8 min)

Say: "The most important effectuation principle for where you are right now is Bird-in-Hand. It asks three questions:"

Who am I? — your values, personality type, Wingfinder strengths

What do I know? — your skills, knowledge, expertise

Who do I know? — your network, ecosystem, relationships

"The businesses that have the best early momentum are the ones where the founder's Bird-in-Hand matches the problem they are solving."

Example: "A student who grew up helping her mom manage a small cleaning business, who speaks Spanish fluently, and whose family is connected to the Latino small business community in South Bend has a very strong Bird-in-Hand for a business that serves that market. She does not have to build all of that from scratch."

Talking Point 3 — Team Building (8 min)

Say: "Here is the honest truth: you cannot build a serious business alone. Even solo founders have a team — advisors, mentors, part-time help, contractors."

"The question is not whether you need a team. It is: what kind of team do you need, and what do they need to bring that you do not already have?"

A great team has complementary strengths — not clones of the founder. Three essential roles in any early-stage company:

The Builder — the person who can make the product or deliver the service

The Seller — the person who can find customers and close deals

The Operator — the person who keeps the business running, tracks money, and executes consistently

"You probably have one of these naturally. You need to find the other two — or at least know where you'll find them."

#### 8:05–8:50 AM — Bird-in-Hand Audit

Students fill in all three columns: Who I Am / What I Know / Who I Know.

Then: "How does your Bird-in-Hand connect to your May-B-P? Where are your means aligned with the problem you are solving? Where are they not?"

Walk the room. Find students whose Bird-in-Hand clearly aligns with their idea — point it out explicitly. Find students whose ideas do not match what they have — ask: "Is there a different version of this idea that starts closer to what you already have?"

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

Preview Tuesday: "Tomorrow you map out the team your May-B-P actually needs — and identify who in this room or your ecosystem could fill those roles."

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Activities: Bird-in-Hand Audit + Team Mapping

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Are you more of a Builder, Seller, or Operator naturally? Tell the person next to you and give one example." 90 seconds. 4-5 share.

#### 7:35–8:00 AM — Team Map Activity

List the 3 key roles needed (Builder, Seller, Operator — or equivalent for their specific business)

Rate themselves: strong fit / partial fit / not my strength for each role

Identify who in their ecosystem could fill the gaps

Walk the room. For each student: "What role would you play in your own business? Who do you already know who could fill the other two?"

#### 8:00–8:25 AM — Crazy Quilt Activity

Students identify one potential partner — someone who would add means and whose involvement would shape the direction of the business.

"Write a one-paragraph pitch for why someone should partner with you on your May-B-P. What do you bring? What do you need them to bring? What's in it for them?"

Pairs share and give feedback: "Does this pitch make me want to join?"

#### 8:25–8:50 AM — Affordable Loss Check

What is the most money you are willing to invest before you know if it works?

What is the most time you are willing to put in before you need to see results?

What is the most public risk you are willing to take?

Say: "Knowing your affordable loss is not pessimism — it is the thing that keeps you in the game."

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Speaker Tour Prep

Distribute Speaker Tour Prep Worksheets.

### Friday

### Friday — Profit or Broke + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Builder, Seller, or Operator — one word. Go around the room." Write the count on the board.

Say: "Look at this room. Most of you said [dominant type]. That means if you form teams in here, you need to be intentional about balance."

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Profit or Broke (Week 9 — Rotation: Profit or Broke)

Scenario 1: "You spend $200 on supplies for your first batch of product. You sell 8 units at $30 each but 3 are returned. Profit or Broke?"

Answer: Revenue $240 - Returns $90 - Supplies $200 = Loss $50. BROKE. Follow up: "Was this inside your affordable loss?"

Scenario 2: "You and a partner split a booth ($40 each). You sell $150 of product. Ingredients cost $55. Profit or Broke?"

Answer: $150 - $55 - $40 = Profit $55. Follow up: "How does a partner change your affordable loss?"

Scenario 3: "You charge $50/job, do 4 jobs, spend $15 in supplies. But it took 12 hours. Profit or Broke?"

Answer: $200 - $15 = $185 Profit. But ~$15/hour. Follow up: "How much would you need to charge to make $25/hour?"

#### 8:05–8:40 AM — 1-on-1 Sessions

1-on-1 focus: "Where does your Bird-in-Hand align most strongly with your May-B-P? What is holding you back from launching it?"

#### 8:40–8:55 AM — Week Reflection

Which effectuation principle resonated most with you — and why?

What did your Bird-in-Hand audit reveal about your strengths?

Who is one person you could bring into your May-B-P?

#### 8:55–9:00 AM — Preview Week 10
