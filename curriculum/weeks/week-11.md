## Lesson walkthrough
_Written as the facilitator's script for the classroom. Use it to know what was taught and how; don't read it back to students._

### Monday

### Monday — Lecture: Customer Discovery + The Mom Test

Facilitator Goal: Students understand why customer conversations go wrong and know how to ask questions that generate honest, useful information.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Has anyone talked to a potential customer about their business idea outside this class? What happened?" 3-4 students share.

Listen for: people being polite, saying "great idea," encouraging them. Say: "That's exactly what we're fixing today."

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: Customer Discovery

Opening (2 min)

Say: "Here is the problem with most customer conversations: people lie. Not on purpose — they just tell you what they think you want to hear. There is a book called The Mom Test by Rob Fitzpatrick. The title comes from this: even your mom will say your idea is great. So you cannot ask if your idea is good. You have to ask about her life."

The 3 Rules of The Mom Test (8 min)

Talk about their life, not your idea. Ask about their experience with the problem — not their opinion of your solution.

Ask about specifics in the past, not hypotheticals about the future. "Have you ever...?" not "Would you ever...?"

Talk less. Listen more. Your job is to understand, not to pitch.

Bad questions:

"Do you think this is a good idea?"

"Would you use this if I built it?"

"How much would you pay for something like this?"

Good questions:

"Tell me about the last time you dealt with [the problem]. What happened?"

"What do you currently do about it? How is that working?"

"What is the hardest part of [the situation] for you?"

"Have you ever looked for a solution? What did you find?"

"What would make this so much better that you'd tell other people about it?"

What to Listen For (8 min)

Pain — how much does this problem actually bother them? Do they lose sleep over it, or is it just mildly annoying?

Workarounds — what do they currently do? A messy, expensive workaround is a strong signal the pain is real.

Budget signals — do they spend money on this problem already? That tells you more than "would you pay" ever will.

Who to Talk To (5 min)

Say: "Goal this week: at least 2 real conversations. Not surveys — conversations. Target early adopters — people who have the problem most acutely. Do not interview your friends. Find strangers or acquaintances who fit your persona."

#### 8:05–8:50 AM — Interview Design

Students write their 5-question interview. Questions must: ask about past experience, focus on the problem (not solution), be open-ended.

Walk the room. For each student: "Read me question 3. Does it pass the Mom Test?" Rewrite together if not.

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

Homework: "Conduct your first discovery conversation before Thursday. Write down exact quotes when possible."

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Activities: Interview Design + Role Play Practice

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Read me your 5 questions. Partner Mom Test scores each one: pass or fail." 90 seconds per pair.

#### 7:35–8:10 AM — Role Play Practice

Pairs. One person interviews, one plays customer. Full 5-question interview, 10 minutes per round, then switch.

Debrief: "What question got the most useful info? Where did you find yourself pitching? What did that feel like?"

Facilitator: listen for students who pitch during role play. Stop them: "That was a pitch. What question could you ask instead?"

#### 8:10–8:30 AM — Interview Prep + Scheduling

Students identify 3 interview targets — real people who match their persona. Goal: at least one interview scheduled before Thursday.

AI prompt: "I am going to interview [describe the person] about [describe the problem]. What follow-up questions should I be ready to ask?"

#### 8:30–8:50 AM — Discovery Tracker Update

Update Discovery Tracker with interview prep — who they are interviewing and what they expect to learn vs. confirm.

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Speaker Tour Prep

Distribute Speaker Tour Prep Worksheets.

### Wednesday

### Wednesday — Speaker/Tour

Run the speaker or tour per standard Wednesday format.

### Thursday

### Thursday — Speaker Recap + Thank You Cards + Interview Debrief

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Who did their first customer discovery interview? One thing you learned that surprised you." 4-5 students share.

#### 7:35–7:55 AM — Speaker Recap

Two columns: "What we heard" | "What customer problem was this person solving?"

#### 7:55–8:20 AM — Thank You Cards

Handwritten. Specific. Required elements: one specific observation, one question it sparked, a genuine thank you.

#### 8:20–9:00 AM — Interview Debrief + Persona Update

Students who completed an interview: update persona. Where were assumptions right? Where were they wrong?

Students who have not interviewed: conduct it now via phone/text, or finalize plans.

1-on-1 focus: "What did your customer say that surprised you most? How does that change your May-B-P?"

### Friday

### Friday — Deal or No Deal + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"One thing you assumed about your customer that turned out wrong — or confirmed right." Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Deal or No Deal (Week 11 — Rotation: Deal or No Deal)

Deal 1: "Your discovery interviews revealed customers already pay $200/month for a clunky solution. A competitor offers to merge for 30% of your company. Deal or no deal?"

Follow up: "What does a paying customer's existing behavior tell you about pricing?"

Deal 2: "A potential early adopter will only try it for free for 3 months. Deal or no deal?"

Follow up: "When is free worth it — and when is it a trap?"

Deal 3: "Interviews revealed the problem is 3x bigger but in a different market. An investor will fund you to pivot there. Deal or no deal?"

Follow up: "How do you decide when a pivot is the right move vs. a distraction?"

#### 8:05–8:40 AM — 1-on-1 Sessions

1-on-1 focus: "Show me the updated persona. What changed after your interviews? Has your May-B-P changed?"

#### 8:40–8:55 AM — Week Reflection

What is the most valuable thing a customer told you this week?

Where did your assumptions match reality — and where were you wrong?

How has your May-B-P changed based on real customer conversations?

#### 8:55–9:00 AM — Preview Week 12

Week 12: MVP Building + Testing + Vibe Coding. "Next week we start building. Everything we've learned goes in front of a real person."
