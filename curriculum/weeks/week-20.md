# Week 20: Unique Selling Proposition + Storytelling + Brand Strategy

Unit 3: Marketing & Brand

**Guiding question:** What makes you different, and how do you tell a story that makes people care?

## By the end of the week, students can
- Define a clear Unique Selling Proposition (USP).
- Use a simple story framework to make their brand memorable.
- Identify the core elements of a brand: voice, look, and promise.
- Connect USP and story to their primary segment from Week 19.

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Lecture: USP + Storytelling + Brand Basics

Facilitator Goal: Students can state what makes them different and tell a simple, memorable brand story.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Name a brand you love and one word for why." Around the room. Note how many answers are about feeling, not features.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: USP + Storytelling

Talking Point 1 — Unique Selling Proposition (8 min)

Say: "Your USP is the one clear reason a customer should choose you over every alternative. Not a list — one sharp thing."

Format: "The only [category] that [unique benefit] for [segment]."

Example: "The only meal kit in South Bend made entirely from local farm produce, delivered same-day."

"A good USP is true, specific, and hard for competitors to copy. If your competitor could say the exact same thing, it is not a USP yet."

Talking Point 2 — Storytelling (8 min)

Say: "People don't remember features. They remember stories. The best brand stories make the CUSTOMER the hero — not the founder."

Simple story framework:

A customer has a problem (the struggle)

They find your business (the guide)

You give them a plan or product (the solution)

Their life gets better (the transformation)

"You are not Luke Skywalker. You are Yoda. The customer is the hero — you are the guide who helps them win."

Talking Point 3 — Brand Basics (8 min)

Say: "A brand is not a logo. A brand is the feeling people get when they think about you. Three pieces you control:"

Voice — how you sound (playful? serious? bold? warm?)

Look — colors, fonts, style that stay consistent

Promise — what customers can always count on from you

"Consistency is the whole game. A small brand that shows up the same way every time feels bigger and more trustworthy than a big brand that is all over the place."

#### 8:05–8:50 AM — USP + Story Draft

Students write their USP (the "only..." format) and a 4-sentence brand story using the framework, with the customer as hero.

Walk the room: "Could a competitor say your USP word for word? If yes, sharpen it."

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Speaker Tour Prep

Distribute Speaker Tour Prep Worksheets.

### Thursday

### Thursday — Speaker Recap + Thank You Cards + Brand Workshop

### Friday

### Friday — Deal or No Deal + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Say your USP: The only ___ that ___ for ___." Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Deal or No Deal (Week 20 — Rotation: Deal or No Deal)

Brand edition:

Deal 1: "A designer will make you a beautiful logo and full brand kit for $600 before you have any customers. Deal or no deal?"

Deal 2: "A bigger company will let you sell under their trusted brand name — but you lose your own brand entirely. Deal or no deal?"

Deal 3: "An influencer with your exact audience will post about you once for $250. Deal or no deal?"

#### 8:05–8:40 AM — 1-on-1 Sessions

1-on-1 focus: "Is your USP truly unique? Is your brand voice consistent across your message and story?"

#### 8:40–8:55 AM — Week Reflection

What is your USP, and what makes it hard to copy?

What is your brand promise in one sentence?

How does your story make the customer the hero?
