# Week 14: Sales Strategy: Mapping Processes, People, and Funnels

Unit 2: Customer & Product

**Guiding question:** What does the path from stranger to paying customer actually look like for your business?

_When it last ran: Week of November 24, 2025 · 7:30–9:00 AM · Half Week — Adapt as needed · Half Week — Adapt as needed. Dates are from a previous year; this year's calendar may differ._

## By the end of the week, students can
- Map a simple 5-stage sales funnel for their business.
- Identify door openers in their target market.
- Distinguish between a lead, a prospect, and a customer.
- Draft a basic 3-message outreach sequence using AI.

## Lesson walkthrough
_Written as the facilitator's script for the classroom. Use it to know what was taught and how; don't read it back to students._

### Monday

### Monday — Lecture: Sales Process + Funnel Thinking

Facilitator Goal: Students stop being afraid of sales and start seeing it as a process they can design and improve.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"What is your reaction to the word 'sales'? One word." Around the room fast. Write all answers on the board.

You will see: pushy, uncomfortable, fake, necessary, scary. Say: "Notice what this says about how we think about sales. Today we change that."

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: Sales + Funnels

Opening (2 min)

Say: "Selling is not convincing people to buy something they don't want. Selling is helping people who have a problem find the solution they are already looking for. If you have done the customer discovery work, the problem is real and people want it solved. Sales is the mechanism that connects them to your solution."

The 5-Stage Sales Funnel (10 min)

Draw on the board — wide at top, narrow at bottom:

Stage 1 — AWARENESS: They know you exist.

Stage 2 — INTEREST: They are curious enough to learn more.

Stage 3 — CONSIDERATION: They are comparing you to alternatives.

Stage 4 — DECISION: They decide to buy. What removes the last obstacle?

Stage 5 — LOYALTY: They come back and refer others.

Say: "Most entrepreneurs spend all their time at Stage 1 — posting, marketing — but have nothing set up for Stages 3 and 4. Customers get interested and fall out because there is no clear next step."

Leads, Prospects, Customers (5 min)

Lead: someone who fits your persona and has the problem. Prospect: a lead who has expressed interest. Customer: a prospect who bought. Track your conversion rate at each stage.

Door Openers (8 min)

Say: "In every target market there are people who have access to many others and who others trust. One relationship with a door opener can open 10 customer conversations."

Examples:

Selling to parents: PTA president, pediatrician, school counselor

Selling to small businesses: chamber of commerce director, bookkeeper who serves 20 businesses

Selling to athletes: head coach, athletic director, team trainer

#### 8:05–8:50 AM — Funnel First Draft

Students draw their sales funnel. For each stage: what happens, what does the customer do, what do you do?

Walk the room. Most common gap: Stage 3 to 4. "What happens when someone is interested but hasn't bought? What do you do?"

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

Preview Tuesday: "Tomorrow you build a real outreach sequence — 3 messages that take someone from awareness to decision."

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Activities: Funnel Mapping + Outreach Sequence

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"What is the biggest drop-off point in your funnel — where do you think you lose the most people?" 3-4 students share.

#### 7:35–8:10 AM — Funnel Map: Final Version

Finalize funnel. For each stage: one action the customer takes, one action you take, what a "yes" looks like before moving to the next stage.

#### 8:10–8:45 AM — Outreach Sequence

Build a 3-message sequence for a cold lead:

Message 1 — Awareness: introduce yourself, name the problem, no ask yet

Message 2 — Interest: share one piece of value (a result, a story, a fact), soft ask to connect

Message 3 — Decision: direct ask — "Can I show you how this works?" or "Are you open to a 10-minute call?"

Use AI to draft all three. Then edit until they sound human.

#### 8:45–9:00 AM — Speaker Tour Prep

Distribute Speaker Tour Prep Worksheets.

### Wednesday

### Wednesday — Speaker/Tour

Run the speaker or tour per standard Wednesday format.

### Thursday

### Thursday — Speaker Recap + Thank You Cards + Sales Work Day

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"One sentence — most interesting thing from Wednesday." Around the room.

#### 7:35–7:55 AM — Speaker Recap

Two columns: "What sales approach did this person describe?" | "What stage of the funnel does that apply to?"

#### 7:55–8:20 AM — Thank You Cards

Handwritten. Specific. Required elements: one specific observation, one question it sparked, a genuine thank you.

#### 8:20–9:00 AM — Sales Work Day

Finalize outreach sequence and identify door openers. 1-on-1 focus: "Who are the 3 door openers in your market? Have you added them to your outreach list?"

### Friday

### Friday — Deal or No Deal + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"What stage of your funnel do you feel least prepared for?" Around the room.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Deal or No Deal (Week 14 — Rotation: Deal or No Deal)

Deal 1: "A door opener will introduce you to 20 potential customers — but wants 15% of every sale from those introductions forever. Deal or no deal?"

Deal 2: "A customer wants to buy in bulk — 10 units at once — but only if you drop price 40%. Deal or no deal?"

Deal 3: "Someone will run your entire sales funnel for $3,000/month before you have revenue. Deal or no deal?"

#### 8:05–8:40 AM — 1-on-1 Sessions

1-on-1 focus: "Walk me through your funnel. Where are you losing people? What is the fix?"

#### 8:40–8:55 AM — Week Reflection

What changed about how you think about selling after this week?

Which stage of your funnel is strongest? Which needs the most work?

Who is one door opener you are going to reach out to this week?

#### 8:55–9:00 AM — Preview Week 15

Week 15: Value Proposition + Pricing Models. "Next week we nail what makes your offer compelling — and what to charge for it."
