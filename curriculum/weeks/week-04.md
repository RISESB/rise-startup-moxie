# Week 4: Business Model Canvas + MVPs + May-B-P

Unit 1: Foundations & Identity

**Guiding question:** What is the simplest version of your business that you could build right now to test if the problem is real?

_When it last ran: Week of September 15, 2025 · 7:30–9:00 AM · Mon–Fri. Dates are from a previous year; this year's calendar may differ._

### Monday

### Monday — Lecture: Business Model Canvas + MVPs + May-B-P

Facilitator Goal: Students understand the BMC as a thinking tool, the difference between an MVP and a May-B-P, and why starting small is not the same as thinking small.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"What is the strongest problem from your Discovery Tracker? In one sentence. Go around the room."

Write 4-5 on the board. These will be reference points throughout the week.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Lecture: BMC + MVPs + May-B-P

30 minutes.

Opening (2 min)

Say: "Last week you found problems. This week we start figuring out what to do about them."

"Before anyone builds anything, the best entrepreneurs ask: what is the simplest version of this that could tell me whether the problem is real and whether people will pay for a solution?"

"Today we learn three tools that help answer that question: the Business Model Canvas, the MVP, and the May-B-P."

Talking Point 1 — Business Model Canvas (10 min)

Distribute BMC templates.

Say: "The Business Model Canvas is a one-page map of how a business works. It was created by Alexander Osterwalder and it is used by startups and Fortune 500 companies alike."

"Nine boxes. Each one answers a key question about the business."

Walk through each box briefly — write on the board or point to the template:

Customer Segments — who are you building this for?

Value Proposition — what problem do you solve and why is your solution better?

Channels — how do you reach your customers?

Customer Relationships — how do you get, keep, and grow customers?

Revenue Streams — how do you make money?

Key Resources — what do you need to deliver your value proposition?

Key Activities — what do you actually do every day?

Key Partners — who do you need to work with?

Cost Structure — what does it cost to run this?

Walk through a quick example using a familiar local business — a food truck, a barbershop, a lawn care company. Fill in the BMC out loud with the class contributing.

Say: "Notice that filling this out raises more questions than it answers. That is the point. The BMC is a hypothesis — not a plan."

Talking Point 2 — MVP (5 min)

Say: "MVP stands for Minimum Viable Product. It is the simplest version of your product that delivers enough value for someone to actually use it — and give you feedback."

"The key word is viable — it has to actually work. Not perfect. Not finished. But it has to deliver the core value."

Example: "Dropbox's MVP was a 3-minute demo video. They did not build the product first. They filmed what the product would do and shared it online. 75,000 people signed up overnight. That told them the market existed before they wrote a single line of code."

Ask: "Why would you build the demo before the product?" Let 2-3 students respond.

Expected: Save time, save money, find out if people care before investing everything.

Talking Point 3 — May-B-P (8 min)

Say: "We use a version of the MVP called the May-B-P — the Maybe a Product. Here is the definition:"

Write on the board: "A May-B-P is a business you can build in 2 weeks that tests whether the market is interested in solving the problem — not whether this is the scalable solution."

"A May-B-P is not your dream business. It is your first experiment."

Show the Professor Burger video (link: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NSkfQS1Brvg). ~3 minutes.

After the video, ask:

"What did he NOT do when starting his business?"

"How does that change how you think about what your first version looks like?"

Expected: He did not build a restaurant first. He did not wait for the perfect setup. He started with what he had and tested the idea.

Say: "Your May-B-P is your Professor Burger moment. What can you do in 2 weeks that proves — or disproves — that someone will pay for your solution?"

Closing (3 min)

Say: "The biggest trap new entrepreneurs fall into is waiting. Waiting for the app to be built, waiting for the logo, waiting for the right moment."

"Your May-B-P breaks that habit. It forces you to do the most important thing: go find out if anyone cares."

"Tomorrow we build your BMC and draft your May-B-P concept. Come with your strongest problem from your Discovery Tracker."

#### 8:05–8:50 AM — BMC Quick Practice

Students choose one of the problems from the warm-up board (does not have to be their own) and fill out a BMC for a hypothetical business that solves it.

This is a practice run — not their actual business. Goal is to get comfortable with the tool.

Pairs: "Share your BMC with your partner. What did they fill in that surprised you?"

Whole group: 2-3 students share one box they found hardest to fill in and why.

#### 8:50–9:00 AM — Close

Preview Tuesday: "Tomorrow you build a BMC for your actual business idea and draft your May-B-P concept using the Now vs. Later framework."

### Tuesday

### Tuesday — Activities: BMC Workshop + Now vs. Later + May-B-P Draft

Facilitator Goal: Students build their own BMC, use Now vs. Later to strip their idea down to its core, and draft a May-B-P concept.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"In one sentence — what is your May-B-P? What could you build or test in 2 weeks?" Go around fast. No judgment — just say something.

Facilitator note: Many students will not know yet. That is fine. This is a warm-up, not a test. The goal is to get them thinking.

#### 7:35–8:00 AM — BMC Workshop: My Business

Students fill out a BMC for their own business idea using a fresh template.

Say: "Fill in every box — even if you are guessing. A guess is better than a blank because it forces you to think. We can fix a wrong answer. We can't fix nothing."

Walk the room. For each student, ask: "Who is your customer segment? Be specific — not 'teenagers,' but 'high school athletes in South Bend who need affordable sports gear.'"

After 15 minutes: Pairs share and give each other feedback on one box.

#### 8:00–8:20 AM — Now vs. Later Activity

Say: "List out every next step you think you need to take to launch your business. Everything. Write fast."

Give them 3 minutes to brainstorm their list.

Then: "Now sort them into two columns: NOW and LATER."

Write on the board:

NOW = anything you need to test whether someone will pay for your solution.

LATER = everything else — logos, websites, business cards, legal setup, scale plans.

Example: "You want to start a meal prep service. NOW: cook one meal, find one customer, sell it. LATER: build a website, create a menu, get a food handler's permit."

After sorting: "What is actually left in your NOW column? That is your May-B-P."

#### 8:20–8:45 AM — May-B-P Draft

Students draft their May-B-P concept. If available, use the May-B-P bot in ChatGPT or Claude to help generate simpler versions of their idea.

Prompt for the AI: "My business idea is [describe]. Help me come up with 3 versions of a May-B-P — the simplest possible test of this idea that I could run in 2 weeks with little to no money."

Students write down their chosen May-B-P concept:

What is it?

Who is the first customer?

What does success look like in 2 weeks?

What is the one thing you need to do first?

#### 8:45–9:00 AM — Speaker Tour Prep

Distribute Speaker Tour Prep Worksheets. Students research and prepare for Wednesday.

### Wednesday

### Wednesday — Speaker/Tour

Run the speaker or tour per standard Wednesday format. Prep sheets out, active listening, notes captured.

### Thursday

### Thursday — Speaker Recap + Thank You Cards + May-B-P Work Day

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"One sentence — most interesting thing from Wednesday." Around the room, no repeats.

#### 7:35–7:55 AM — Speaker Recap

Two columns: "What we heard" | "How it connects to our May-B-P"

Pull 3-4 moments. Ask: "Did anything the speaker said change how you think about starting simple?"

#### 7:55–8:20 AM — Thank You Cards

Handwritten. Specific. Required elements on the board:

One specific thing you heard or observed

One question it sparked

A genuine, specific thank you

Walk the room. Hand back generic cards.

#### 8:20–9:00 AM — May-B-P Work Day

Students refine their May-B-P concept. Focus: getting specific about the first customer and first action.

If using AI: "Ask Claude or ChatGPT to poke holes in your May-B-P. Prompt: 'Here is my May-B-P concept. What assumptions am I making? What could go wrong in the first 2 weeks?'"

Use this time for 1-on-1s. Key questions:

"Who is your first customer — do you know them by name?"

"What is the one thing you are doing this week to test your idea?"

### Friday

### Friday — Founder Fight + 1-on-1s + Week Reflection

Facilitator Goal: Energy and reflection. Connect Founder Fight arguments to the BMC thinking from this week.

#### 7:30–7:35 AM — Warm-Up

"Tell your partner your May-B-P in one sentence. They have to say back: who is the customer and what is the test." 90 seconds.

#### 7:35–8:05 AM — Founder Fight (Week 4 — Rotation restarts: Founder Fight)

Same format as Week 1. Two students, assigned businesses, 60 seconds each, class votes.

Week 4 matchups — make them more complex than Week 1:

Round 1: A subscription meal kit service vs. a personal chef service

Round 2: An online tutoring platform vs. an in-person study center

Round 3: A resale sneaker business vs. a custom sneaker design business

Debrief tie-in: "Which of these businesses could start with a May-B-P right now? What would that look like?"

#### 8:05–8:40 AM — 1-on-1 Sessions

Students work on May-B-P or BMC independently.

1-on-1 focus:

"Walk me through your BMC. What box are you least sure about?"

"What is the first real-world action your May-B-P requires?"

"When are you going to do it?"

#### 8:40–8:55 AM — Week Reflection

Ask:

"What changed about how you see your business idea after filling out the BMC?"

"What is in your NOW column that you can actually do this weekend?"

"What are you still waiting for that you don't actually need yet?"

#### 8:55–9:00 AM — Preview Week 5

Week 5: AI Basics and Outreach.

"Next week we learn how to use AI — Claude and ChatGPT — as business tools. Not for homework. For real work: research, outreach, problem-solving."
